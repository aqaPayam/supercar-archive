## Evidence boundaries

The Peugeot reference is the 2003 European RC programme. Peugeot Austria's
30 June release supplies native 177 PS, four seats and 205/40 R17 tyres.
GoAuto's actual left-hand-drive European launch drive supplies 130 kW at
7000 rpm, 202 Nm at 4750 rpm, naturally aspirated two-litre 16-valve four,
five-speed manual and chassis changes. It is not a final Australian equipment
sheet. The road assessment includes the harsh ride and unsatisfying shift.
No RC-to-GTi 180 double count, 180-bhp conversion, planned annual production,
WRC racing hardware or direct homologation claim is imported.

Volkswagen's own 2.8 VR6 table supplies 2792 cc, 128 kW / 174 PS at 5800 rpm
and 235 Nm at 4200 rpm. Its history explains the 15-degree, shared-head,
transverse six-cylinder layout and first six-cylinder Golf. The October 1992
Motor Sport road test supplies explicitly identified UK equipment and driving
assessment. The accompanying four-cylinder GTI's mass/handling praise and the
2.9 Corrado/Syncro tune are not VR6 2.8 specifications. No universal world-first
claim, generic Golf drag coefficient or five-speed gearbox inference is made.
The UK test's octane-dependent torque shorthand is not silently reconciled.

Ford's 2006 anniversary press release, printed page 7, establishes the original
XR2's 1981 introduction, Dunton SVE handling development and equipment. Its
2017 German release, printed page 5, corroborates the original 1.6-litre OHV
engine and native 84 PS. Both PDF pages were rendered and visually read.
The programme involved Cologne and Dunton; Germany records the European
manufacturer programme, not the factory of every pictured example. No later
CVH engine, five-speed transmission, precise disputed cc figure, unexplained
mass or conflicting acceleration claim is imported. The road-test PDF hosted
by World Radio History returned HTTP 403 to the normal research request and
was not used as evidence. No access restriction was bypassed.

Mazda's public history establishes the 1985 turbo/full-time-4WD Familia/323
programme and the February 1987 Swedish WRC victory. The selected technical
reference is explicitly the May 1988 US Car and Driver test, republished in
2026. Japanese 140 PS, German 150 PS and later BG hardware are not transferred.
US published hp remains hp; no power or 0–60-to-0–100 conversion is made.
The selected technical fields remain a concise factual extraction. Public
low-resolution previews of Mazda's late-revision 1988 US brochure were viewed,
but its tiny technical table was not treated as a precise specification source.
Premium scans and the password-protected German press archive were not used.
