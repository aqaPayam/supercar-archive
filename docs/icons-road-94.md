## SiR E-EF9 five-speed manual — Japan August 1991 archive reference

The EF9 SiR brought Honda’s original high-revving DOHC VTEC concept to the Civic hatchback, pairing its 160-PS B16A with four-wheel double-wishbone suspension. It marks the Civic’s transition into the VTEC performance lineage before the later EG and Type R generations. Count this absent fourth-generation performance-road family once; SiR/SiR II grades, equipment packages, years and racing derivatives add no extra entries.

Japan-market fourth-generation E-EF9 Civic SiR three-door, five-speed manual, using Honda’s archive explicitly dated August 1991. This is a dated selected specification, not a claim that the generation or SiR began in 1991: the generation was announced September 1987 and the VTEC SiR programme in September 1989. B16A, 1595 cc, 10.2 compression, 160 PS net at 7600 rpm and 15.5 kgm net at 7000 rpm. Original PS and kgm notation is retained without conversion to mechanical hp or Nm. The archive marks five-speed manual available and four-speed automatic unavailable for this SiR column; generic automatic boilerplate does not make this an automatic reference. Base SiR equipment is selected; viscous LSD was optional, not standard. No SiR II power steering/equipment, EG 170-PS engine, B16B Type R figures, competition tune or measured performance is copied.

## XSi three-door, five-speed manual — April 1989 Japan launch reference

The second-generation Integra launched Honda’s DOHC VTEC production-car era in April 1989. Its naturally aspirated 1.6-litre B16A used switchable valve timing and lift to combine high-rpm performance with everyday operation, a landmark that shaped later Honda sports engines. Count this absent Integra generation once across Honda/Acura identities, three-/four-door bodies and XSi/RSi grades; later years, transmissions and cosmetic treatments add no separate entries.

Japan April 1989 production launch, Integra XSi three-door coupe with five-speed manual. Original April 19 release announces three-door sales April 20, with the four-door following May 12; its net DOHC VTEC table gives 1595 cc, 160 PS at 7600 rpm and 15.5 kgm at 7000 rpm. The stated high-output condition is five-speed with premium unleaded fuel. Honda’s B16A retrospective explicitly labels its engine specification 1989 Integra XSi and supplies configuration, bore/stroke, compression and valvetrain. Native PS/kgm net values remain unconverted. Honda’s separately dated April 1993 XSi archive confirms the DA6 three-door identity but is not silently treated as an April 1989 production sheet. Its dimensions, weights, ratios and equipment are not imported. No four-door DA8 body, lower-spec ZC engine, automatic 150-PS calibration, later B16A evolution, US Acura B18 or DC2 Type R specification is copied.

## Evidence boundaries

Honda's EF9 SiR table is explicitly current as of August 1991 and says that
specifications can vary by year. It is used as that dated reference. The
fourth-generation announcement in September 1987 and VTEC introduction in
September 1989 are different events. The original September 21, 1989 release
states sales began September 22 and distinguishes base SiR LSD availability
from SiR II's combined LSD/ALB option. No optional differential or ABS is
silently labelled standard, and four-door/Shuttle four-wheel-drive equipment
is not transferred to the selected hatchback. The archive's own availability
marks select five-speed manual; generic automatic clutch boilerplate is not
an assertion that this SiR column offered an automatic. Mechanical fields,
ratings, bore/stroke, brakes, tyres and double-wishbone suspension were read
from the model-specific table. No kerb weight, fuel-consumption result,
acceleration or race result is imported.

The original April 19, 1989 Integra release and Honda's B16A retrospective
were actually read. The latter explicitly labels its engine table 1989
Integra XSi. The original launch release supplies selected five-speed output
conditions, native net ratings and shared double-wishbone/rear multi-link
layout. Three-door sales started April 20; the four-door followed May 12.
The three-door April 1993 XSi archive was read only to confirm E-DA6 identity;
it is not rewritten into an April 1989 specification sheet. Its weights,
ratios, tyres, dimensions and equipment are not imported for the launch car.
The RSi archive and later lineup were also read as research cross-checks,
not sources of additional launch fields. A search hit labelled 1989 Integra
was the older E-AV/ZC generation ending that year; it is not the DA6 reference.
An indexed aftermarket table is not substituted for original Honda data.

Both records retain 160 PS and 15.5 kgm net in the factory notation; no PS-to-
mechanical-hp or torque conversion is introduced. The Integra automatic's
150-PS/15.3-kgm tune, later EG Civic's 170 PS and B16B Type R's 185 PS are
not imported. Honda's development history was read for the VTEC engineering
context; no universal world-first claim, racing output, absolute superiority,
award or unverified competition result is asserted for these exact road cars.
Honda's technical chronology and dated model-specific references support
admission on landmark engineering grounds, rather than advertising adjectives.

The legacy Japanese archive required its declared Japanese encoding to read
correctly. The initial automatic-decoding mojibake was detected, then the
actual bytes decoded as CP932 for inspection. Invalid byte sequences were
replaced for display; intact table labels, numbers and availability marks
were read rather than inferred from garbled text. No source page was modified.
No paid, blocked or authenticated material was bypassed.
