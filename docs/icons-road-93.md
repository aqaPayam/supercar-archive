## GSX turbo AWD, five-speed manual — 1990 US factory reference

The original Eclipse turbo/AWD performance programme combined an intercooled twin-cam four with full-time four-wheel traction in a compact sports coupe. Its viscous-coupled centre differential and independent rear suspension distinguish the selected GSX from ordinary front-drive trim. Count the first-generation Diamond-Star road family once, including Eclipse, Eagle Talon and Plymouth Laser aliases; badges, transmissions and model years add no extra entries.

## GSX turbo AWD, five-speed manual — 1995 US factory reference

The second Eclipse generation substantially renewed the Diamond-Star performance coupe with four-wheel independent multi-link suspension and a 210-hp intercooled turbo manual reference. The GSX paired that chassis with AWD and centre/rear limited-slip differentials, sustaining the programme’s distinctive high-performance role. Count this generation once across Eclipse and Eagle Talon; badges, GS/GS-T/GSX grades, facelifts, gearboxes and Spyder bodies do not supply additional entries.

1995 US-market second-generation Eclipse GSX, standard five-speed manual. Original Mitsubishi MMSA-ADV-5618 brochure, copyright 1994: actual high-resolution pages 14–15 inspected. Turbo/intercooled 2.0-litre DOHC 16-valve four, multi-point injection, 210 hp at 6000 rpm and 214 lb-ft at 3000 rpm. These are native advertised US figures; no unstated SAE convention, Japanese PS value or inferred metric torque/displacement conversion is supplied. Automatic is optional and has a different 205-hp / 220-lb-ft rating, not selected. AWD with centre and rear limited-slip differentials is standard on GSX; ABS is optional. GSX manual tyres are 215/55VR16, not the automatic size. The 2877-lb curb weight shown is GS-T manual, not GSX, and is omitted. No NA GS/RS Chrysler engine figures or Talon test measurements are copied. 1995 is the chosen US model year, not the Japanese launch date; Japanese-market second-generation specifications differ. The family photograph depicts a later facelift, not an exact 1995 body.

## Factory evidence actually inspected

The four public 1990 GSX AWD brochure JPEGs were inspected individually.
Pages 2–4 supply the clear 1997-cc, 195-hp/6000-rpm, 203-lb-ft/3000-rpm,
DOHC four-valve, multi-point-injection, air-to-air-intercooled turbo,
five-speed, full-time-AWD, viscous centre coupling, normal 50:50 split,
front MacPherson / rear multi-link and four-disc/front-ventilated fields.
The small bore/stroke, compression and tyre print is not imported. The
rear torque-control link describes suspension behaviour, not an active
rear torque-vectoring system or proof of a rear LSD. The brochure identifies
DSM's Normal, Illinois factory. All scans are public low-resolution previews;
the high-resolution download is donation-gated and was not accessed. A
separate late-1990 brochure was discovery HTML only, not read as scan evidence.

The 1995 US brochure MMSA-ADV-5618 has an explicitly sponsored free high-
resolution download on ImportArchive, available through 31 December 2026.
The normal public Google Drive large-file confirmation led to an intact
70,356,395-byte ZIP with 16 JPEG members; selected JPEGs were read as images,
with no executable content used. Actual high-resolution pages 13–15 were
visually inspected. Page 14 distinguishes standard GSX AWD with centre/rear
limited-slip differentials from optional ABS. Page 15 separates GSX/GS-T
2.0-litre turbo/intercooled DOHC 16-valve data from the GS/RS NA engine.
It explicitly separates the manual's 210 hp/6000 rpm and 214 lb-ft/3000 rpm
from automatic 205 hp and 220 lb-ft. GSX manual 215/55VR16 tyres differ from
GSX automatic 205/55VR16. Four-wheel multi-link, front/rear stabilizers,
sport-tuned dampers, five-speed with overdrive and GSX two-piston front
calipers are explicit. The tyre and equipment columns are read by model,
not copied from the neighbouring GS-T or GS/RS columns.

The listed 2877-lb curb weight applies to GS-T manual, not GSX, and is omitted.
No exact cc, compression, engine code, centre torque split, acceleration or
maximum-speed figure is inferred for 1995. Native US horsepower and lb-ft
remain native; an unstated SAE convention, Japanese 230 PS, conversion or
wheel-output rating is not invented. The 1995 brochure's copyright 1994
is not used to mislabel the selected US model year or Japanese launch.
Low-resolution 1995 pages 1, 2 and 13–16 were also inspected; high-resolution
2, 3 and 16 were extracted but not visually inspected for imported evidence.
Remaining 1995 pages were not individually read. No whole-brochure certification
is implied. Manufacturer model-history text was used to separate Japanese
GSR-4/FWD configurations and launch dates from the US references, not to
supply Japanese-market figures to these US records.

The independent 1989 award supports family recognition, not an exact AWD GSX
test. The October 1994 review tested a naturally aspirated GS; its explicit
GSX preference supports performance context, while its measured performance,
weight and NA engine figures are omitted. A June 1994 comparison was also
read for programme context: its tested Eclipse was FWD GS-T and its AWD car
was a Talon TSi. Those measurements are not silently transferred to GSX.
No film-hero identity or modified/tuner output is asserted.
