# TVR, Maserati and lightweight road icons — batch 50

Reviewed 29 October 2026. Five absent road families are selected in
`data/icons-road-50.json`. No cosmetic editions, extra model years, roof
derivatives or dedicated racers are counted. Existing records are preserved.

## Selection and identity

The complete TVR, Maserati, Ariel and Caterham inventories were checked before
import. T350 is absent from the TVR records. No A6G/54 or Mistral exists among
the earlier Maserati additions. Ariel has Atom 4 but no Nomad. Caterham has the
independently suspended CSR generation, not the traditional Series 3 road
family represented here by one Duratec R500. The R500 launch release explicitly
identifies its Series 3 chassis; the contemporary road assessment distinguishes
the narrow traditional car from CSR. No extra R300, R400 or K-series R500 is added.

## Evidence boundaries

### TVR T350C

TVR's own historic-model index dates T350 to 2002–2006. Its heritage catalogue
establishes the 3605-cc straight-six, four valves per cylinder, dry sump,
five-speed manual and standard 304/282-mm ventilated brakes. The date is
programme context, not proof of the photographed 2003 car's exact build state.
Power-unit equivalence and conflicting acceleration labels are excluded.
Larger optional brakes, the later T350T roof derivative and a searched LS7
conversion are not transferred into this closed production-road reference.

### Maserati A6G/54 Allemano

The original manufacturer-issued *Maserati Coupé: The History* by Jürgen
Lewandowski, publication M18/02 (2002), was inspected as a public facsimile.
Its physical PDF page 133 identifies the Allemano column separately from the
adjacent racing 450 S and later 3500/5000 GTs: 1985 cc, inline six, double
camshaft, 76.5 × 72 mm and 2550-mm wheelbase. Physical page 27 was rendered
and read for the road adaptation, distinct from A6GCS competition specification.

The 1954 reference dates the family introduced with a Frua body; it does not
claim the photographed 1956 Allemano was built in 1954. The book's narrative
and table disagree about the power peak, so power is omitted. Body-dependent
speed, unclear weight basis, racing dry sump and optional carburettor equipment
are not imported. One road family is counted across coachbuilders.

### Maserati Mistral 3700

The same factory book's physical pages 43 and 134 were rendered and read.
Only the Mistral 3700's own 3694.4-cc, 86 × 106-mm engine data, Lucas injection,
twin ignition and 2400-mm wheelbase are selected. Adjacent Sebring columns,
4000 data and acceleration spanning engine versions are not mixed in.

The [factory Classiche announcement](https://www.maserati.com/de/de/news/maserati-certification-of-authenticity-the-new-maserati-classiche-programme-begins)
explicitly identifies a road 3700 produced in March 1969. That independently
establishes the reference-year context; it does not identify the separately
photographed 1967 coupe as that certified car. No restoration certificate,
exact photographed engine capacity or Spyder specification is implied.

### Ariel Nomad 1

The original-family manufacturer catalogue supplies Honda K24, 2354 cc,
chain-driven DOHC, four valves per cylinder, 235 bhp, 300 Nm and six-speed
manual. Its road configuration explicitly requires Road Pack, individual
vehicle approval and registration. The selected reference includes that UK
road configuration; not every configurable Nomad is assumed road-approved.

The [factory history](https://www.arielmotor.co.uk/our-history/) independently
dates the original K24 family introduction to 2015. The current legacy
catalogue is an undated original-family specification. Its optional supercharger,
short final drive, brake/wheel upgrades, Nomad R and Ford-powered Nomad 2 are
excluded. Acceleration with ambiguous mph/km/h labels is omitted.

### Caterham Superlight R500

The actual Caterham launch-release body reproduced by Serious Wheels is read
separately from the host's editorial specification table. It establishes
Series 3 chassis, all-alloy naturally aspirated 2.0-litre Duratec four-cylinder,
263 bhp, 177 lb-ft, rear drive, roller-barrel throttles and standard six-speed
manual. Sequential transmission and launch control are optional. Brake and
Avon CR500 descriptions come from that same release body.

Jethro Bovingdon's [actual June 2008 Scottish road drive](https://www.evo.co.uk/caterham/7/6607/caterham-superlight-r500)
supports the road-car identity and performance-icon case. Its tested optional
sequential gearbox and pre-production launch control do not establish standard
fitment. The earlier dealer newsletter's inconsistent units and the obsolete
manufacturer-history URL redirecting to an unrelated event are not used as
technical evidence. No racing-series rules or newer Seven specification is added.
