## Duplicate review

The complete 499-car inventory was checked for families, generations and aliases.
Ford's existing GT, Mustang, Sierra Cosworth and RS200 records do not cover
Focus, Fiesta or Cortina. Three Focus generations and two Fiesta generations
receive one reference each, without additional ST/RS trims, doors, paint schemes
or performance packs. Focus RS500, later special editions and Fiesta ST200
are not extra entries. Fiesta's B299/B479 codes distinguish the generations
without relying solely on inconsistent international Mk numbering.

Lotus Cortina is one Ford record; it is not duplicated under Lotus. Vauxhall
Lotus Carlton and Opel/Lotus Omega are aliases of the same Type 104 programme,
represented once. No Carlton or Omega existed. Talbot/Chrysler Sunbeam Lotus is
also one record; its Lotus badge does not create another manufacturer entry.

## Evidence boundaries

- Focus I: Ford's 2018 RS history confirms the original 2002 road model's
  Duratec engine family, 2.0-litre class, turbocharger, 215-PS notation, front
  drive and standard LSD. That is the scope of the imported technical data.
  The March 2001 introductory brochure was read but describes a preliminary
  200-plus-power specification and early hardware. Its brake-caliper and other
  provisional fields are not treated as final production equipment. Exact
  displacement, gearbox, brakes, peak rpm and unqualified modern power
  conversion remain unfilled; WRC specifications are excluded.
- Focus II: Ford's history explicitly supplies 224 kW / 305 PS, 440 Nm,
  turbo five-cylinder, 2.5-litre class, driven front axle, Quaife differential,
  RevoKnuckle, 5.9 seconds to 100 km/h and 263 km/h. The following RS500
  section's 350 PS, modified hardware and performance are not transferred.
- Focus III: the original US 2017 brochure was rendered and its RS page 11
  and technical/equipment page 17 inspected. It specifies 350 hp and 350 lb-ft
  with 93-octane fuel, six-speed manual and torque-vectoring AWD. These are
  US ratings, not the European 350-PS / 440-Nm values. Factory-tested 0–62 mph
  and 165 mph stay in native units. Later front-LSD editions and optional RS2,
  tyres and winter wheels are excluded. The photo is a European-market 2016
  body-generation reference, not authentication of a US 2017 build.
- Fiesta B299: original US 2014 brochure pages 3 and 11 were rendered and
  inspected, including 93-octane and overboost notes. It specifies 197 hp,
  202 lb-ft, six-speed manual and peak-torque extension up to 17 seconds.
  The 182-PS European three-door, base PowerShift and later ST200/1.5-litre
  engine are not mixed in. Recaro seats remain optional. Top Gear's own
  historical award account supports the generation's significance; it does
  not claim the jury tested this selected US specification.
- Fiesta B479: Ford's May 7, 2018 press release is read through a clearly
  attributed reproduction at Auto International. Its opening and core launch
  figures were cross-checked against the indexed original Ford release. The
  original PDF could not be directly retrieved and is not claimed as visually
  inspected. The reproduction supports the 200-PS, 290-Nm three-cylinder,
  combined port/direct injection, cylinder deactivation, Ti-VCT and directional
  rear-spring development. Optional Quaife LSD and Launch Control are not
  standardised. Exact capacity, gearbox and numerical performance stay unfilled
  in this selected record; later 320-Nm facelift data is excluded. The award
  organiser separately confirms the 2018 Car of the Year recognition.
- Cortina: Lotus's own standard Type 28 column gives 1558 cc, four cylinders,
  twin cam, 105 bhp and four speeds; its original-model narrative supports
  light-alloy panel skins and aluminium clutch/differential housings. SE and
  racing power, Mk II/FVA equipment and undefined mass are excluded. The photo
  description's incorrect 1588-cc claim is not used as technical evidence.
- Carlton: Vauxhall's heritage record supplies 3615 cc, twin Garrett T25
  turbochargers, twin water-cooled intercoolers, 377 bhp, 419 lb-ft and chassis
  layout. Lotus's engineering-partner record supports six-speed manual. The
  latter page's erroneous fastest-production-car-overall claim is rejected;
  the admission concerns exceptional saloon performance. Undefined mass and
  contradictory performance conventions are omitted.
- Sunbeam: the original UK brochure C9579/1/20, October 1979, was rendered,
  with the complete specification page 6 and publication line visually
  inspected. It specifies the 2172-cc, twin-cam sixteen-valve Lotus engine,
  9.44:1, twin Dellortos, five-speed and road chassis/brakes. Four valves per
  cylinder is derived from sixteen valves and four cylinders. Native 150
  (DIN) bhp is retained without assuming metric PS or modern net kW.
  Lotus's current table incorrectly lists 1588 cc; that field is rejected.
  Its narrative and championship history support the collaboration's
  significance, without transferring near-250-bhp rally specifications.

All eight photos were visually checked. Competition Cortinas and a three-door
Fiesta were rejected for the selected road/two-door-saloon and five-door
references. The chosen Cortina is an early road-car exterior with accessory
wheels; photos do not authenticate original engines, options or build dates.
Creators, licences, source links and unchanged image bytes are preserved.

## Candidates still outside the catalogue

Escort RS1600 Mk I, RS1800 Mk II, RS1600i Mk III, RS Cosworth, Racing Puma,
Capri RS2600 and Lotus Cortina Mk II remain research candidates. Some sources
were inaccessible, lacked original road technical detail, or showed competition
cars. Listings labelled as period Escort brochures at OnlyManuals instead
contained a modern RS lineage poster; those are not used as period technical
evidence. A factory Group A Escort Cosworth build manual describes competition
modifications and cannot substitute for a standard road specification.
None of these seven candidates counts as an imported car. Restricted sources
were not bypassed and incomplete evidence did not prompt arbitrary replacements.
