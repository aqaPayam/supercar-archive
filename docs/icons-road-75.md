## Admission, reference and evidence

### Studebaker Golden Hawk

The Golden Hawk represents an important early American performance-coupe formula: a sleek hardtop body with a production supercharged V8. Its 1957 change from Packard power to Studebaker’s own boosted engine is documented by the marque museum. That purposeful forced-induction engineering and established sporting identity justify one original Golden Hawk family entry, rather than its fins, trim or rarity.

### Studebaker Avanti

The Avanti combined a radical Loewy-team coupe design with supercharged V8 performance engineering, standard front disc brakes and an overhead steel safety arch. Its fibreglass-on-steel construction and distinctive place in Studebaker’s final American performance-car programme make it an engineering and design icon. One original Studebaker family is admitted; later Avanti II cars and modified speed-record machines are not extra versions of this reference.

## What was actually read

The seven-page 1957 Hawk PDF was downloaded and rendered. Its final interior
specification spread (PDF index 5) and back cover (6) were visually inspected;
this is not a claim to have read all seven pages. The spread explicitly
separates Golden Hawk from Silver Hawk engines, brakes and wheelbase. Golden
Hawk keeps native 289 cu in, 275 hp, 333 lb-ft, compression and 120.5-in wheelbase.
No modern net/gross standard, speed, acceleration or power-to-weight ranking
is inferred. The Studebaker National Museum's actual exhibition text confirms
the supercharged 1957 change. Savoy's actual collection account was also read,
but its rounded dimensions, automatic gearbox and performance figures are not
substituted into the brochure reference.

The nineteen-page 1963 range PDF was downloaded and rendered. A contact sheet
located the relevant pages; Avanti page 2, power/transmission page 15, safety
page 16 and specification page 18 were then visually read at full size. The
other pages were not all read. Both PDFs are scanned and text extraction was
empty. R2/supercharged 9:1 is distinct from the unsupercharged 10.25:1 column.
The optional four-speed manual is not claimed as standard. The supercharged
column supplies no hp rating, so neither a guessed rating nor Bonneville-car
performance is imported. Front discs are distinct from 11-inch rear drums.
Absolute first-disc-brake claims are not adopted.

LeMay's actual restoration/provenance account was read. It supplies separate
fibreglass-on-steel and design-team context. Its early design date and varying
62R/63R and R1002/RS1002 strings are not used to certify a chassis or chronology.
Museum #1001 is a documented production application and restoration subject,
not a claim that the representative photo depicts it. Later Avanti II and
record-setting R3 cars do not define this original R2 road reference.
