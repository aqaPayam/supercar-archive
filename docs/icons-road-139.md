# Road-icon batch 139: original Bugatti Type 18 Black Bess

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 935 existing identities. One absent Type 18
road programme brings the working inventory to 936 records: 541 curated road
identifiers across 139 manifests plus 395 preserved baseline records. This is
not a certified qualifying count toward the 1,000-icon goal.

## Admission and identity

The Type 18 belongs through its early high-performance road engineering:
a large four-cylinder engine, overhead camshaft, three-valve cylinders and
sporting transmission. The Garros/Black Bess example connects that engineering
to an established part of Bugatti history. Low production and famous ownership
alone are not admission reasons; no exclusive fastest-car priority is asserted.

The full inventory was checked for Bugatti Type 18/Type18/T18, Black Bess,
Roland Garros and chassis 474 aliases. No original Type 18 road family was
present; an unrelated Toyota ST185 substring hit is not a match. Count all
Type 18 years, coachwork, survivors and racing appearances once. The later
Veyron Black Bess cosmetic tribute is not a new historical car. Dedicated
race machines, replicas and further individual chassis are not additionally
admitted.

The selected reference is the **Labourdette two-seater delivered to Garros in
September 1913 and preserved at Louwman Museum**, rather than a modern Veyron
or a generic racing-engine specification.

## Manufacturer and collection evidence

[Bugatti's original 10 April 2014 release](https://newsroom.bugatti.com/en/press-releases/les-l-gendes-de-bugatti-world-premiere-for-black-bess-at-auto-china-beijing-2014)
explicitly identifies Type 18 as a road car and documents the two-seater delivery
to Garros. Its historical sections and complete Type 18 technical description
were read. Ten attributes map to those sections: inline four, quoted 5 L,
100-mm bore, 160-mm stroke, three crank bearings, overhead camshaft, three
valves per cylinder, four forward gears plus reverse, 1.25-m track and two
seats. Track converts exactly to 1250 mm; no exact cc value is calculated
from the rounded displacement label.

The modern Veyron tribute sections are kept separate. W16 engine, performance,
carbon body, gold trim, interior leather and price do not become Type 18
specifications. No proof of present registration or independent chassis
originality certification is claimed from the retrospective release.

[The current Louwman Museum entry](https://www.louwmanmuseum.nl/en/collections/automobiles/bugatti-type-18-sports-two-seater-black-bess)
was read in full relevant scope. It confirms the 1913 collection reference and
Labourdette coachwork. Three attributes map here: quoted 100 hp, coachbuilder
and angled/staggered bucket seats. The output field explicitly lacks a stated
test standard or rpm; museum hp is not silently converted from manufacturer
PS/CV. Claimed original upholstery is an attributed museum description, not
an independent physical authentication.

## Disagreements kept out of specifications

The [French manufacturer counterpart](https://newsroom.bugatti.com/fr/communiques-de-presse/-les-l-gendes-de-bugatti-premi-re-mondiale-de-la-black-bess-au-salon-auto-china-beijing-2014)
was read in the relevant historical and technical sections and compared:

- English wheelbase is 2.45 m; French is 2.54 m. No wheelbase is imported.
- Manufacturer speed is 160 km/h; museum is 150 km/h. Neither becomes a measured
  maximum-speed field.
- English uses 100 PS in its technical paragraph and over 100 PS in narrative;
  French uses over 100 CV. The selected output remains the museum's separately
  quoted 100 hp, without inferring a shared test standard or numerical conversion.
- Chain-drive wording differs in specificity. No assumed camshaft or final-drive
  function is imported from that wording.
- Manufacturer and museum disagree on the aviation flight year and describe
  the later name's origin differently. Neither story is imported as fact.

No torque, output rpm, mass, acceleration, total production/survivors, trim
originality, naming etymology or detailed racing result is needed for admission.

## Licensed photograph

API metadata and [CC0 1.0 Universal dedication](http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en)
were read. The 1280 x 960 JPEG rendition was individually viewed and copied
unchanged. Black early road body, brass-coloured radiator/headlamps and upright
screen match the selected museum body. The image does not authenticate exact
engine, delivery date, chassis 474, upholstery or body originality, and does
not establish the modern tribute's gold-plating materials on the historic car.
