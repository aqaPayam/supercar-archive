## Admission and identity

The HBR5 carried Deutsch-Bonnet’s small-engine racing know-how into a series-built grand tourer. Its streamlined glass-fibre/polyester body over a lightweight steel backbone, combined with an air-cooled opposed twin and front-wheel drive, makes it a distinctive early lightweight GT. One original HBR5 coach family is counted. DB marque racing achievements explain the engineering context, not the tune or competition history of a particular road car; later engine options, Super Rallye names, body variations and years do not add separate entries.

The complete 873-car inventory was searched for HBR, Deutsch, Bonnet, Panhard,
DB and D.B. identities. No HBR5/Deutsch Bonnet/Panhard family was present.
The Bonnet match was the preserved René Bonnet/Matra Djet, an already-labelled
distinct mid-engine road programme, not an alias for this earlier front-drive
Panhard-derived coach. The DB substring hit in Subaru GDB was unrelated.
Original coach body variations, 851/848/954 cc engines, Super Rallye names and
model years will not add separate counts within this admitted HBR5 family.

The GT belongs for the lightweight streamlined composite/steel-backbone
engineering brought into a production grand tourer by a specialist with
substantial small-engine competition experience. No absolute first/second-ever
fibreglass-production claim or rarity-only rationale is required for admission.
DB marque race results are context, not a claim that every HBR5 road coach or
the photographed car achieved those victories.
