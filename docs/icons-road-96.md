## Series 1 XJ12, 9:1 carburetted V12 automatic — September 1972 English factory reference

The XJ12 brought Jaguar’s 5.3-litre V12 into the classic XJ sports saloon, combining a twelve-cylinder performance engine with independent suspension and four-wheel disc brakes. It is a landmark of Jaguar’s fast, refined four-door tradition. Count the classic Series 1/2/3 XJ sporting-road family once across XJ6/XJ12, Daimler Sovereign/Double Six, bodies, wheelbases and years; no trim or later engine tune adds an extra entry.

Original Series 1 XJ12, 1972 English-language Jaguar factory brochure reference, 9:1 carburetted specification. Actual PDF index 9 specification page, print footer 9/72, supplies 5343 cc sixty-degree V12, one overhead camshaft per bank, four carburetters, 309 bhp gross at 6250 rpm and 332 lb-ft gross at 4000 rpm. Gross ratings are explicit and remain gross; no modern net output, inferred kW/Nm conversion, later fuel injection or HE calibration is substituted. Borg-Warner automatic with torque converter is specified; an unprinted model number or gear count is not inferred. The brochure warns that specifications vary by country, so this is its printed reference rather than a claim that every 1972 export car had this tune. ## X300 XJR AJ16 supercharged, optional GM four-speed automatic — 1995 European technical-guide reference

The X300 XJR introduced supercharging to Jaguar’s R saloons. Its AJ16 straight-six paired an Eaton blower and liquid-cooled charge-air system with a production four-door body, establishing a defining strand of later Jaguar performance cars. Count this absent XJ40-derived X300/X308 sporting-saloon lineage once for now; naturally aspirated Sport trim, six-/eight-/twelve-cylinder variants, Daimler badges, transmissions and subsequent years do not add separate counts.

1995 model-year X300 XJR 4.0 supercharged catalyst engine, selected European optional GM 4L80E four-speed automatic. Actual Jaguar August 1994 technical introduction JJM 10 15 12/50 supplies model/market availability and selected engine/transmission details. The 4.0 SC column gives 3980 cc, DOHC inline six/four valves per cylinder, 91 × 102 mm, 8.5:1, 240 kW DIN at 5000 rpm and 512 Nm DIN at 3050 rpm. The same table prints 326 PS and 321.8 BHP, which are different units; native kW/Nm are retained without recomputing or calling 326 mechanical bhp. Eaton M90 with belt drive and an air-to-liquid intake-manifold intercooler are explicit. The guide says the Getrag five-speed is standard in Europe and the selected GM automatic optional there; it is not labelled universally standard. The 2013 retrospective’s different power-unit wording is not technical authority. Normally aspirated 3.2/4.0, X300 6.0 V12, later X308 V8, pre-production Thrust fire-car modifications and XJR-15 race data are excluded.

## Factory evidence actually inspected

The intact English XJ12 brochure PDF is 972,978 bytes and 19 pages. Its
specification sheet is PDF index 9, not the final PDF page. Actual full page 9
was visually read, including the September 1972 print footer and country-
variation disclaimer. It explicitly labels 309 bhp and 332 lb-ft GROSS,
with peaks at 6250 and 4000 rpm respectively. It states 5343 cc, sixty-degree
V12, one overhead camshaft per bank, four carburetters, 9:1, transistor ignition,
Borg-Warner torque-converter automatic, 3.31 rear axle, independent rubber-
mounted front/rear subframes, all-steel four-door body and four-wheel discs
with front ventilation, outboard fronts and inboard rears. No unprinted gearbox
model/gear count, net-output conversion, later injection/HE calibration or
E-type tune is inferred. The brochure's differing-country warning is retained;
this is its printed 9:1 reference, not every export calibration.

Actual XJ12 full indexes 0 (cover), 17 (refinement text) and 18 (photograph)
were also inspected. The all-page thumbnail contact sheet was navigation,
not a full technical audit. Other brochure pages were not individually read.
Jaguar's current historical account supplies the classic XJ context and V12
introduction. Its modern 198-kW/265-hp summary and fastest-saloon claim are
not substituted for the original gross table or certified as universal test
results. V12-era performance importance supports admission without importing
a top-speed or acceleration claim.

The intact X300 PDF is 29,452,018 bytes and 136 scanned pages. Its actual cover
identifies Jaguar Technical Publications, August 1994, 1995 model year,
JJM 10 15 12/50. PDF sequence and printed page order differ substantially;
references below identify both, rather than assuming a fixed page offset.
Actual full indexes 41 (printed 2, lineup), 57 (printed 18, AJ16 table),
2 (printed 22, SC manifold), 6 (printed 26, blower/intercooler), 66 (printed 33,
charge-air cooling) and 18 (printed 57, transmissions) were read, plus cover 0.
Only their selected statements supply imported technical fields.

The engine table's 4.0 SC catalyst column gives 3980 cc, inline six, DOHC four
valves per cylinder, 91 × 102 mm, 8.5:1, 240 kW DIN/326 PS/321.8 BHP at
5000 rpm and 512 Nm DIN at 3050 rpm. These printed units are not interchangeable:
the later retrospective's 326-bhp wording is not adopted. No peak-speed,
maximum-rev or naturally aspirated 3.2/4.0-column value is accidentally selected.
The period guide's native kW/Nm values are retained without recalculation.

The selected induction is the second-generation Eaton M90, belt-driven, with
an air-to-liquid fin-and-tube intercooler integrated into the inlet manifold.
The cooling page shows the electric intercooler pump and circuit; it is not
mislabelled air-to-air. The lineup specifically associates 4.0 SC with XJR.
The transmission text specifies the GM 4L80E four-speed for this engine,
standard outside Europe and optional in Europe, where the Getrag 290 five-
speed is standard. This record selects the European automatic option; it does
not assert universal standard fitment. Neither the V12 column nor generic
naturally aspirated automatic descriptions replace this model-specific text.
No fuel-system, suspension, braking, weight, tyre, driven-wheel or individual
ratio field is inferred from unseen pages just to lengthen the profile.

The X300 contact sheets covering indexes 0–69 and 105–135 were visually used
for navigation only; the 70–104 sheet was generated but not viewed. Other full
renders were generated but not all inspected. Both PDFs have empty extracted

The guide warns that it complements service publications; this is a bounded
reference review, not certification of every page or every production revision.

Its earlier XJ40, later V8 X308 and later aluminium XJ material are not new
