## Technical evidence boundaries

The S660 is the Alpha six-speed manual launch reference. Its rounded engine
class remains rounded; exact displacement, power and unprinted fields are not
guessed. The first-mini-vehicle six-speed statement retains Honda's March 2015
internal-research qualification and does not apply to all compact cars worldwide.
CVT, Concept Edition and later Mugen derivatives do not create extra entries.

The 1987 Prelude selects the DOHC Si with mechanical steering-angle-sensing 4WS,
not the SOHC engine, export tune, universal ABS or a later electronic system.
The precise steering innovation is distinguished from earlier forms of 4WS.
Native net PS and kg-m are retained. The fifth Prelude uses the original 1996
Type S engine, ATTS and specification sections, whose model columns were read.
Its 220-PS engine, five-speed manual and torque-transfer hardware are not the
SiR, an export Type SH tune, automatic or rear-steering option. H22A is labelled
as an engine code; its inline-four layout maps to the specification matrix.

Toyota's December 1989 table is read by column: the three-door GT has its own
4E-FTE turbo-EFI engine and native net output. The blank kW field stays blank.
Soleil and Si columns, later naturally aspirated four-wheel-drive cars, inferred
transmission codes and the already archived EP91 are excluded. No speculative
torque or later modified-car figures are used.

The G87 M2 announcement is dated 2022 but states April 2023 introduction. It
selects the international manual option; the US test's standard-equipment wording,
horsepower and measured times are not transferred. The contemporary drive supports
the engaging engine/chassis case while acknowledging weight and steering-feel
limitations. Carbon roof, buckets, track tyres, raised speed limit and later
power updates are not universal. The F13 M6 uses the original German 2012 coupe
release, with native kW/Nm and coupe-specific roof, not the US model-year convention,
convertible, four-door Grand Coupe or later Competition tune.

The B8 RS 4 uses the original 2012 naturally aspirated V8 estate release, including
its dual-clutch and crown-gear centre differential. The rear sport differential,
DRC, ceramics, larger wheels and raised speed are optional. The B9 uses Audi
Netherlands' 2017 unveiling account, which explicitly expects local arrival in
2018; the latter is the selected introduction year. Its biturbo V6 and eight-speed
automatic are not combined with B8 specifications. Optional equipment and later
facelifts/packages do not create additional records. A separate release host's
TLS certificate-chain failure was not bypassed; the working official brand
release supplies the selected evidence.
