### Renault Safrane

The Safrane Biturbo is an exceptional engineering collaboration within Renault’s performance-road history: Hartge developed the twin-turbo PRV V6 while Irmscher worked on the chassis, paired with permanent four-wheel drive and controlled suspension. Its significance lies in this integrated high-performance flagship programme, not simply luxury or rarity. One original Safrane sporting family is counted; RXE/Baccara trim, model years, ordinary Safranes and the Long Cours show-car derivative do not add separate entries.

### Renault 21

The Renault 21 2L Turbo is an established French sporting saloon of the late-1980s turbo era. Its road engine combined coordinated electronic ignition, injection and boost control with standard ABS, while the much more powerful Ragnotti racing derivative established the family’s touring-car heritage. One original 21 sporting family is counted, not ordinary saloons, Medallion badges, Quadra drivetrains, catalysed tunes, model years or racing evolutions as extra cars.

## Primary evidence actually inspected

The Renault-authored French press kit *Renault : une saga turbo depuis 40 ans*,
dated July 15, 2019, was downloaded as a 21-page facsimile from AM Today. Its
Renault-branded cover and actual PDF indexes 16 and 17 (printed pages 17–18)
were rendered and visually read in full. The selected chapters expressly
identify the R21 2L Turbo under 1987 and Safrane Biturbo under 1993. These are
historical programme references, not proof of a photographed chassis's model
year or exact customer-delivery date. Other PDF chapters were not all read.

The R21 chapter describes the 1995 cc type-J-based road engine, directly states
129 kW / 175 ch DIN at 5200 rpm and 270 Nm at 3000 rpm, and explicitly separates
the 162-ch catalysed version. It identifies coordinated electronic ignition,
injection and boost control, water-cooled/electronically controlled Garrett T3,
two air-to-air intercoolers, light-alloy engine and standard ABS. It describes
a much more powerful Ragnotti racing derivative separately. That racing output,
Superproduction 4x4 specification and ice-record preparation/speeds are not road
attributes. Exact engine-code suffix, gearbox, curb weight, acceleration and
a measured top-speed claim for this car are not invented.

The Safrane chapter describes Hartge engine and Irmscher chassis development,
twin KKK K04 turbochargers with electronic management, permanent four-wheel
drive and controlled suspension. It prints **193 kW (268 ch DIN)** at 5500 rpm.
That numerical pairing is internally inconsistent. The record retains only the
original 268 ch DIN figure and does not import 193 kW or silently insert a
corrected conversion. Its 365 Nm at 2500 rpm is separately sourced. It describes
a powerful luxury/performance flagship rather than a stripped racing car;
admission rests on the integrated engineering programme, not a claim of track
specialisation or merely being expensive/rare. No Long Cours concept, Monaco
safety-car or ordinary V6 Quadra specification is imported into the road record.

The actual full indexed English Safrane and French R21 museum profile texts
were read through web search. Direct museum opens returned 503, so this is
explicitly indexed manufacturer evidence rather than an unseen live page.
The Safrane profile supplies 2963 cc and road-model context; its 1992 show
presentation and 1993 display reference do not establish first customer
availability, so the database's Safrane model-year fields remain unspecified.
The R21 profile supports the inline-four/front-drive road layout and explicitly
separates Quadra and race references. Its 1993 display header is not substituted
for the original 1987 programme. Exact production totals, dimensions, ice-record
measurements and current-record claims are not imported.

The official Alpine event landing page was visible in indexed search, but its
raw request failed certificate verification. Renault Germany history pages
were visible through the web reader/search, but their raw requests also failed
certificate verification; their remaining text is not silently treated as read.
An English facsimile host returned 403. Neither certificate verification nor
access controls were bypassed. The actual French facsimile read above is the
technical source; no unseen English PDF or brochure/shop listing supplies data.
