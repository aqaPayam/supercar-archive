## Admission and reference

The Samba Rallye was a purpose-developed sub-1300 cc sporting road hatchback and a foundation for Talbot’s customer rallying programme. Its 1219 cc engine with twin Weber carburettors and five-speed front-drive layout, together with documented FIA production recognition, give it a genuine place among early French rally-oriented hot hatches. One original Samba family is counted. The different 1360 cc UK Samba S, later engine versions, badges and competition evolutions do not add separate entries.

## Evidence actually inspected

The original A-5157 recognition was downloaded as a 32-page combined PDF.
Base pages at PDF indexes 0–5 and 9 were rendered and visually read. These
identify the production model, four seats, steel unitary body, 1219 cc engine,
front transverse layout, aluminium block/head, wet liners, bore/stroke, chain-
driven overhead camshaft, rocker operation, two Weber 40 DCOE carburettors,
front drive and the manual gearbox. One inlet and one exhaust valve per
cylinder support the two-valve entry. The additional gearbox column and
alternative final drives are not selected or generalized into road equipment.
The other pages and option extensions were not all read.

The 13-page N-5157 supplement was downloaded. Its cover, mechanical and
gearbox pages at PDF indexes 0, 4 and 5 were rendered and visually read.
It expressly complements A-5157 and shows the base five-speed manual set.
Competition minimum weight, maximum permitted bore/capacity, cages, modified
suspension/brakes, optional ratios and later correction/evolution sheets are
not imported. A Group B 232 PDF was downloaded as a separate research lead
but was not visually read or used for specifications. Recognition forms do
not certify a particular photographed chassis's registration or original tune.

The scanned covers stamp A-5157 July 1, 1983 and N-5157 December 1, 1983;
the portal metadata shows the following day. The scanned dates identify the
reference documents, rather than establishing a road-car production-start date.
No horsepower, torque, road performance, production total, competition minimum
weight or current event eligibility is claimed from these forms.

The actual Simca Talbot Club road-model history was read for the sporting
purpose, customer-rallying context and market distinction. Its French/Benelux/
German 1219 cc Rallye differs from the 1360 cc UK Samba S. Its output, speed,
production and ownership claims are not substituted for primary mechanical
evidence. The admission is based on the documented sporting model and production
recognition, not on an ordinary Samba merely being old or uncommon.

Brochure searches found blocked/unavailable hosts. Two tiny hosted promotional
images were viewed but do not provide readable technical tables and are not
used as specification evidence. No unseen brochure content or membership-only
material was used and access controls were not bypassed.
