## Technical evidence boundaries

### Simca

The original Chrysler France leaflet's body spread and technical back page
were read from full-size scans. It explicitly offers road performance alongside
competition use, with four discs, reinforced suspension and a synchromesh
gearbox. Native CH DIN is retained. The leaflet is undated: its host's date
range and Le Mans advertisement are not proof of a particular print year.
The 1972 introduction is separately documented by the
[Caramulo custodian](https://museudocaramulo.pt/en/colecoes/simca-1000-rallye-2/).
That museum's later prepared race car does not supply the factory road tune.
Its speed/power differences are not silently combined with brochure figures.
Rallye 1, Rallye 3 and extreme competition conversions are excluded.

### Copen

The Japanese launch release establishes the selected five-speed Active Top
reference. Its four-cylinder/16-valve description gives four valves per cylinder.
No unprinted displacement is guessed. The first-kei powered-roof claim remains
qualified to June 2002. The manufacturer’s
[20-year history](https://www.daihatsu.com/jp/news/2022/20220619-1.html)
adds evidence of the original car's enduring following and flagship role.
Later detachable-roof and Sports Pack equipment, Ultimate trim, export engine
and the second generation's D-Frame/DRESS-FORMATION are not transferred.

### Delahaye

Museo Nicolis documents its own 1939 Chapron 135 M cabriolet and Cotal
mechanism. Power and speed are retained as custodian quotations. Its blank
capacity and implausible dimension units are excluded. No invented correction,
racing S/CS tune, MS engine specification, modern automatic-transmission claim,
film appearance or original-condition assumption supplies technical evidence.
The family is admitted for its established touring/sporting identity, not
rarity, luxury, museum display or celebrity association alone.
