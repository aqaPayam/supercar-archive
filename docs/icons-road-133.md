## Admission and identity

The 1093 is an early Renault series-built rally homologation saloon, with
substantial French rally and driver-development heritage. Its 1962 Tour de
Corse success explains admission; being an old, rare Dauphine does not.
The complete inventory was checked for Dauphine, Ondine, R1093/1093, Renault,
Gordini and related Alfa Romeo/Argentine identities. None of the original
Dauphine family was present. Existing Renault 8/12/17 Gordini profiles are
different vehicle programmes, not alternative spellings of a Dauphine.

Count the original Dauphine sporting-road programme once. Do not additionally
count Gordini/Ondine relationships, years, individual racers, restored chassis,
export cars, Argentine conversion kits or equipment updates. Ordinary Dauphines
are not separately admitted. Pending variant/racer questions remain pending.

## Early factory road reference

[Renault's original announcement](https://www.dauphine1093.fr/Acceuil/Documentation/Html/lancement.html)
is dated 30 November 1961 and explicitly offers a complete factory car for
everyday sporting use as well as rallying, rather than individual conversion
of a standard Dauphine. Its two complete text pages, lancementr.jpg and
lancementv.jpg, were downloaded and individually visually inspected. Renault
letterhead and date establish origin. A later JUL 2 1962 stamp on the verso
is not the announcement date. The two ancillary photographs were downloaded
but not used as technical evidence. Selected reference: early French R1093,
1962 series; no claim that every 1962 car was identical.

Thirteen attributes map to this announcement: 845 cc, 49 CV DIN (55 CV SAE)
at 5600 rpm, 9.2:1 compression, Solex 32 PAIA 3, dry filter, domed pistons,
double valve springs, reinforced Ferodo clutch, special springs, finned front
drum brakes, launch tyre alternatives, 12 V and 180-mm lamps. Keep DIN and SAE
labels explicit. No generic 55-hp value or later disc brakes are substituted.
No measured performance, claimed top speed, optional final drive or production
total is needed for admission.

The [registration description](https://www.dauphine1093.fr/Acceuil/Documentation/Html/notice.html)
was inspected in all three displayed images, fichmin1.jpg, fichmin2.jpg and
fichmin3.jpg. It explicitly describes a passenger car and registration purpose,
despite competition in the type title. Six bounded attributes use it: rear
longitudinal engine, inline four, four-speed manual, independent wheels, four
doors and four seats. The sample includes individual conformity paperwork,
1961 approval dates and a 12-62 printing. No chassis history is inferred.
Its 5800-rpm peak-power reference differs from the launch's 5600 rpm; retain
the selected launch output intact. Differing track/overhang values, unclear
small print, handwritten ratios, mass and dimensions are not imported.

The [FIA index](https://historicdb.fia.com/car/renault-dauphine-1093)
supports RWD, homologation 1084, Group T, 845 cc, four cylinders and France.
The index and complete extension list were read. It says 23 November 1961,
while other listings say 22 November: do not assert an exact homologation day.
Gearbox, brake, commercial-name and later equipment extensions are not early
road specification. The linked full PDF was discovered but not downloaded or
read; no claim to have inspected its pages is made.

## Historical context

[Gilles Bonnafous's original 25 July 2005 event feature](https://www.motorlegend.com/evenement-automobile/rassemblement/world-series-renault/renault-dauphine-1093/6,11573,11569.html)
was read in full. It reports Renault Histoire et Collection's restored 1093
and Jean Vinatier at Le Mans, then explains the programme's rally history.
Its account identifies the 1962 Tour de Corse victory by Pierre Orsini and
Jean Canonicci and the model's role in driver development. This is original
event coverage and retrospective history, not a contemporary 1962 road test.
The feature also identifies drum braking as a weakness. No universal dominance,
stock-road race performance, exact later brake-extension date, production
total or authenticity claim for the collection car is imported.

Owners-club history was read as discovery/context; it supplies none of the
20 technical fields. The official Originals Renault Gordini page was located
but its standard Gordini figures were not borrowed for the 1093.
