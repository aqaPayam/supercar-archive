## Identity and duplicate review

The complete 621-car inventory was searched across identifiers, manufacturer,
model family, generation and variant for Escort, Capri, RS1600 / RS 1600,
RS1800 / RS 1800, RS2600 / RS 2600 and Cosworth aliases. None of these four road
identities was present. The existing Sierra RS Cosworth is a distinct road family,
although it contributed engineering to the Escort. Mk I and Mk II Escorts have
distinct bodies/generations. RS1600i is a later front-drive model, not RS1600.
No additional Escort Twin Cam/Mexico/RS2000, Custom/Luxe, engine/year/facelift,
Capri RS3100, competition evolution or export badge is counted.

The older pending Escort RS Cosworth queue item is resolved and replaced by this
reviewed French road reference. The inaccessible UK brochure is not claimed as read.

## Technical evidence

Mappings and public admission reasons are saved in
[data/icons-road-26.json](../data/icons-road-26.json).

Escort RS Cosworth uses an original Ford France August 1992 brochure obtained
through BCVN/InspiraAuto's public PDF link. Its nine scanned PDF pages were
rendered as a contact sheet; pages 2, 6 and 7 were separately inspected at
reading resolution (printed pages 2–3 and 12–14). The back cover was also
read at full resolution and confirms the printed August 1992 date. They establish nominal 2.0-litre
DOHC/16-valve, native 227 ch DIN at 6250 rpm, 290 Nm at 3500 rpm, 8:1 compression,
Garrett T3/04B, two-stage charge cooling, Weber Marelli injection/management,
34:66 AWD, centre/rear differential control, road suspension and ABS with
278-mm ventilated front / 273-mm solid rear discs. These technical features are
common to the road Motorsport and Luxe columns; Luxe convenience features are
not universal standard equipment. Motorsport is a road trim, not a prepared
rally car. Exact cc and gearbox details absent from the reviewed pages are not
borrowed, and native ch is not relabelled UK bhp. Later small-turbo hardware,
race outputs, world-first and numeric downforce claims are omitted.

Capri uses original Ford German road-handbook scans hosted by Capri Switzerland.
The technical-data leaf (bordbuch-c) was visually read: effective 2637 cc, native
150 PS DIN at 5800 rpm, 22.8 mkp at 3500 rpm and Kugelfischer injection. Tax
capacity 2615 cc is not effective displacement. The gearbox leaf (bordbuch-e)
was visually read for four forward ratios. The handbook edition date is not
established: 1970 marks family introduction, not the exact revision of this
scan. Compression, brake changes, weight, top speed and acceleration are omitted.
Adjacent tuning/competition/accessory illustrations do not establish standard
road equipment. Ford's July 31, 2018 retrospective, explicitly credited to Ford
and reproduced by FordFan, independently documents V6, four seats and the road
chassis development, separating it from lightweight competition cars. The native
torque unit is retained without conversion.

RS1600 uses the original Ford/RAC base recognition form 1605, list 1970/10,
preserved in FIA's 74-page file. Pages 1–11 were rendered; pages 1 and 8–11 were
individually read at useful resolution. The printed base form identifies the
production model, start of manufacture and October 1, 1970 recognition. It
specifies 1601 cc, inline four, cast-iron block/aluminium head, toothed-belt twin
overhead cams, four valves per cylinder, twin Weber 40 DCOEs, water cooling,
wet sump and a four-speed manual. Page 10 expressly labels the output as
manufacturer catalogue declarations: 122 BHP at 6500 rpm and 112 lb-ft at
4000 rpm. Those exact native declarations are labelled in the public fields;
no DIN/SAE standard or kW conversion is invented. They are not silently combined
with later 115-PS/120-hp summaries. No maximum-speed claim is made where the form
says it was not declared. Alternative ratios and LSD wording are not universal
standard equipment. Later Group 2 options, two-litre engines, RS1800 amendments
and modern historic-racing updates are excluded.

RS1800 uses Ford Rallye Sport Marketing's original FP326 October 1975 outer
foldout, reproduced as a readable scan by My Classic UK. The centre RS1800 column
explicitly describes a road version and gives an aluminium four-cylinder
16-valve BDA, native 115 PS DIN at 6000 rpm, heavy-duty body/suspension,
four-speed all-synchromesh gearbox and road air dam/spoiler. Standard bucket
seats are separate from Custom reclining seats/headrests. The left RS2000 column
is excluded: its 1993-cc OHC engine, 110 PS, carburettor, brake sizes and detailed
suspension geometry do not supply RS1800 fields. Exact displacement, torque and
competition tunes are omitted where this selected road column does not supply
them. Only the outer foldout is claimed as individually read.

Ford's current factory rally-history article independently explains the Mk I
and Mk II lineage and competition achievements. Those results justify admission;
they do not mean the stock road engines won WRC events. The Mexico-winning
Kent-powered car is not asserted to have been an RS1600. Current article section
headings describing competition years do not replace original road launch dates.

Some legacy Ford HTML links now redirect to the current homepage; old PDF routes
and the UK Cosworth brochure returned errors. OnlyManuals' purported early sales
brochure summaries describe a later RS timeline, not usable original technical
brochures, and its challenge was not bypassed. No inaccessible source or search
snippet is treated as reviewed technical evidence.

## Photography

All four final images were visually reviewed, with unchanged Commons image bytes,
creator, licence, original description URL and SHA-256 retained. Cosworth uses
Calreyn88's CC BY-SA 4.0 1994 UK road car with the large wing; aftermarket wheels,
later year and market are qualified. A wingless/uncertain earlier candidate was
not selected. Capri uses Alf van Beem's CC0 1973 RS2600 at Zandvoort in 2010;
facelift body details and restoration do not authenticate an early build or the
undated handbook revision. The earlier Capri image lacked clear creator metadata
and was not selected; unrelated ordinary 2600 GT/export cars are not substitutes.

RS1600 uses MrWalkr's CC BY-SA 4.0 white 1971 road-shaped car, with normal road
interior and qualified supplementary lamps/restoration. RS1800 uses Andrea
Boano's CC BY 4.0 indoor-show photograph of a 1975 road saloon. Current trim,
wheels and restoration do not certify the selected engine or standard/Custom
specification. Viewed Daily Mirror/circuit RS1600s and Cossack/competition RS1800s
were rejected because their flares, cages or preparation do not represent the
selected stock road references. Factory brochure scans are evidence, not
open-licensed replacement gallery photography.
