generation and variant for W116/450 SEL/6.9, M100/Type 100/Elan and Vallelunga.

neither is added again. The existing Mangusta is a different De Tomaso programme.
No Vallelunga, W116 or M100 reference was present. This is a bounded admission

## Evidence boundaries

The 450 SEL 6.9 uses Mercedes-Benz Classic's 21 April 2020 factory press release,
pages 1–2, downloaded and rendered for visual reading. Its spring/May 1975
reference retains 6834 cc, 210 kW at 4250 rpm, 550 Nm at 3000 rpm, adapted
three-speed automatic, dry sump and hydropneumatic level control. The earlier
W109 6.3 and US catalyst tune are not substituted. The source's awkward sentence
about unchanged displacement before describing an enlarged bore is not imported.
No current valuation, universal best-car claim, acceleration or mass is asserted.
Its performance-saloon engineering, not an ordinary luxury grade, earns admission.

The M100 uses the original Lotus technical supplement's Elan SE column on PDF
page 3, rendered and visually read. It specifies 1588 cc, transverse four-cylinder,
DOHC 16-valve aluminium head/iron block, turbo/intercooler, multipoint injection,
123 kW at 6600 rpm, 200 Nm at 4200 rpm, front-wheel drive, five-speed manual
transaxle and 3.833 final drive. The undated supplement is not claimed to have
been printed in 1989: Lotus heritage dates the programme's sale to that year.
The separate main brochure's final panel carries the Lotus/Hethel identity.
Naturally aspirated 97-kW/142-Nm data, US 162-hp equipment, later S2 and generic
heritage mass/power are excluded. Lotus heritage acknowledges the unusual
front-drive direction and commercial disappointment. William Jeanes's original
March 1991 US driving assessment, read in Car and Driver's archive, supports the
chassis interest while criticising price/exclusivity. Its US measurements and
equipment are not transferred to this early UK/European SE. One M100 generation
is counted, not every powertrain, model year or market.

De Tomaso's own Vallelunga history/table supports the Ford 1498-cc naturally
aspirated four, Weber carburettors, two valves per cylinder, fibreglass body,
backbone/rear tubular structure, structurally used engine, Volkswagen/Hewland
four-speed manual, rear drive, disc brakes and rack-and-pinion steering.
Its 1963–1968 programme heading includes the 1963 Turin concept. Bonhams's
public 2019 catalogue, printed page 60 / PDF page 212, explicitly dates the first
road car to 1965. That downloaded page was rendered and visually read. The entry
itself is the P70 sports racer: its V8 and competition specifications are not
Vallelunga evidence. A separate Vallelunga Competizione auction result was also
excluded from this road reference. Factory story/table power conflicts (104 bhp
versus 100 hp) remain unresolved, so numerical power is omitted. No uncertain
production total or dry-mass basis is imported. First-road-programme significance
and the chassis lineage to later De Tomaso engineering provide the admission case.
