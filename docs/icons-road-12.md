## Identity and duplicate review

The complete inventory and earlier manifests were checked. Renault's existing
mid-engine 5 Turbo is separate from the later front-drive Supercinq GT Turbo.
The two are not aliases. There is no second Phase 2 or Alain Oreille record.
The R8 is absent, including its numeric/worded aliases. Spider is absent and
receives one road reference, with the optional windscreen qualified; the Trophy
race car is excluded.

Mégane was absent. The R26.R and 275 Trophy-R represent its second and third
generations respectively, not extra performance trims of an existing generation.
Clio III is distinct from the existing Clio I Williams and Clio II V6. Its
Cup road trim does not mean a dedicated Cup race car. No 197, Raider or
additional same-generation hatch is added.

The existing Alpine A110 is the modern aluminium mid-engine model. The
original rear-engine Berlinette is a substantially different historical
generation. Only one original road specification is selected; 1300, SC/SI,
SX, Group 4 and licensed local builds are not extra entries in this batch.

## Technical evidence and limitations

- **R8:** Renault's museum explicitly separates the 1108-cc four-speed and
  1255-cc five-speed road versions. The latter is selected. Native museum
  110-hp notation remains unconverted because its rating convention is
  undefined. Race achievements explain the family's importance without
  supplying race power or chassis modifications to the road record.
- **Supercinq:** the museum distinguishes Phase 1's 115-hp/197-km/h figures
  from Phase 2's 120-hp/204-km/h figures. Only Phase 1 data is used, including
  the 1397-cc cast-iron four and Garrett T2. Native hp remains qualified.
  Oreille's rally success is historical context, not a road power rating.
- **Spider:** the museum's 1998-cc four-cylinder, rear mid-engine, rear drive,
  aluminium chassis and native 150 hp describe the road series. The 180-hp
  Trophy is excluded. Renault's separate open-car history explicitly confirms
  an optional windscreen, supporting the photographed body. That retrospective
  gives a different 147-PS output and different mass/speed; these are not
  combined with the museum reference. No undefined mass is imported.
- **R26.R:** Renault Deutschland's archived technical release directly gives
  169 kW, 310 Nm, 5500-rpm power peak, six-speed manual, front drive and
  standard LSD. Although the page is dated April 2009, it retains the 2008
  launch narrative; Renault's original July 22, 2008 announcement corroborates
  the October introduction and category benchmark. Optional titanium exhaust,
  roll cage and Toyo tyres are not assumed. Exact displacement and undefined
  weight remain unfilled. An inaccessible PDF is not claimed as inspected.
- **275 Trophy-R:** the June 2014 French-market equipment release separates
  standard Cup chassis, LSD, Öhlins dampers, Cup 2 tyres, titanium exhaust and
  two seats from the optional Record Kit. Its English 275-bhp notation is
  retained as written, without conversion. A late paragraph incorrectly says
  rear-wheel drive; the release's explicit front-drive category description
  is used instead. Lithium battery, accessory brakes and harnesses are not
  assumed. No undefined mass becomes a curb-weight figure.
- **Clio:** the original UK 2012 Renaultsport range brochure was read and PDF
  page 11 (printed pages 20–21) rendered and visually inspected. Its 200 Cup
  road column directly gives 1998 cc, 147.5 ISO kW, 215 Nm, 7100 rpm and
  chassis details. Optional Recaros and full-equipment 200/Sport-chassis or
  later turbo/EDC figures are excluded. The photo is an Australian-market
  body-generation reference, not proof of UK equipment.
- **Original A110:** Renault Classic's bilingual *Les Cahiers Passion*
  monograph separates road and race versions. Page 34's version table was
  rendered and inspected: 1600 VB, 1565 cc, 122 CH DIN, Renault 16 TS origin.
  The narrative's 1970 catalogue section adds dual-circuit braking and front
  radiator. The table dates the version to 1969; using a 1970 road reference
  does not create a separate year variant. Its native 122 CH DIN is preserved,
  without substituting other popular ratings or competition figures. Group 4
  1596/1796-cc engines and later SC/SI specifications are excluded. Alpine's
  own heritage article separately supports the backbone/glassfibre structure;
  that article's erroneous production-date span is not used.

## Photography and held candidates

All seven selected photos were visually inspected. A wrongly labelled R8
Major and a competition-prepared Gordini were rejected. The chosen R8 image
identifies a 1968 road saloon. The Supercinq has accessory cosmetic details.
The Spider shows its optional windscreen; the photo's title does not establish
its production year. The R26.R photo identifies a 2009 example. The Trophy-R
photo shows a 2015 Australian-market car of the same generation. The Clio
photo identifies a 2012 Australian 200 Cup. The A110 image identifies a 1971
1600 S exterior with auxiliary lamps; its exact road/modified configuration,
installed engine and factory equipment are not authenticated. Gallery captions
preserve these limits. Creators, licences, original links and image bytes are
retained unchanged.

Peugeot 306 GTi-6 remains a research candidate. Manufacturer history supports
its significance and six-speed identity, but the original technical brochure
was not successfully retrieved; third-party service data does not substitute
for the selected primary-source technical evidence. Alpine A310/A610 and the
fourth-generation Mégane Trophy-R likewise remain outside this import pending
complete reference review. None counts as a catalogue addition.
