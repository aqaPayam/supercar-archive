## Identity

The complete inventory was checked for Alfasud/ti/Sprint, Saxo/VTS/16v,
R-Type/R Type/Continental, Turbo R/Mulsanne, VX220/Speedster, Metro/6R4,
Flaminia/Sport/Super Sport and Visa/1000 Pistes/Mille Pistes aliases.
All eight families were absent. Existing Bentley Continental GT is the modern
2003 W12 family, not the post-war R-Type. Other marques' Speedsters and Japanese
Turbo RS/Type R substring matches are unrelated.

VX220 and Opel Speedster are market counterparts and contribute one addition.
Each other family also receives one representative reference. No Alfasud
engine/body grades, low-output VTS, other Bentley coachwork/LWB, VX220 2.2/VXR,
Metro International, earlier Flaminia Sport/other coachbuilders or Visa Evolution
is counted. These selections do not depend on the pending decisions about
extra same-generation variants or dedicated racers/prototypes.

## Evidence and boundaries

Every imported field and admission reason is mapped in
[the manifest](../data/icons-road-42.json).

- Alfasud uses the manufacturer's actual engineering history. It explicitly
  separates the 1973 two-door ti with 1.2-litre engine and twin-choke Weber from
  the 1971 standard-car table. Original shared chassis principles support the
  boxer/FWD, suspension and four-disc layout. Native English hp notation is
  retained without conversion. Later 1300/QV/Sprint and race values are excluded.
- Saxo uses Citroën's April 2026 history, selecting the original 120-hp TU5J4
  16v performance car. VTR and later low-output VTS badges are not equivalent.
  The article's erroneous under-30-second 0–100 figure and unspecified-basis
  mass are omitted. Redline and power peak remain distinct, hp is the source's
  own notation, and Super 1600 hardware is not assigned to the road car.
- R-Type uses Bentley's three-page May 2022 release, rendered and read in full.
  The original programme is distinguished from the 1951 Olga prototype and
  modern Continental GT. Bentley's owned BC16C/JAS949 record establishes the
  1953 manual/original 4.6 reference. HJ Mulliner construction and original tyre
  choice do not assert the current equipment of every surviving chassis.
- Turbo R uses the actual original-programme section of Bentley's October 2025
  PDF, visually read, plus the factory model chronology. Early 1985 chassis
  changes are separated from 1987 fuel injection, the 1991 collection example
  and 2025 Flying Spur tributes. Native quoted bhp has no guessed rpm or rating
  standard. Performance superlatives, other variants and build totals are omitted.
- VX220 uses the main body of an explicitly attributed original company press
  release hosted by Ultimatecarpage. Unattributed compiled technical tables
  supply no fields. The original Turbo engine installation, chassis, suspension,
  ABS, tyres, cooling and spoilers are distinguished from tuned cars. A separate
  factory history establishes rear drive, Hethel assembly and the Opel/Vauxhall
  alias; its Opel-market outputs are not transferred to the UK reference.
- Metro uses the actual Austin Rover brochure, with Clubman and International
  technical columns/engine pages rendered and read. The 250-DIN-bhp single-throttle
  synchromesh customer reference does not inherit six throttles, dog engagement
  or 380–410-bhp works tune. The contemporary Motor January 1986 feature was
  rendered at enlarged readable resolution and read; it explicitly calls the
  customer car a road car. The museum independently identifies a 1985 Clubman.
  Conflicting compression, shell-material descriptions and stage ratios are
  omitted. This does not add the dedicated works International competition car.
- Flaminia uses the actual custodian's 1965 Super Sport catalogue, cross-read in
  Italian/English, plus manufacturer heritage recognition. The museum's conflicting
  150/187 quantities, malformed dimension units, absolute claims and uncorroborated
  headlamp wording are excluded. Only supported six-cylinder/displacement/native-CV
  and body/design details are imported; no unprinted gearbox or engine-bank
  specification is guessed. Earlier Sport, Touring and Pininfarina data are excluded.
- Visa uses the actual manufacturer/FISA base B-258 cover, engine/camshaft,
  transmission and construction pages and signed production certificate, rendered
  and read. The cover is effective 1 March 1984; 1360.7 cc is the actual capacity,
  not permitted overbore. Optional and Evolution extensions are excluded. Citroën's
  own history separates the 1983 experimental winner from production. Aguttes'
  original description of a dealer-demonstrator customer example explicitly
  records French road registration. Sporting homologation itself is not presented
  as a street-approval document. Works Evolution listings and the failed external
  catalogue download are not treated as read evidence.

Citroën Origins downloaded successfully using normal system trust after the
custom research CA bundle lacked its issuer; certificate verification stayed on.
Access errors on other sources were not bypassed. No purchase or seller contact
was made.

## Photographs

All eight selected Commons images were visually inspected, with original source
pages, named creators, CC0/CC BY/CC BY-SA versions, licence links and unchanged
downloaded-byte hashes retained. Captions qualify exact year, market, chassis,
engine tune and current equipment. Original-body Alfasud and pre-facelift 16v Saxo
images were preferred. The R-Type image is not passed off as BC16C; the Metro is
identified as the museum's Clubman despite its competition livery. Heavily
modified VX220 matches with large added wings, Visa prototypes and works-driver
display images were not selected. The Flaminia photographer's alternate output
figure is not adopted as technical evidence. Appearance alone
does not authenticate a Visa base-versus-Evolution engine.
