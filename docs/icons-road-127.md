# Road-icon batch 127: second-generation Legacy GT-B

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 923 existing identities. One absent BD/BG
sporting-road family brings the working inventory to 924: 529 curated road

## Admission and identity

The GT-B belongs through its role in Japan's sporting-wagon boom and the
combination of two-stage twin-turbo boxer power, AWD and Bilstein inverted
dampers with practical wagon use. It is not admitted simply for horsepower.
The full identity inventory, every Subaru and Legacy/Liberty, BD5/BG5,
GT-B, Lancaster and Outback names were checked. Existing first-generation
BC/BF, third-generation BE/BH S401 and fourth-generation BL/BP are separate
generations. No BD/BG sporting family was present. The unrelated AMG GT Black
Series substring match is not a Subaru identity.

Count this second-generation family once across sedan/wagon, GT/GT-B/RS,
Limited, engines, transmissions, years, regional names and related
Outback/Lancaster derivatives. There is no extra body or trim count.

## Selected reference and sources

The selected reference is **1996 Japan Touring Wagon GT-B automatic**.
All 16 technical attributes map to Subaru's [museum catalogue reference](https://www.subaru.jp/campaign/boxer60th-anniversary/library/2nd-legacy-tw/),
whose relevant full text/table was read. It says catalogue information was
used; an original period brochure was not individually inspected. This car's
260 PS at 6500 rpm and E-4AT are kept separate from the 280 PS manual.
Engine code remains EJ20, without a suffix. A generic DIT footer does not
establish direct injection for this 1996 engine. Mass, starred interior
measurements, detailed centre-control ratios and speed-record figures are omitted.

The [engineer interview](https://www.subaru.jp/campaign/boxer60th-anniversary/interview/)
was read in its introduction and relevant GT-B development section, rather
than claimed as a complete multi-part interview audit. It explicitly separates
280 PS five-speed manual and 260 PS automatic, and explains daily usability
as a development aim. Manual-project injector and EJ20R details are not
assigned to the automatic reference.

Subaru's [2005 generation chronology](https://www.subaru.co.jp/en/news/archives/press/2005/05_03_15e.html)
was read through its relevant body and timeline. It records the second-generation
launch, sporting-wagon market role and June 1996 GT-B/Bilstein introduction.
Its generic 280 PS entry is qualified by the transmission-specific evidence.
Record runs and earlier-generation rally results do not supply GT-B road facts.

The [GQ original retrospective drive](https://www.gqjapan.jp/cars/article/20191016-subaru-legacy-vol2)
was read through its second-generation GT-B section, including the handling
and AWD discussion. Its 1997 first-registered automatic is not the selected
1996 reference. Cultural popularity and wet-road/cornering appeal are retained
alongside the aged test car's noise, steering, body, ride and braking limits.
Conflicting mass figures are omitted; no technical values are imported from it.
Exact source/attribute/fact mappings are in the [manifest](../data/icons-road-127.json).
