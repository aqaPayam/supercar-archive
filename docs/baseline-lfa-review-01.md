# Lexus LFA baseline evidence and media review

Reviewed 30 October 2026. This reviews the existing `LEX-LFA-STD` road record;
it adds no car. The working catalogue remains 863 records, including 468 curated
additions across 80 road batches and the preserved 395 baseline records.
The wider baseline eligibility, semantic-identity and evidence audit remains open.

## Eligibility and identity

The LFA qualifies through its purpose-built high-revving V10 and the programme's
in-house CFRP production and joining technology. These are substantive engine,
materials and manufacturing achievements, rather than rarity or trim alone.
The website now includes a sourced admission fact making that reasoning explicit.

A search of all 863 identities found the standard LFA and the already preserved
Nurburgring Package. The package belongs to the same original production-road
programme, with genuine tuning and aero changes; its separate counting remains
pending the owner's variant boundary. No model year, paint or package has been
added. The early LF-A design studies and competition prototypes are distinct
from the December 2010–December 2012 road production run.

## Technical evidence actually read

The [Toyota global launch release](https://global.toyota/en/detail/308263) and
[Lexus UK media guide](https://media.lexus.co.uk/lexus-lfa/) were actually read,
including their tables and engineering/instrumentation sections. The 44 stored
attributes agree with those sources and the pricing evidence below, with these
qualifications:

- The European launch table labels its specifications manufacturer measurements
  and targets. It supports the stored 1LR-GUE, 4,805 cc, 412 kW / 560 PS, torque,
  gearbox, chassis, dimensions and performance fields. Performance is not an
  independently measured road test, and the 1,480 kg vehicle-weight reference
  does not guarantee every equipped car's kerb mass.
- The UK guide's engine-code table prints `1LR-GSE`, whereas Toyota global names
  `1LR-GUE`. The existing Toyota-linked code stays unchanged. The UK weight range
  also differs in scope from the single global launch-table vehicle weight.
- The 278.4 kW/tonne entry is a calculation, 412 / 1.480 rounded to one decimal,
  not a separate measured or explicitly published performance result.
- Four existing engineering facts agree: the LCD/TFT instrument operation,
  Lexus-developed CFRP cabin, compact V10 with individual throttles, and
  coordinated induction/exhaust acoustics. The cabin photograph is not proof of
  the instrument display's operation.
- The actual two-page [US pricing PDF](https://pressroom.lexus.com/?generate_pdf=54738)
  was downloaded and its text read, alongside the equivalent
  [manufacturer HTML release](https://pressroom.lexus.com/lexus-announces-price-of-all-new-lfa-supercar/).
  It confirms $375,000 announced US MSRP excluding delivery, processing and
  handling fees. This is US launch pricing, separate from European specifications
  and from any present market estimate. No PDF layout inspection is claimed.

The [completion release](https://global.toyota/en/detail/95770) and
[European final-delivery release](https://media.lexus.co.uk/final-lexus-lfa-delivery-in-europe/)
were actually read. Manufacture and later delivery are distinct; the final
500th car was completed on 14 December 2012. The actual
[Nurburgring Package release](https://pressroom.lexus.com/lexus-introduces-2012-lfa-nurburgring-package/)
confirms that its 50 cars are within the total 500. This does not mean 500
standard cars plus another 50. Package tuning, shift time and fixed aero do not
replace the standard record's European specifications.

No technical value, original fact, car identity or count was changed. Source
notes now retain the scope, calculation and source-table discrepancy above.
Auction records, current market claims, colour/distribution estimates, and a
complete package-record audit are outside this review.
