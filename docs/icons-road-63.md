# Modern Swift Sport generations — batch 63

Reviewed 29 October 2026. Three absent production-road generations represent
an established junior hot-hatch lineage. Contemporary driving, not just a Sport
badge, supports admission. Each generation contributes one selected reference.

## Identity and admission

The complete 820-record inventory was searched across manufacturer, family,
generation and variant for Suzuki, Swift, Cultus, Ignis and ZC31S/32S/33S.
The existing 1986 Swift/Cultus GTi is an earlier programme. None of these three
modern generations was present. Suzuki explicitly identifies the 2011 model
as a full model change and the 2017 model as a further new-platform generation.
The older HT-series Japanese Swift/Ignis naming is not a fourth entry here.
Three- versus five-door bodies, gearboxes, markets, facelifts, the later ZC33S
mild hybrid and cosmetic final editions do not create extra counts.

## Evidence boundaries

Suzuki's own digital archive provides separately labelled Japanese sections
and model codes. Native net outputs stay in their original units; no foreign
power, gearbox or curb-mass basis is silently substituted. The ZC31S is a
programme-level heritage reference with gearbox unspecified, rather than a
claim that every later family option existed at its original introduction.
The manual columns are selected for ZC32S and ZC33S. Their automatic/CVT columns
are excluded. Abbreviated VVT descriptions do not establish intake/exhaust scope.
The archive's Japanese K14C displacement is retained with its market identified.

The final November 2011 Japan announcement supplies production chassis details
and confirmed output. The earlier August show release marked its performance
figures provisional. The 2006 European announcement distinguishes the Japanese
five-door and European three-door; its adjacent Splash concept table is unrelated.
Unavailable brochure hosts are not evidence for imported specifications.

Dave Jenkins's actual December 2006 UK driving report supports the first
generation's engine/chassis character while noting motorway refinement and
steering limitations. Sam Philip's January 2012 driving report supports the
second generation's naturally aspirated, agile character while criticising
low-rev performance and plastics. Ben Miles's October 2018 driving report
supports the turbo generation's accessible performance but notes reduced
adjustability and steering feel. These foreign-market assessments justify the
family admission; their equipment, mass, pricing and numerical shorthand are
not the Japanese specifications. Manufacturer superlatives and projected resale
claims are not adopted.
