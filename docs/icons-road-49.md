## Technical evidence boundaries

### Saab 9000 Aero

The original Saab Great Britain brochure was downloaded from its public
facsimile archive. Physical PDF page 12 identifies GB Art. No. 260349 and
final editing in November 1992. Printed technical page 12 (physical PDF page
11), printed design page 10 and adjacent performance text were rendered and
read. They establish the 2.3-litre intercooled turbo, twin overhead cams,
four valves per cylinder, Trionic control, five-speed manual and 350 Nm at
1950 rpm, plus the native 0–60-mph claim.

The host's 1993 filename alone is not proof of an exact model year, so that
field remains null while the publication-date reference is explicit. The
brochure's paired 225-bhp/166-kW wording is inconsistent: neither is silently
recast into a new rating. Power and an unestablished engine layout are omitted.
The different automatic tune and later equipment are not transferred.

### Saab 9-3 Viggen

The actual cover states 1999. The original English EEC brochure's physical
page 28 was rendered and read, not reconstructed from a later buying guide.
It establishes the 2290-cc four-cylinder turbo, twin cams, Trionic electronic
throttle, five-speed manual, front drive and torque limitation in first and
second gears. Its torque is 342 Nm, retained without replacing it with a
remembered figure. The selected coupe uses 6.5-second 0–60 mph, not the
convertible's different time or an invented 0–100-km/h conversion.

The publication covers multiple bodies; its date does not prove simultaneous
launch in every market. No extra convertible/five-door record, later tune,
US power figure, body-spanning kerb-weight range or Pikes Peak racer is added.

### Ford Racing Puma

Stephen Dobie's 17 December 2016 Top Gear article includes an actual road
drive and examination of the original coupe. Its model description supports
the 1.7-litre naturally aspirated engine, quoted 153 bhp, revised engine
management/cams, Tickford wide-body conversion, firmer suspension and
Alcon-developed four-piston brakes. The admission rests on its distinctive
chassis and lasting enthusiast identity, not low production alone.

Optional LSD, replacement dampers, stopping-force claims, exact track widths,
press-car spacers and proposed ST160 power are excluded. A separate 2011 test
uses Ford's V1 FRP heritage press car; its special history and specifications
are not used to establish every production car. The obsolete Motoring Research
URL redirects to the homepage and supplies no evidence. The brochure-cover
photograph and editorial UCP page are not presented as a read technical manual.

### Aston Martin DB2

RM Sotheby's own 2019 inspection/history-file catalogue identifies customer
chassis LML/50/102, its 2.6-litre straight-six and aluminium factory coachwork.
It cites the Heritage Trust certificate and factory service card for Vantage
uprating before delivery. Its original engine was rebuilt, and the car was
later converted to right-hand drive. Those facts prevent a claim of untouched
original condition or universal factory output.

Aston Workshop's engineering account separately describes the original
2.6-litre DB2 architecture: twin overhead cams with chain drive, detachable
cast-iron hemispherical head, wet liners and sump-fed oil pump. Those shared
architecture fields are distinguished from the workshop's own 2.9-litre
DB2/4 Vignale and later capacity changes. Standard H4 carburettors, 105-bhp tune
and numeric compression are not transferred to the uprated Vantage reference.
No prototype, Le Mans Lightweight, DB2/4 or unique open coachwork is substituted.
