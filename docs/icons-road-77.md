## Admission, selected references and sources

### AMC Rebel

The Machine captures AMC’s distinctive contribution to the American muscle-car era: a dedicated 390 V8 road package with revised induction/exhaust, a functional fresh-air hood and upgraded chassis equipment. Its historical sporting identity earns one original Rebel family entry. The contemporary test’s excellent-brake assessment is retained alongside criticism of handling, shift quality and presentation; period significance does not imply flawless execution.

### Dodge Dart

The Dart GTS established a potent compact-muscle formula around Chrysler’s 340 V8, with strong straight-line performance and capable road manners in a smaller package. A contemporary extended test praised its handling and braking while identifying fuel-starvation and seating faults. One original US Dart family is admitted for that sporting identity; big-block options, Swinger names, Hemi competition cars and paint do not add counts.

## Reading and scope boundaries

Both contemporary Hot Rod facsimiles were downloaded, rendered and visually
read. The Machine file contains one spread, printed February 1970 pages
52–53. The Dart file contains two PDF pages: a spread and a continuation,
printed April 1968 pages 36–38. Steve Kelly wrote both reports. The original
Machine scan also resolves the publisher web archive's corrupted numerical
strings; no number is silently guessed from OCR.

The Machine reference keeps the tested optional 3.91 limited-slip axle separate
from standard 3.54 and automatic options. The stock cam is distinct from the
dealer performance cam and suggested carburetion/ignition/shock tuning. The
review describes previously worn rear bushings, poor shift installation,
weight transfer and an awkward tachometer, alongside effective brakes. These
are material context, not a declaration that every example has those faults.
No claim of superior all-round handling is made.

The Dart reference keeps its automatic cam and optional 3.55 Sure-Grip separate
from manual-transmission specifications. Its reported stock drag pass differs
from later faster runs with air-cleaner parts removed. No quarter-mile figure
is imported. Fuel starvation in hard corners, automatic shift behaviour and
seating/column concerns remain part of the assessment. A fitted 383 engine in
an exterior photo does not define the selected 340 test specification. Hemi
competition cars and big-block derivatives are not additional counts.

The first Commons Dart text search returned a busy error; a public category
lookup returned no files. A subsequent narrower public search supplied the
metadata. Neither response established absence of a licensed photograph.
