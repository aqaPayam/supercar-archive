## Identity

The complete 657-car inventory was checked for Fox/SN95/Cobra, SR II/GTS,
AC/Shelby, Sprite/AN5/Frogeye/Bugeye, Spitfire, 156/GTA and Judge aliases.
Existing Mustangs are S197/S550; the 1965 Shelby GT350 represents an earlier
Mustang generation. Existing Vipers are first-generation RT/10 and modern VX
ACR. The existing GTO is the 1964 first generation, not the redesigned second
generation selected here. No additional same-generation engine or body version
is counted alongside these new references.

The earlier AC Ace-Bristol is distinguished from the named Ford-V8 Cobra
production programme. The Spitfire and derived GT6 are separate named production
models, with four-cylinder open versus six-cylinder closed bodies. Their shared
architecture is documented rather than hidden. AC/Shelby names, Sprite/Midget
rebadges, reference years, convertible/coupe choices, later revisions and optional
equipment do not create extra entries. Pending variant/racer decisions are not
assumed resolved.

## Technical evidence

Exact public field mappings are in [data/icons-road-34.json](../data/icons-road-34.json).

The 1993 Cobra uses the original four-page Ford SVT brochure, printed April 1993.
All pages rendered; development PDF2 and specification PDF4 individually read.
The table declares native 235 SAE-net hp at 4600 rpm and 280 lb-ft at 4000 rpm,
five-speed T-5, 3.08 limited-slip axle, 9.0:1 compression, injection and standard
disc brakes. Cobra R five-lug wheels/brakes, modified GTs and later modular
engines are excluded. The marketed 5.0-litre/302-cu-in class is not turned into
an invented exact metric displacement.

The 2003 Cobra uses Ford Division's seven-page ordering guide. All pages
rendered; PDF1/2/7 read separately. Its March 1, 2002 overview states 390 hp and
390 lb-ft; earlier February 1 pages still show TBD rating cells. Those blanks do
not establish compression, rpm or a test standard. Six-speed manual, 3.55
Traction-Lok, water-to-air intercooler, ABS discs, 17 × 9 wheels and the convertible
roof are explicit. Coupe test weights/acceleration, Cobra R and later modified
engines are not mixed into this selected convertible reference.

The Viper uses Chrysler's 1997 USA full-line brochure, printed 4–5 / PDF4. All 26
spreads rendered, with that page enlarged individually. The selected road GTS
retains its 8.0-litre V10, native 450 hp at 5200 rpm / 490 lb-ft at 3700 rpm,
9.6:1 compression,
pushrod two-valve heads, injection and independent aluminium-arm suspension.
The separate contemporary assessment supports its significance, not imported
test figures. Concept, GTS-R, ACR and later engines are excluded.

The small-block Cobra uses the original AC chassis instruction book, hosted as
a road-owner reprint. Of 65 pages, the first 24 were rendered; foreword PDF2,
useful-information PDF3 and brake PDF23 read separately. Chassis equipment
includes Powr-Lok differential, native tyre/wheelbase notation, water cooling
and Girling discs. Haynes Museum separately identifies the 4727-cc Ford V8
family; its conflicting 1964/1965 labels and 280-bhp claim are not imported.
The FIA historical page supplies 1963 chronology. Its original competition form
was examined and found to specify 12:1/four-Weber tune: this is excluded from
the road profile. 427/428, later coil-spring 289 Sports, replicas and current
continuations are excluded. No power, gearbox, steering revision or survivor
originality is invented to fill an unproven field.

Sprite uses its custodian Ural Ataman Museum's restored 1960 AN5 table: native
57.82 cu in / 42.5 bhp at 5200 rpm, OHV four, twin SU H1, floor-shift four-speed, front
coils/rear quarter-elliptic leaves and hydraulic drums. Generic “synchro” does
not mean all ratios are synchronised. Broad totals, absolute firsts, launch
country and unqualified race-victory wording are not imported. Later square-body
Spridgets and engine changes are excluded.

Spitfire uses Standard-Triumph/RAC application 1018, FIA 94 GT. All 11 pages rendered;
cover/engine/gearbox/chassis PDF1/3/5/6 read separately. The cover actually says
29 January 1963; the index's 30 January date is not substituted. The 1147-cc, twin-SU
HS2, pushrod base uses four speeds without OD, 4.11 non-limited-slip axle and
front discs/rear drums. Optional servo, alternate 4.55, later OD/carbs/ratios,
Mk II/III/1500 and racing equipment are excluded. The Sports Six Club supplies
history without its combined Mk I/II performance replacing the base reference.

The GTA uses Alfa Romeo's 29 January 2002 launch dossier, with separate saloon,
Sportwagon, manual and Selespeed rows. Selected Sportwagon manual: 3179 cc,
184 kW at 6200 rpm and 300 Nm at 4800 rpm, dedicated suspension/steering, original
305-mm Brembo fronts and 276-mm rears. The English 250-bhp parenthesis is not
silently relabelled PS. Later 330-mm brakes and differential conversions are
excluded. The name does not imply original 1960s aluminium-lightweight structure.

The Judge uses the original Pontiac 1969 Performance brochure, 11 panels/spreads
rendered. Its identified Steve Kelly/Hot Rod showroom assessment and technical
panel on PDF5 were enlarged and read. Selected 400 Ram Air / 366 hp, close-ratio
Hurst four-speed, native 11.1-inch front discs / 9.5-inch rear drums and
3.90 limited-slip are a specific
equipment reference, not standard equipment on every Judge. Native period
power is not converted to modern net; Ram Air IV/455, alternative transmissions
and modified drag-car performance are excluded.

## Photographs and held research

All eight selected Commons photographs were visually reviewed and retain
creator, licence, source page, unchanged downloaded bytes and SHA-256 digest.
The red Fox Cobra was chosen over a white heavily modified example. A standard
blue/white Viper coupe was chosen over a pace car. The small-block Cobra photo
identifies the actual Haynes Museum road car, supporting provenance beyond an
unidentified replica. The Sprite and Spitfire show their original road bodies;
the GTA correctly shows the selected estate rather than a saloon. Captions
qualify year, restoration, trim, mechanical originality and option authentication.
Judge badges do not replace factory build documentation.

Marcos 1800 and additional Cobra/first-generation Mustang variants remain research
candidates, not additions. Chevette HS remains held for a precise production-road
reference. No dedicated racers or prototypes are newly counted.
