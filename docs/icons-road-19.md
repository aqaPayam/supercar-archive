## Identity and duplicate review

The complete manufacturer/model/generation/variant inventory and earlier manifests
were checked. No Morris/MINI, Volvo, Saab or Opel model was present. The original
Morris Cooper S and modern R53 have entirely different chassis/engine generations.
Austin and Morris badges, further original Mini displacements, R53 JCW/GP, later
power revisions and convertibles do not create additional records in this batch.

The existing Peugeot 205 Turbo 16 is the mid-engine AWD homologation model.
The conventional front-engine GTI is a distinct production performance-car
identity, not another T16 tune. One 1.9 road reference represents the GTI series;
1.6, Griffe, catalyst-market revisions, CTI and restomods are not extra cars.

Volvo 240, 850 and S60 are distinct missing model/platform generations. The 244
road saloon does not inherit Group A specifications. T-5R saloon and later 850 R
are not added again, and V70 R is not counted separately from this S60 R package.
Saab 99 and classic 900 are separately named production model generations;
additional 900 body styles, later GT/SPG market editions and the second 900
platform are not inserted merely to fill a range. The original Opel GT is the
production coupe; Aero GT and electric/diesel record cars remain outside it.

## Evidence and specification boundaries

Technical sources and image attribution are linked in
[data/icons-road-19.json](../data/icons-road-19.json). Period brochures were read
at their relevant specification/equipment pages, rendered or opened as original
scanned leaves, and checked against adjacent models, columns and footnotes.

- Morris: H. & E. 6364 is a period manufacturer brochure hosted by Brochuremuseum.
  Its specification leaf is printed 2/64, despite the host's 1963 model heading.
  The entry explicitly uses that February 1964 British 1071-cc road reference.
  Standard 68-bhp and separate 70-gross notation are preserved without converting
  them into a modern DIN/SAE-net rating. Rubber suspension, four-speed gearbox
  with synchromesh on second/third/top, SU H2 carburettors and servo brakes are
  read from that leaf. Later 970/1275, Hydrolastic and Australian differences
  are excluded. BMW/MINI's heritage account supplies lineage/significance;
  victories by later Cooper S versions are not assigned to the 1071 road tune.
- R53: BMW's original March 2002 UK launch kit establishes the June road launch,
  120-kW/210-Nm supercharged package, six-speed and Sports suspension PLUS.
  Standard 16-inch tyres remain separate from optional 17-inch wheels/DSC.
  No later power update, GP/JCW, convertible or R56 turbo data are imported.
- 205: the dedicated 1987 French 130-ch brochure's page 7 has separately labelled
  CEE 93.5-kW and DIN 130-ch power entries. Both retain their native conventions;
  they are not treated as interchangeable conversions. The technical table,
  five-speed and front-ventilated/four-disc road brakes are specific to this
  reference. Turbo 16 competition imagery elsewhere in the brochure does not
  supply GTI drivetrain data. Peugeot's GTi lineage account supplies context.
- 244: Volvo's Swedish range/history brochure has distinct 1981–1984 rows.
  Printed page 12's 1981 Turbo row supplies B21ET, 114 kW DIN, four-speed plus
  electric overdrive, tyres and steering. Later equipment is not backdated.
  Volvo's 2015 history supplies original 2.1-litre road performance/context,
  separately from the adjacent Group A development. No B21FT or intercooled
  upgrade is silently combined with the early Swedish road reference.
- T-5R: Volvo Car UK's January 5, 1995 press kit, pages 2–3 and 5, explicitly
  limits increased boost to at most 30 seconds. The 240-bhp output retains
  that condition. The period torque sheet and retrospective disagree, so no
  torque value is imported. Manual estate, load-compensating rear suspension,
  dedicated tyres and chassis are separate from the saloon spoiler/times,
  automatic, later 850 R and naturally aspirated BTCC racing drivetrain.
  Launch allocation is not asserted as a final worldwide production total.
- S60 R: July 2003 UK introduction/equipment/technical spreads were checked.
  The manual column supplies 220 kW at 5500 rpm and 400 Nm at 1950–5250 rpm;
  Geartronic has different torque and power-peak speed. Native kW avoids
  treating the brochure's bhp shorthand as a clean conversion. Four-C,
  Haldex, Brembo and standard 17-inch tyres are explicitly documented.
  The UK calendar reference is not relabelled as US model-year specification.
- Saab 99: the public archive ZIP contains the original Saab-Scania 1978
  international brochure. Technical page 15 was visually read: 107 kW DIN,
  235 Nm, 1985 cc, Bosch CI, four-speed and front drive. Rare two-door US
  pre-production test cars, later rally bodies, APC/intercooler and US-market
  power figures are excluded. Saab Car Museum independently dates its
  Frankfurt debut and historical relationship to the subsequent 900.
- Saab 900: the archive's eight-spread manufacturer brochure explicitly says
  1985 model folder and copyright/printing 1984. Original international 129-kW
  Turbo 16S engine/APC pages were visually checked. US SPG rating, sloping-front
  revisions, GT edition, convertible and later platform are not mixed. Gearbox,
  torque and displacement values absent from the selected brochure pages are
  left unfilled rather than borrowed from an unverified specification table.
- GT: Opel's own 50-year account separates production GT 1900 from 1.1, automatic,
  concepts, competition and record vehicles. The original manual road data and
  90-hp English historical notation are retained without claiming SAE net.

Web-tool reads succeeded for Volvo and Opel/Peugeot manufacturer histories;
local HTTP 403 responses were not bypassed. Their photographs are not reused
under an assumption that press availability grants an open image licence.

## Photography and held research

All nine selected photographs were visually checked. Morris is explicitly
identified as a 1963 1071-cc road S; it represents the original body alongside
the February 1964 brochure reference. R53 and 205 are identified 2002 and 1987
road bodies. The 240 Turbo saloon photo does not certify B21ET versus B21FT,
year or later intercooling. T-5R is a Hong Kong estate photo; original wheels,
tune, transmission and selected UK equipment are not authenticated. S60 R
is the early R body, not later R-Design. Saab 99 is the museum's 1978 production
Combi Coupe, not a two-door pre-production car. Saab 900 is a 1985 flat-front
Turbo 3S DOHC 16. Opel GT's event placard and fictitious registration remain
qualified; neither supplies competition specifications.

Captions retain year/market, modifications, drivetrain and originality limits.
Creators, licences, source links, visible photographer credit and unchanged
image bytes are retained. Rally cars, Tolman restomod, ordinary Mini Cooper,
late Saab GT/convertibles, later S60 R-Design and generic turbo dashboards were
rejected as representatives of the selected entries.

Calibra Turbo has useful factory evidence, but no clearly identified licensed
road-body photo was selected. It remains research in the candidate queue;
no generic Calibra or competition image is substituted to round up the count.
HSV GTSR W1, Caterham and the other previously held candidates remain pending.
