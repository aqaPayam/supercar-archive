## Admission and identity

The JS2 belongs through Guy Ligier and Michel Têtu's French mid-engine GT
project, distinctive road engineering and a significant competition lineage.
The 1974 Tour de France Automobile win explains the programme's importance;
it is not a stock road-car result. Rarity alone is not admission evidence.

The complete inventory was checked for Ligier/JS2/JS 2 identities. None was
present. Group the original road engines, body updates, equipment, years and
chassis once. JS1/JS3 prototypes, race JS2s and the modern 2018 JS2 R are not
selected or counted. Shared Citroen SM/Maserati components do not make their
distinct chassis/body programmes aliases of this Ligier.

## Selected early road reference

The reference is the **early French 2.7-litre road coupe, 1972 reference year**.
All 23 attributes map to the [original Ligier brochure facsimile](https://www.guide-autosport.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Ligier-JS2-1971-Catalogue-France.pdf).
The four-page image-only PDF was downloaded and every page was individually
rendered and inspected. Cover branding and the Automobiles Ligier Abrest-Vichy
back-cover imprint establish manufacturer origin. Technical panel is index 2.

The document has no established printed publication date: the host filename
says 1971 and the cover bears a 1972 annotation. The manufacturer history places
the effective production debut in 1972. No exact printing date or launch month
is asserted. This is an early specification reference, not proof all cars built
in a calendar year shared it.

The brochure explicitly describes a two-seat mid-engine grand routiere derived
from the competition JS1. Preserve its own 2670 cc, 170 CV DIN at 5500 rpm,
23.5 mkg at 4000 rpm and Citroen SM synchronised five-speed. Do not replace
these with other catalogues' displacement or later 3.0-litre output. Chassis
is described as a central sandwich backbone with tubular extensions; no metal
skin is inferred. Original metric dimensions convert metres to millimetres.
The stated tyre fitment is Michelin 195/70 VR14 on 6.5 x 14 aluminium wheels.

No exact engine-code suffix, carburettor brand, mass basis, top speed, measured
acceleration, total production, later 195/220-hp road or competition 270/330-hp
and DFV values are imported. The brochure's weight and claimed maximum speed
are not needed to establish the bounded reference used here.

## Motorsport history and original testimony

The full relevant [manufacturer Tour de France account](https://ligierautomotive.com/en/news/the-ligier-js2s-starred-in-the-1973-and-1974-tour-de-france-automobile/)
was read. It explains that road production fell short of the 500-unit threshold
for the intended GT category, so race versions competed as prototypes. Do not
call this a successfully homologated production GT racing special. Significant
racing development preceded the 1974 one-two; early setbacks and weak road
sales are retained. Inconsistent article race numbers, 1973 stage counts,
1975 car numbers and engine-production chronology supply no selected facts.

The introduction and complete relevant Ligier section of [Michel Têtu's original interview](https://www.classiccourses.fr/michel-tetu-ou-lart-de-lingenieur/)
were read. It describes the JS2's more civilised road accommodation and the
competition aluminium-skin chassis distinction. It supports the programme's
engineering significance but supplies no extra technical field. This is not
a review of his whole career or the second interview instalment.

The full [Classic Driver road-car visit and history](https://www.classicdriver.com/en/article/cars/guy-ligiers-legacy-lives-maserati-powered-js2)
was read. Its car is a final-iteration 1975 example, not the early brochure
reference. Practical cabin/luggage packaging and historic purpose support
admission; numerical details, later wheels/body, retrofitted Maserati badge,
dealer equipment and varying production claims are not imported. Race engine
failures are not presented as universal road-car faults. No contemporary road
test, measured drive or investment claim is made.

Ligier's [2024 exhibit account](https://ligierautomotive.com/en/news/ligier-automotive-showcases-the-ligier-js2-at-retromobile-2024/)
was also read in full relevant text. It explicitly separates the 1975 Merak SS
road car, 1973 racing JS2 and modern JS2 R. This is additional identity/scope
context, not a sixth database source or early-road specification authority.
The [history timeline](https://ligierautomotive.com/en/company/history/) was read
in its relevant early Ligier section; other eras supply no selected facts.
Exact source/attribute/fact mappings are in the [manifest](../data/icons-road-132.json).
