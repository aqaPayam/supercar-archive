## Admission and identity

The 1800 TI helped establish BMW's sporting-saloon character through its
production engineering, touring-car programme and lasting historic-racing
relevance. Manufacturer history and an independent historical feature support
this admission; horsepower, age and rarity alone do not.

The full inventory and every BMW, Neue Klasse/New Class, 1500/1600/1800/2000,
TI/TISA/TI-SA/tilux/tii and 02 identity were checked. No original Neue Klasse
four-door sporting-road family was present. The existing 2002 turbo belongs
to the later compact two-door 02 Series. BMW's [02 Series account](https://www.bmwgroup-classic.com/en/models/bmw-classics/product-description-page.ad-1-1.bmw-02-series.html)
and [2016 anniversary release](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/detail/T0258579EN/journeys-through-time-and-anniversaries%3A-bmw-group-classic-at-techno-classica-2016?language=en)
were read in their relevant complete model/anniversary sections. They explicitly
identify the separate later model series while acknowledging shared engine and
chassis technology. These identity-only sources supply no selected specifications.

Group the original four-door Neue Klasse programme, engines, TI/SA/TISA, 2000 TI,
tilux, tii, years and markets once. There is no second sporting-derivative count.

## Exact selected road reference

The selected car is **1964 standard road 1800 TI with base four-speed manual**.
Seventeen of 20 attributes map to the [original FIA recognition form 1286](https://historicdb.fia.com/sites/default/files/car_attachment/1601083801/homologation_form_number_1286_group_t.pdf).
Its 13-page scanned PDF was downloaded. First eight PDF indices 0-7 were
individually rendered and inspected, covering all initial printed sheets 1-8
in scan order 1,2,3,6,7,8,4,5. Later extension pages were not inspected or used.

The initial engine sheet explicitly states 110 PS DIN at 5800 rpm and 1773 cc.
Base Getrag four-speed is separate from the optional five-speed column and
alternative final drives. Larger permitted bore is not selected displacement.
The 53-litre base tank is separate from the 105-litre option. Standard front
discs/rear drums, steel wheels and body/engine construction are bounded to
these initial sheets. No competition-car mass or altered performance is used.

BMW's [30 March 2011 history](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/attachment/T0102981EN_US/151688)
was read as web PDF text in its opening context and relevant 1800 TI, TISA and
competition sections. Printed p.14 identifies the road-trim 1800 TI with 9.5:1
compression and two Solex twin-barrel carburettors, resolving alternatives in
the recognition form. These two attributes map to that source. The complete
22-page document was not visually audited: local HTTP returned HTML and web
screenshot calls exposed no viewable image, so no visual PDF-page claim is made.

TI/SA's Weber carburettors, 10.5:1 compression, 130 PS, limited-slip equipment,
large tank and gearing are not standardised on the selected road TI. Later
1766-cc 1800 engines are not used. Accounts differ on sump capacity and rear
anti-roll-bar standard fitment; both are omitted, alongside torque, weight and
seating ambiguities. Prototype 1500 and later 2000 figures are kept separate.

The [FIA index](https://historicdb.fia.com/car/bmw-1800-ti-1773) was read in full
relevant context and controls rear-wheel drive. It labels recognition 12 April
1964, while the cover handwriting appears 11 April; no exact-day claim is
imported. Historical consultation forms are not current-event certified papers.

## Sporting history and limits

BMW's history records Hahne's 1964 German circuit title with 14 wins from 16
races and a Nurburgring touring-car endurance win. These are competition-programme
achievements, not stock road performance or the record of every example. The
later 2000 TI Nordschleife and Spa results are not assigned to this 1800 TI.

The publicly available [Motor Sport historical feature](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/november-2017/146/bmw-1800ti/)
and visible builder interview were read through the Continue reading boundary.
The issue is November 2017; the page carries a May 2018 web date. It supplies
independent sporting-saloon context and original historic-racing builder testimony,
not a contemporary road test. Modern specialist engines above 200bhp, race
weight and its TI/SA equipment claims supply no road attributes. Unavailable
further material is not claimed as reviewed.

Exact source/attribute/fact mappings are in the [manifest](../data/icons-road-129.json).
The modern BMW museum TI/SA page labels AI-created content; it was discovery,
not the controlling source for the selected 1800 TI. A 2017 manufacturer rally
release was also read but supplies no selected TI technical fields.
