# Ginetta, Alvis, SSK and Duesenberg road icons — batch 51

Reviewed 29 October 2026. Four absent road families are selected in
`data/icons-road-51.json`. Their sporting engineering and documented road
identities justify admission; rarity, prestige and museum display alone do not.
No extra engine grades, years, coachworks or dedicated racers are counted.

## Technical evidence boundaries

### Ginetta G4

Motor Sport's actual April 1964 road report describes a virtually new car
run in before a fortnight of public-road testing. The selected factory-offered
configuration has the 1498-cc Cortina GT engine prepared by Ginetta, a single
downdraught twin-choke Weber, Series 2 removable hardtop and optional front
Girling discs. Its approximate 95-bhp statement remains approximate. The
report identifies the bonded glass-fibre/tubular chassis, front wishbones
and live BMC rear axle. It is a documented road/club-sport car, not a dedicated
race prototype inferred to be road-approved from FIA recognition.

The archive's 2014 web date is not the vehicle year. The 997-cc FIA base,
earlier Ford axle, optional twin-sidedraught engine and later DARE continuation
are not mixed in. Experimental racing tyres do not become standard fitment.
Corrupt first-gear and 0–100-mph table entries, and universal acceleration
claims, are excluded. The later photographed original G4 is not presented as
the tested 1964 car or proof of its axle, engine or brake specification.

### Alvis Speed 25

The National Motor Museum's own 1937 Charlesworth-bodied car establishes
3571 cc, inline six, overhead valves, quoted 110 hp at 3800 rpm, independent
front suspension, all-synchromesh four-speed gearbox and servo-assisted brakes.
This advanced fast-road package supplies the admission case. Native hp stays
as quoted; no conversion or dynamometer standard is asserted. No Speed 20,
4.3-litre or earlier Alvis front-drive equipment is transferred, and the
photograph does not certify untouched condition or a different car's test time.

### Mercedes-Benz SSK

The original English Model 710 Short brochure's physical pages 2 and 4 were
rendered, enlarged and read. It explicitly offers a two-seat sports car to
owner-drivers for touring, alongside competition use. The selected hardware
includes the rounded 7.1-litre six-cylinder, pedal-engaged supercharger,
overhead camshaft with shaft/helical-gear drive, four forward speeds plus
reverse, rear drive and four-wheel drum brakes.

The [manufacturer's historical account](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/tradition/company-history/140-years-vehicle-icons.html)
independently dates SSK to the 1928–1932 programme and distinguishes racing-only
SSKL. The brochure is undated; 1928 is family context, not an invented print
date. Exact displacement, power tune, race results, Trossi special equipment
and replica specifications are excluded. A separately researched custodian's
competition car does not establish the selected road-brochure configuration.

### Duesenberg Model J

Haynes Motor Museum's own 1931 Derham Tourster record establishes 6882 cc,
twin overhead cams, four valves per cylinder, quoted 265 bhp, front beam/live
rear axles and vacuum-assisted four-wheel hydraulic brakes. It identifies the
car's coachwork while describing first ownership as a belief, not certainty.

The original factory Model J technical text on printed pages 19–20 (physical
PDF pages 10–11) was rendered and read in a later Floyd Clymer facsimile reprint.
It explicitly establishes the inline-eight layout and three forward speeds
plus reverse. The host's 1933 filename is not a proven publication date; the
1931 vehicle reference comes from its custodian. Separate supercharged pages
do not supply this car's power. Current equipment originality, measured output,
SJ/SSJ tune, prototype and record-racer specifications are not certified.

The Model J is selected as the sole family representative. The conflicting
speed and implausible gearbox/dimension fields on a separately researched SJ
museum page are not imported. No failed-source substitute or research candidate
is counted as a second car.
