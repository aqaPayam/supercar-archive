# Road-icon batch 130: BMW 700 Sport

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 926 existing identities. One absent original
700/LS production-road sporting family brings the working inventory to 927:
532 curated road additions across 130 manifests plus the preserved 395-record
baseline. This is not a certified qualifying count toward the 1,000-icon goal.

## Admission and identity

The 700 Sport belongs for the production programme's self-supporting-body
engineering and small-capacity competition lineage, supported by an original
road test. The ordinary model's sales or role in BMW's recovery alone would
not establish performance-icon eligibility. Noise, basic trim and demanding
handling near the limit are retained alongside the sporting strengths.

The complete inventory and every BMW/700/Sport/CS/LS coupe or saloon identity
were checked. No 700 production family was present. Count all production
saloon/coupe/cabriolet, Sport/CS/LS, short/long bodies, outputs, years and markets
once. Purpose-built tubular-frame aluminium 700 RS/GT racers are not selected.

## Bounded production reference

The selected reference is the **Coupé 700 Sport, initial 1960/61 recognition
sheets, selected 1961 reference year**. Eighteen of 20 attributes map to the
[original BMW/FIA form 1060](https://historicdb.fia.com/sites/default/files/car_attachment/1601085901/homologation_form_number_1060_group_t.pdf).
The 22-page scanned PDF was downloaded; first eight pages, indices 0-7 and
printed sheets 1-8, were individually rendered and inspected. Later pages
were not inspected or used.

The form explicitly identifies the 1960/61 production model. Its base 697 cc,
78 x 73 mm, 9:1 twin-carb engine and four-speed are kept separate from crossed-out
overboring, optional ratios, 40-litre tank and alloy/13-inch wheels. Selected
fuel tank is 33 litres and wheels are 12-inch steel. Body height, seat count,
weight, oil capacity, numerical gear ratios and cylinder-block material
terminology are omitted where scopes differ or need further interpretation.

BMW's [3 June 2009 German account](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/deutschland/article/detail/T0022634DE/der-bmw-700-der-retter-wird-50?language=de)
was read through its complete relevant release body and final 700/LS history.
It controls the 40 PS at 5700 rpm output attribute and distinguishes the road
Sport from the 70-PS, tubular-frame RS racer. The claimed summer-1961 press
introduction conflicts with earlier period/form evidence. No exact launch
month or first-production claim is imported. First self-supporting BMW body
and Hans Stuck's 1960 programme success explain significance; they do not
turn every production Sport into the championship race car.

The [FIA index](https://historicdb.fia.com/car/bmw-coup%C3%A9-700-sport) was read
in full relevant context and controls rear-wheel drive. It labels recognition
13 April 1961 while the scanned cover stamps 12 April; no exact-day claim is
imported. Later wheel/name/gearing extensions remain separate. Historical
consultation forms are not certified papers for present-day event entry.

The current museum page's AI-labelled description was discovery only and
supplies no imported attribute. General 30/32-PS coupe, later CS/LS, tuned
60-PS or dedicated 70-PS RS figures are not combined with this road reference.

## Original contemporary road evidence

The [preserved original test](https://steyr-puch-freundeskreis.de/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Layout-Thondorf-2019_web.pdf)
is auto motor und sport, Heft 23/1960, original printed pp.20-23. It is reproduced
in Thondorf 34/2020, as identified in the extracted contents despite the 2019
filename. The preservation PDF has 55 spreads; indices 51-52 were individually
rendered and read, including virtues/defects, handling cautions, conclusion
and technical/measured panel. No whole-publication visual audit is claimed.
A following spread was rendered but not inspected or used.

This test supports useful sporting performance and chassis quality, while
criticising engine/exhaust noise, modest equipment and the care demanded
at the handling limit. Its measured performance, gearing, mass, oil capacity
and proposed optional equipment supply no selected technical attributes.
Exact mappings and qualifications are in the [manifest](../data/icons-road-130.json).

Lane Motor Museum's [collection account](https://www.lanemotormuseum.org/collection/cars/item/bmw-700s-coupe-1960/)
was also read in its full relevant description and panel. It supports the
road-versus-racing context but supplies no selected specification, production
count, date or performance figure; it is not an additional database source.
