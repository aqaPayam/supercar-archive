## Admission and identity

The original D-500 is a landmark early Dodge performance-road programme, combining a hemispherical-head V8 with upgraded chassis and brakes. Racer Brown’s contemporary 1,300-mile evaluation documents its unusually complete performance, handling and stopping ability while explicitly distinguishing the road car from the D-500-1 competition package.

The full inventory was checked for D-500, D500, Custom Royal, Royal, Lancer
and Coronet aliases. Lancer hits identify unrelated Mitsubishi models; the
existing Coronet is the late-1960s Super Bee. No 1955–1956-body Dodge target
was present. Count this original road programme once across trim, body styles
and package alternatives. No racing D-500-1 or later D-501 specifications.

## Technical evidence

The [Dodge AMA sheets](https://over-drive-magazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/1956-Dodge-AMA-Rev-2-56-1-25.pdf)
are a 25-page, 10,652,364-byte scan. Full PDF indexes 0, 1, 3, 5, 12, 13,
14 and 18 were visually read. Other rendered pages were not individually
viewed. The D-500 columns and D-500-1 footnotes are kept separate throughout.

Twenty mapped attributes use the revised 15 February 1956 factory reference.
The sheets establish 9.25:1 compression, 260 advertised brake horsepower at
4800 rpm, 330 lb-ft at 3000 rpm, one Carter four-barrel, PowerFlite automatic,
standard D-500 wheels and 12-inch brakes. Native engine units are retained
with the factory sheet’s stated SAE test-accessory convention; no modern net
conversion is inferred. Page 19 expressly identifies the two-door hardtop.
Default sedan dimensions and shipping weights are not copied to that body.

The [May 1956 HOT ROD first-hand test](https://www.hotrod.com/features/dodge-d-500-may-1956-982-390-24-1)
by Racer Brown supplies admission context through extended road, handling and
braking evaluation. It distinguishes street and competition versions. Its
inconsistent compression statements defer to the factory sheet. Tuned shift
points, wheel-dyno readings, acceleration, suggested modifications and
speculative competition outputs are not imported as factory specifications.
The Over-Drive fact-sheet page located original documents; its secondary
summaries, including brake dimensions, are not technical authority.
