Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 915 identities. One original GT40 road family
brings the working inventory to 916 records: 521 curated road additions across

## Admission and identity

The original GT40 road programme translated a defining Anglo-American endurance-racing design into an exceptional road sports car. The Petersen and Shelby American Collection document the purpose-built Mk III street version and its historical importance. This entry represents the original road family once; victories by racing GT40 variants are not attributed to the Mk III.

No GT40 identity was present. Existing Ford GT entries are the 2005 and 2017
road programmes and modern 2023 track Mk IV. Count the original 1960s road
family once across original Mk I road cars, Mk III, years, individual chassis
and steering positions. Dedicated racing Mk II/Mk IV, continuations and replicas
are not this road reference. The selected car is Petersen MK3 1105, listed 1967.
British origin describes the development/build side of an Anglo-American Ford
programme; the separate 1102 custodian history identifies English construction.

## Evidence and technical limits

The [current Petersen collection record](https://petersen.org/pages/petersen-vehicle/1967-ford-gt40-mk-iii)
was read in its relevant full history, selected chassis identification and quick
specifications. It supports 289-cu-in V8, 306 bhp, left-hand drive, round lamps
and extended luggage deck. Its [earlier museum spotlight](https://kumquat-disc-ty3a.squarespace.com/vehicle-spotlights/1967-ford-gt40-mk-iii)
was read in the relevant full body and explicitly identifies ZF five-speed manual
for the same MK3 1105. These are custodian records, not a claim to have inspected
original factory literature. Seven source-mapped attributes are imported.

The [Shelby American Collection’s M3/1102 history](https://shelby-american-collection.squarespace.com/gt40s/1966-ford-gt40-m3)
was also read. Its late-1966 build and factory-literature power/torque peaks belong
to another chassis reference and are not copied to 1105. Its exclusive statement
about Mk III being the only street GT40 is not adopted; Mk I road versions remain
within the duplicate boundary. Racing success explains programme significance,
not a claim that this Mk III won Le Mans.

No torque, peak speeds, measured acceleration, top speed, mass, dimensions,
production count or price is assigned. The nominal 40-inch name explanation
is not treated as an exact measured height. Secondary specifications disagree
on engine capacity, weight and performance and are not controlling evidence.
Search-discovered Ford historical PDFs and factory literature are not claimed
as downloaded or read. Exact mappings remain in the
[manifest](../data/icons-road-119.json).
