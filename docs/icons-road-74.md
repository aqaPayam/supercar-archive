## Admission, references and evidence

### Maserati Indy

The Indy extended Maserati’s sporting V8 GT tradition into a genuine four-seat Vignale coupe, combining twin-cam engine engineering with long-distance usability. A first-hand drive records the original 4.2-litre engine’s strong upper-rev response and fast touring character. It belongs as a distinct historic Maserati performance GT, not simply through its badge or the Indianapolis-inspired name.

Selected reference: 4200 five-speed manual — original 1969 factory heritage reference.

Original 4,136 cc AM116 road specification, with the standard five-speed manual identified by the factory’s 2019 retrospective. The 4.7/4.9 engines, optional automatic, later Citroen hydraulic brakes and years do not add counts. Conflicting later power/speed/production figures, power-peak rpm, weight and unverified performance are omitted. The name honours 8CTF Indianapolis victories; the Indy road coupe did not win those races.

### TVR Tasmin / Wedges

The Tasmin wedge marked a major design and chassis departure for TVR. Installing the Rover V8 created the 350i and established the marque’s defining V8 sports-car direction under Peter Wheeler. Its light sports-car concept and lasting place in TVR history justify one wedge-family entry, grouping its engines, body revisions and renamed variants rather than treating them as extra cars.

Selected reference: 350i V8 open car — original 1983 programme, club technical reference.

TVR brochure download returned 403, was not read and was not bypassed. Quoted
