## Identity and duplicates

The complete inventory was searched across identifiers, manufacturers, families,
generations and variants for 456/456M/GT/GTA, 612/Scaglietti, DB9 and XJ-S/XJS
aliases. None was present. The 456 and 612 are separate models/generations,
not separate transmissions of one car. Existing FF, 550/575, DBS, DB7, Vanquish,
XK/XKR and E-type records do not cover these four families. A shared engine
lineage or related chassis does not turn a distinct model into another badge.
No extra GTA, 456M, Venice estate, Sessanta/One-to-One/HGTS/HGTC, DB9 Volante/GT,
Sports Pack/facelift, HE/six-cylinder/6.0/convertible/XJR-S or race entry is counted.

## Technical evidence

Complete field/source mappings and public admission reasons are saved in
[data/icons-road-27.json](../data/icons-road-27.json).

Ferrari 456 uses the original Ferrari 456 GTA / 456 GT owner's manual, explicitly
US Version Model Year 1997, hosted by IACPFA. The 224-page scanned PDF was
successfully downloaded locally; the web reader rejected its size, which did
not prevent local rendering. The first 24 pages were rendered in a contact sheet.
Title page 2, A10/PDF15, B5/PDF27 and manual-transmission D12/PDF76 were individually
read at useful resolution. They establish F116 C, 65-degree V12, 5474 cc,
436 SAE-net hp / 325 kW at 6250 rpm, 540 Nm at 4500 rpm, 10.6:1 compression,
four seats, six synchronised forward gears and a lamellar self-locking differential
in the gearbox/final-drive unit. GTA automatic performance and transmission
columns are excluded. No European 442-CV/550-Nm figure is mixed with this US car.
The exact 1997 US reference does not create an extra record for each year.

The Ferrari-branded Ferrari70 anniversary PDF, hosted by Motorshow.me, was
extracted and its 456 history on page 6 rendered and visually reviewed. It
independently supports the 1992 introduction, return of a front-V12 2+2 after
three years without one, new engine/transaxle and Daytona-inspired Pininfarina
styling. Its rounded 5474-cc figure and 442-hp/6200-rpm wording are historical
narrative, not substitutes for the selected US manual table. No copied
superlative or claim that Ferrari had no front-engined cars since 1968 is used.

612 uses the original 2004 launch engineering account and final technical table
reproduced by NetCarShow. The relevant model text was read separately from its
related-model summaries. It specifies aluminium spaceframe and body, front-mid
65-degree 5748-cc V12, 11.2:1 compression, 588 Nm at 5250 rpm, rear transaxle,
active damping, CST stability/traction control and four seats. Six-speed manual
and electrohydraulic F1A are explicitly alternatives; the record selects manual.
The final table's native 540 CV at 7250 rpm is retained, rather than the nearby
English narrative's bhp notation or an invented kW conversion. No 2006 equipment,
HGT packages, ceramic brakes, Sessanta or One-to-One hardware is imported. Current
record/first-in-the-world and performance/mass claims are omitted.

DB9 uses the original launch engineering release and final table reproduced by
NetCarShow, dated in the narrative to Frankfurt September 9, 2003 and presented
as the 2004 car. It specifies 5935-cc quad-cam/48-valve V12, native 335 kW / 450 bhp
at 6000 rpm, 570 Nm at 5000 rpm and 10.3:1 compression, plus bonded/riveted
aluminium structure, selected ZF six-speed shift-by-wire automatic rear transaxle,
LSD, double wishbones and steel 355-mm front / 330-mm rear brakes. Manual Graziano
ratios, clutch and acceleration are excluded. Later owner manuals and brochures
use other power-peak/compression notation; those do not silently modify this
launch snapshot. Aston Martin's accessible factory media history independently
confirms first Gaydon-built model and first VH platform. The current consumer
history page mixes launch narrative with later 510-bhp, ceramic-brake and adaptive
specifications; those later fields are excluded. Older brochure routes failed
retrieval and are not claimed as read. This resolves the earlier mixed-source
DB9 research hold with a clearly scoped launch reference.

XJ-S uses an original Jaguar Cars English brochure and technical leaf, publication
3294/B, hosted in a 16-page PDF by Jaguar Club Russia. Its cover/contact sheet,
technical leaf PDF6 and publisher footer PDF8 were visually reviewed. The footer
identifies Jaguar Cars, Browns Lane, and 3294/B. The host filename says 1975, but
the technical leaf includes GM400 automatic data and represents a later pre-HE
revision. No exact brochure edition year is asserted: the record's 1975 is family
introduction, independently confirmed by the Heritage Trust. The selected manual
is explicitly four-speed all-synchromesh. Its 60-degree V12, 5343 cc, one overhead
cam per bank, 9:1 compression, Lucas electronic manifold injection, steel monocoque,
four seats and road disc brakes are specified. Native 285 DIN HP at 5800 rpm and
294 lb-ft at 3500 rpm remain labelled; no conversion or relabelling as the
heritage narrative's bhp is made. The GM400 column does not become 1975
Borg-Warner automatic data or manual data. The Trust's preserved 1975 show-car
account supports the first international-GT-class direction, XJ basis and
Sayer/Lyons design lineage; its show paint/registration and speed claims do not
replace the brochure reference. HE, later engines and competition specifications
are excluded.

Ferrari's current model pages presented a verification challenge and were not
bypassed. A former FerrariDatabase workshop-PDF route returned current HTML,
which was rejected. Dealer listings and search snippets located documents but
did not supply the selected technical fields. Readable factory documents and
clearly scoped reproduced launch material provide the evidence instead.

retained unchanged. 456 uses Thesupermat's CC BY-SA 3.0 1994 GT at a Paris sale,
with original bonnet ducts/bumper. Its European body does not certify the 1997

DB9 uses free photos & art's CC BY 2.0 documented 2004 Netherlands coupe with
original rear lamps/body; it does not authenticate the selected automatic or tune.
XJ-S uses Charles01's CC BY-SA 3.0 January-1978-registered pre-HE road coupe at
Weston Park, with qualified manual/build/mechanical originality. An earlier
candidate labelled 1975–1981 XJ-S visibly showed a different XJ saloon coupe and
was rejected. Creators, licence URLs, original description links, captions and
SHA-256 hashes are stored in the public evidence. Factory press availability is
not treated as an open image licence.
