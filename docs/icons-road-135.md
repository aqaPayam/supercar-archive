# Road-icon batch 135: production-road Vauxhall Prince Henry

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 931 existing identities. One absent original
Prince Henry road family brings the working inventory to 932 records:

## Admission and identity

The Prince Henry belongs through its established role in early production
sports-car engineering and sporting-road history. This is an evidence-based
historical admission, not an addition merely because the car is old or rare.

The complete identity inventory was checked for Prince Henry/Prince-Henry/
Prinz Heinrich, Vauxhall C/C10/C-10/A11C, touring and sporting relationships,
and 30-98/30/98. No Prince Henry road family was present. The C-type programme
is counted once across three-/four-litre engines, years, coachwork and chassis.
Its later E/OE 30-98 descendant is a different named production programme,
not a second spelling or coachwork version of the selected C type. Design
continuity is explicit; it is not a claim that they share no components.
D-type staff/touring cars, trials/record machines, pre-production EI641 and
modern replicas receive no additional record.

## Original production catalogue

The [Vauxhall sales catalogue](https://www.vauxhallregister.com/uploads/7/5/7/1/75715739/1914_sales_brochure.pdf)
is expressly issued 1 January 1914, No. 22. The downloaded 24,993,252-byte
image-only PDF contains 41 scanned pages. Contact-sheet thumbnails were used
for navigation. The following pages were individually rendered and visually
read: contents/date/imprint index 0; dedicated C-type chassis specification
index 6, printed page 7; C-row dimensions table index 19, printed page 20;
and Prince Henry fast-touring car with Denbigh coachwork index 38, printed
page 39. The B-type page index 5 and unrelated Stratford illustration index
40 were also viewed, but supply no selected data. This is not a claim to have
read the full catalogue or every generic component description.

All 19 technical attributes map to the dedicated C-specific specification and
dimensions row. The factory specifies four cylinders, 95-mm bore and 140-mm
stroke. The explicitly labelled **calculated swept volume** is rounded to
3969 cc from pi/4 x bore squared x stroke x four / 1000; it is not presented
as printed displacement. The 25 h.p. heading remains a model designation,
not a brake-power measurement. No numerical power/rpm/torque is imported.

The selected catalogue reference includes its multi-disc clutch, right-side
four-speed/reverse gearbox, worm-and-wheel steering, rear-hub and propeller-
shaft brakes, pressure-fed rear fuel tank, specified wire wheels and Dunlop
tyres. No carburettor brand or later equipment is inferred. Chassis dimensions
convert using exactly 25.4 mm per inch: 10 ft wheelbase = 3048 mm; 4 ft 6 in
track = 1371.6 mm; 13 ft 4 in overall length = 4064 mm; 5 ft 6 in overall
width = 1676.4 mm. These describe the catalogue chassis reference, not every
finished coachbuilt road car. Direct-top overall ratio is 3:1. Approximate
chassis weight, prices and optional equipment are omitted.

## Historical and prototype boundaries

[National Motor Museum's collection account](https://nationalmotormuseum.org.uk/collections/vehicles/vauxhall-c-type-prince-henry/)
was read in complete relevant scope. It places the Prince Henry among the
first true sports cars and explains its trial origins and later 30/98
relationship. Its restored 1915 example supplies historical context; none of
its technical values are transferred to the 1914 catalogue reference.

[Vauxhall's 31 March 2020 account](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/vauxhall/press/vauxhall-c-10-prince-henry)
explicitly calls its familiar earlier three-litre EI641 a likely pre-production
car. It is not the admitted production reference. The account records no
award in the initial 1910 trial. Single-seat/competition speed and world-first
marketing claims are not treated as stock touring performance or settled
worldwide priority.

The [original Motor Sport account in the January 1946 archive](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/january-1946/10/veteran-types-xxviii/)
was read, including its firsthand drive of the long-wheelbase 1914 C97 and
its distinction between C and later E/OE types. Its web transcription carries
a July 2014 date. Production-year/totals claims differ from modern histories;
none are imported. Changed wheels, C97 gearing/wheelbase, output and expressly
estimated acceleration without a speedometer are not catalogue specifications
or measured performance. No figures from that test enter the technical fields.

The earlier 47-page 1911 catalogue was downloaded and its contact sheet and
contents page viewed during discovery. It concerns standard 20/30 hp models;
no blanket transfer of their specifications to Prince Henry was made. The
three-litre British Motor Museum page was also read; its mph/km/h inconsistency
and early reference do not enter this record. The Register's retrospective
C-type article was read for navigation/context, not used as technical authority.
