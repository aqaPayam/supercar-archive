# Charger E49, original Torana XU-1 and XB Falcon GT — batch 68

Reviewed 30 October 2026. Three absent Australian performance-road programmes
are admitted for distinctive engineering and documented sporting history.

## Identity and admission

All 837 existing records were searched for Valiant, Pacer, Charger, Torana,
Falcon, XU1 and XU-1 across their complete identity fields. The Dodge Daytona
and modern Charger Hellcat are separate American programmes. The later LX
A9X does not represent the original LC/LJ compact six-cylinder XU-1. Existing
XY GT-HO and FG FPV F6 references do not cover the XA/XB/XC body generation.

One reference represents each absent original programme. Charger tuning and
styling series, LC/LJ updates, Falcon sedan/hardtop bodies, Cobra, appearance
packages and revised reference years do not inflate the count.

## Evidence boundaries

The Charger is the six-cylinder E49 in Wheels' actual November 1972 comparison,
republished in February 2026. Its 265 cubic inches, 302 bhp at 5400 rpm,
320 lb-ft at 4100 rpm, four-speed Borg-Warner and 3.5 axle remain explicitly
that published test reference. The article's unit standard is not silently
identified as net, gross or a modern rating, and no conversion is imported.
The accompanying E55's 340 V8, automatic, gearing, steering and equipment are
not E49 specifications. Numerical top-speed/rpm shorthand and differing later
retellings are left out. Contemporary praise is balanced by its brake-fade
criticism; no unqualified fastest-in-the-world or Ferrari comparison is repeated.

The original GMH LC XU-1 brochure was obtained from the club's public facsimile,
and both scanned pages were rendered and visually read. It supplies 3048 cc,
160 gross hp at 5200 rpm and 190 gross lb-ft at 3600 rpm, triple manually choked
1.5-inch Strombergs, the standard 3.36 limited-slip rear axle, revised suspension,
front discs and two aerodynamic aids. Only the readable 1970 imprint year is
used. No inferred gearbox specification, optional 3.08 axle or later LJ 202/
3.3-litre engine/race output is substituted. A modified museum-car description
with widened wheels/replacement engine was reviewed but not used as the factory
technical reference. Supercars' account separately documents the programme's
later 1972 Bathurst victory; that racing car is not the 1970 LC road car.

The Falcon reference is the automatic, power-steered XB GT Hardtop in Wheels'
January 1974 road test, republished in 2016. Its Australian-built 351 4V engine
with smaller-port 2V heads differs from the accompanying manual sedan's imported
engine. The public article supports four-wheel discs, revised bushes, rear leaf
springs and steering details, but not a precise power rating or automatic gear
count. No missing technical table is guessed. 4V/2V terminology is not a valve-
count claim. The handling, visibility and body-quality criticisms remain part
of the assessment. Supercars' 1976 XB GT title listing supplies heritage only;
competition specifications, film-car modifications and optional sunroof fitment
are not road specifications.
