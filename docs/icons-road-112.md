Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 908 identities. One original Firebird road

## Admission and identity

The first Trans Am established a defining Pontiac performance identity within the original Firebird family, combining Ram Air V8 power with a purposeful handling package and distinctive functional bodywork. First-hand interviews with Pontiac development participants document its creation and lasting recognition, distinguishing the production road car from experimental racing programmes.

Full identities were checked for Firebird, Trans Am, TransAm and Ram Air,
plus every Pontiac identity. Existing Firebirds are second and third generation;
the separate GTO families do not fill this gap. Count the 1967–1969 Firebird
road family once across years, engines, roofs and packages. No 303 racing
engine, Ram Air IV prototype, GTO rating or later-generation equipment.

## Technical evidence

The [factory print-ad collection](https://over-drive-magazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/1969-Pontiac-Firebird-Print-Ads-1-6.pdf)
has six pages, 14,018,307 bytes. Full index 4 was visually read: the actual
Trans Am launch advertisement. It establishes the 335-hp 400 Ram Air, heavy-duty
three-speed, 3.55 axle and specific handling, steering, brake and body equipment.
Index 5 was also viewed, but its ordinary GTO/Firebird imagery supplies no
selected fields. Other pages were rendered but not individually viewed.

The [factory AMA file](https://over-drive-magazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/1969-PONTIAC-Firebird-1-33.pdf)
has 33 pages, 12,759,384 bytes, issued 11 September 1968. Full indexes 0, 2
and 3 were visually read. The hardtop body is 22337. The power-team table
separates the selected 335-hp 400 H.O. row from the 330-hp 400 and 345-hp
Ram Air IV. Its detailed engine ratings match the later production launch
advertisement. That later ad controls package equipment and axle ratio;
generic AMA base-model chassis choices are not assigned to the Trans Am.

Eighteen mapped attributes retain the period factory Ram Air/400 H.O.
terminology. Ram Air III shorthand does not create another identity. No
modern net conversion, acceleration, prototype weight, unlisted front spoiler,
specific tyre size, racing output or production total.

The [development-participant interviews](https://www.hotrod.com/news/how-the-pontiac-trans-am-was-born-part-3)
provide admission context from Herb Adams and Jim Wangers, including differing
perspectives. The relevant full article was read. Retrospective bulletin lists,
prototype road tests, racing-engine projects, royalties and production totals
are not imported specifications. Contemporary preview, PHS sample and other
leads were search-only or navigation, not read technical authority.

The [manifest](../data/icons-road-112.json) retains exact field/source mappings.
