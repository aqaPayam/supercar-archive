## Evidence and specification limits

BMW's original two-page May 2014 M3/M4 technical sheet, valid March 2014,
was rendered and visually read in the M3/manual column. The M DCT bracketed
figures and M4 body data are excluded. BMW M's own history supports the
first-turbo M3 milestone and M3/M4 naming split. Original 317-kW/550-Nm data
are distinct from later Competition/CS specifications.

The international M4 GTS release supplies road legality, water injection,
368 kW/600 Nm, seven-speed M DCT and dedicated chassis/aero. Its European
carbon bucket seats differ from North American seats. US 493-hp notation,
optional Clubsport cage/harnesses and actual safety-car equipment are not
combined with the selected international production road car.

BMW's June 2011 F10 launch account supplies original 412-kW/680-Nm data,
seven-speed M DCT, rear drive and Active M Differential. The company history
independently documents its turbocharged transition. North American manual,
later packages and optional speed increase are excluded. For F90, the August
2017 launch release explicitly schedules spring-2018 first deliveries. Its
441-kW/750-Nm original specification is separate from First Edition trim and
Competition/CS. Selectable 2WD requires DSC off. The corresponding factory
highlights PDF was rendered and visually checked.

BMW UK's November 2005 M6 kit supplies 4999-cc V10, 507-hp native notation,
520 Nm, SMG, M Variable differential and carbon roof. UK hp is not relabelled
as US SAE output. The road engine shares configuration/development methods
with Formula 1, not the actual race engine. Unrestricted marketing speeds,
optional equipment and later open/manual references are excluded.

Audi's 2006 Annual Report, hosted by KU Leuven, was rendered and checked at
printed pages 41, 63 and 134 / PDF 43, 65 and 136. It documents the original
R8's first-half-2007 market launch and V8 FSI/aluminium/quattro architecture,
including 309 kW and 430 Nm. It does not select a transmission; no manual,
R tronic or later S tronic identity is invented. A blocked brochure host was
not bypassed. Later V8 power updates, V10, Spyder and optional equipment are
outside this original reference.

Audi's June 2012 RS4 Avant press kit was visually checked at page 19/20.
Its B7 historical section explicitly separates the 2005/2006 V8 saloon's
4.8-second acceleration from the document's new B8 and preceding B5 subjects.
B7 manual gearbox, normal 40:60 torque split, hydraulic DRC and 309-kW/430-Nm
engine are recorded together. No Cabriolet/Avant acceleration or optional
post-2007 speed increase is imported. This resolves the earlier held B7
research without mixing a later-generation technical sheet.

Volkswagen's own R history supplies the original European R32/manual data.
The optional DSG's different acceleration is not combined. Mk5 GTI comes
from Volkswagen Classic's original 147-kW/280-Nm reference; manual 7.2-second

checked at pages 3 and 7–11. German scope, sustained 228-kW output, two-seat
