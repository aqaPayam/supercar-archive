# EP71 Starlet Turbo S, Pulsar VZ-R N1 and Storia X4 — batch 67

Reviewed 30 October 2026. Three absent Japanese performance-road generations
are selected for distinctive engineering and documented sporting significance.

## Identity and admission

The complete 834-record inventory was checked for Starlet, Pulsar, Storia,
Sirion, Familia and adjacent performance identities. EP82 GT and EP91 Glanza
are already present, but the original EP71 turbo generation is absent. The
existing N14 GTI-R is separate from the absent N15 VZ-R N1. No Storia/Sirion
programme is present. This batch counts one reference per absent generation;
Turbo R/S/Super Limited names, Lucino rebadging, N1 Version II and alternative
road/competition equipment do not create extra counts.

## Evidence boundaries

Toyota's 8 January 1986 launch release supplies the 1295 cc 2E-TELU, native
105 PS / 15.2 kg-m normal-mode output, 91 PS low mode, driver-selectable boost,
intercooling, knock control and tyres. Motor Magazine's 2019 feature explicitly
revisits its own period test and gives the 1986 three-door Turbo S manual
reference. It supports the original peak speeds, SOHC layout and difficult
handling assessment. No later 110 PS engine, ordinary Si mass/output, TEMS
standard-fit assumption, automatic specification or acceleration conversion is
imported. Cultural context is supported by an independent publisher's history
as well as the factory programme; advertising slogans alone are not admission.

Both pages of the official Autech September 1997 Pulsar brochure were rendered
and visually read. The selected N1 is the standard road-equipped version,
rather than the separately tabulated race-base equipment deletions. Its
SR16VE NEO VVL, head/port work and balancing establish the engineering case.
The original front viscous LSD is retained; no later helical unit is inferred.
Large spoiler and side stripes are optional. The announced 200-car offering
covers Pulsar and Lucino together and is not verified actual production; no
production total or separate rebadge/Version II count is imported. Native PS
and kg-m remain in their original units.

Daihatsu's 10 April 1998 retail launch identifies M112S-GMNZ as a small passenger
car developed as a motorsport base, with road equipment. Only its published
engine, drivetrain, chassis and tyre details are used; no unsupported output,
boost, mass, capacity-rule calculation or ordinary Sirion Rally tune is added.
JAF's actual 29–30 April 2000 A1 result records an M112S Storia winning one
All-Japan Dirt Trial Championship round. This establishes actual competition
heritage beyond the factory's planned campaign. It does not prove an overall
championship or transfer modified competition hardware into the road record.

The Subaru Vivio RX-R remains a research candidate. Its motorsport history is
supported, but a sufficiently readable road-specification source has not yet
been obtained. It is not counted in this batch.
