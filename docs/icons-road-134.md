# Road-icon batch 134: Vauxhall 30-98 E/OE family

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 930 existing identities. One absent sporting-road
family brings the working inventory to 931 records: 536 curated road additions
across 134 manifests, plus the preserved 395-record baseline. This is not a
certified qualifying count toward the 1,000-icon goal.

## Admission and identity

The 30-98 is a landmark early British production sports-car programme, with a
lasting reputation for performance and sporting-road engineering. Museum and
manufacturer accounts explain its historical place and the special configuration
behind its 100-mph reputation. Being old or rare alone is not admission evidence.

The complete inventory was checked for Vauxhall, 30-98, 30/98, 30–98, E/OE,
Velox, Wensum and Prince Henry relationships. No original 30-98 was present.
Existing Carlton, Firenza, VX220 and Chevette records are different programmes.
Count the E/OE road engines, years, coachwork and individual chassis once.
Prince Henry C-Type, OD 23/60 and competition specials are not selected or
additionally admitted. No extra coachwork or engine version is counted.

## Selected museum road reference

[British Motor Museum's 1926 OE-Type Velox Tourer record](https://www.britishmotormuseum.co.uk/online-collections/details-page?row_id=91714258694),
accession L2021-109, was read in full relevant scope. All nine attributes use
that collection reference: four cylinders, 4224 cc, 112 bhp, petrol, overhead
valves, Sport Tourer body and stated 4240/1780/1610-mm length/width/height.
Dimensions describe that displayed road tourer; they are not universal
chassis-only or other-coachwork dimensions. No assumption of exact originality
or individual chassis identity is required for the bounded reference.

The museum's opening historical sentence calls introduction 1923, while its
own text discusses pre-war cars and a 1922 engine change. Do not use that
sentence to establish a family launch date. Selected year is explicitly the
1926 collection-car reference. No total or survivor count, price, brake-change
chronology, acceleration or maximum speed enters the technical fields.

[Vauxhall's 1 April 2020 heritage release](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/vauxhall/press/vauxhall-30-98)
was read in complete relevant scope. It qualifies the historic 100-mph factory
warranty with high axle ratio and pared-down coachwork. This is a historical
claim for that configuration, not the selected standard Velox tourer's maximum
speed. Its account of centre throttle, heavy flywheel and weak brakes prevents
an unqualified claim of effortless performance. No numeric acceleration, fuel
consumption, speed, production/survivor count or price is imported. Its wording
about Velox/Wensum does not establish independent coachbuilder companies.

## First-person road account and source relationship

[Simon Hucknall's original 28 April 2022 drive](https://www.hagerty.co.uk/articles/classic-car-reviews/driving-britains-first-ton-up-car-vauxhalls-car-of-grace-that-sets-the-pace/)
was read in complete relevant scope, including the OE-type fast-facts table.
The driven car is the 1926 OE268 with touring body and upright screen; the
historical fast two-seater has different bodywork and purpose. Hucknall also
appears as Vauxhall's PR/heritage contact on the 2020 release. This original
first-person drive is not presented as independent manufacturer corroboration.

No Hagerty technical table values are imported. In particular, its 115 bhp at
3400 rpm differs from the selected museum's 112 bhp, and its 17-second
acceleration differs from the manufacturer's 29.8 seconds. Keep the museum
output intact without borrowed rpm and omit acceleration. No torque, weight,
measured historical speed, valuation or originality certification is transferred.
