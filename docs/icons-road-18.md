## Identity and duplicate review

The complete relevant manufacturer inventory, aliases and earlier manifests
were checked before import. Lexus previously had LFA records, not IS F or LC.
No Cadillac was present. One original CTS-V and one second-generation wagon
represent missing chassis/engine generations; the second-generation saloon
and coupe are not extra entries. CT5-V Blackwing is a new named generation,
not an ordinary V6 CT5-V or another CTS-V engine tune. CT4-V Blackwing and
other Cadillac V models are not inserted simply to complete a range.

Camaro previously contained the first-generation Z/28. Fifth-generation ZL1
and sixth-generation ZL1 1LE are missing platforms with distinct LSA/LT4 and
magnetic/spool-valve chassis development. No extra fifth-generation body,
sixth-generation standard ZL1, SS or V6 1LE is added. Challenger previously
had the 1970 Hemi, not the modern Hellcat. Later Redeye, Widebody, Demon and
Demon 170 do not multiply this batch's count.

No Holden Monaro was present. Its original 2001 V2 CV8 represents the revived
coupe once; CV6, later power revisions, HSV coupe and Pontiac/Vauxhall/Chevrolet
rebadges are not extra entries. Existing RUF CTR is the modern carbon-monocoque
Anniversary. The original Carrera-shell Yellowbird is a distinct historical
chassis/engineering generation, not a renamed anniversary car or an extra
Porsche 911 year. Customer Porsche-VIN conversions do not create more records.

## Evidence and specification limits

Lexus's original April 2008 UK IS F launch pack explicitly distinguishes
itself from later model changes. The engine table and equipment pages were
rendered and checked. Native 417 bhp / 423 DIN hp and 505 Nm remain distinct
from US 416-hp notation. No later mechanical LSD is imported. Ambiguous
adaptive-suspension wording in the appendix is not used as a technical claim.
For LC, the original MY2018 US brochure's separate V8/hybrid columns and
standard-equipment panel were visually checked. Only V8 coupe data are used;
optional carbon roof, steering, spoiler and differential equipment are not
standard claims. The original Lexus launch account independently documents
GA-L, LF-LC development and production-design awards.

The 2004 CTS-V dealer-order guide, published April 9, was read through its
public PDF text, particularly standard-equipment pages 2–3. It supplies LS6,
400 hp, six-speed Tremec, rear drive, FE4 and Brembo brakes. Its incorrect
metric torque conversion is omitted, along with preliminary 2003 torque
estimates and later LS2 data. This particular guide was text-checked; no
successful local render is claimed. A separately retrieved 2004 full-line
brochure was visually inspected and proved to describe ordinary CTS/V6,
not CTS-V: those specifications are not used. Cadillac's own 2023 anniversary
account identifies the inaugural production V-Series.

The original 2011 CTS brochure's wagon-specific panel, PDF page 7, was
rendered and read. It explicitly gives the 556-hp V8, manual transmission,
Magnetic Ride Control, Brembo brakes, tyres and asymmetrical half-shafts.
Adjacent sedan lap times/acceleration and optional automatic/Recaro equipment
are not generalised. CT5-V Blackwing uses Cadillac's original February 2021
release for MY2022, reproduced by Ultimatecarpage. Only company-release text
is used, not its editorial table. The standard manual, 498 kW, 893 Nm and
MR 4.0 remain separate from the CT4 V6, optional automatic, carbon-ceramic
brakes and aero. An interactive brochure's apparent six-speed-automatic
typo is rejected; a separate expired-certificate download was not bypassed.

The 2012 ZL1 dedicated brochure's pages 4, 6 and 12 were visually checked.
Manual/automatic values remain separate. The 2018 Camaro brochure's dedicated
1LE panels and equipment conditions were also visually checked. DSSV and
manual are specific to the original ZL1 1LE package; ordinary ZL1 magnetic
damping, other 1LE engines, later automatic availability and automatic-only
cylinder deactivation are excluded. No unqualified masses are imported.

Chrysler Group's July 1, 2014 release, distributed through PR Newswire,
documents the original MY2015 Hellcat's first factory-supercharged HEMI,
707-hp/650-lb-ft engine, eight-speed transmission and red/black key control.
R/T and Scat Pack manual specifications are not Hellcat evidence. Historical
marketing superlatives are not asserted as current records. Canadian assembly
is distinct from manufacturer origin.

The original 28-page Holden brochure is publicly preserved by Holden Retirees
Club. Scanned pages 26–27 were visually read in the CV8/manual column; the
footer identifies November 2001 and AD10965. Original 225 kW, 5665 cc and
460 Nm remain separate from CV6, optional automatic and later 235/245/260-kW
revisions. The National Motor Museum's own annual exhibition record links
Monaro Mania to the 2001 CV6/CV8 launch and original model lineage. Brochure
copyright does not licence its pictures; the selected photograph is separate.

RUF's manufacturer history explicitly separates original 1987 469-PS CTR
and measured Ehra-Lessien result from modern Anniversary data. Mechanical
description is supported by a first-hand November 2013 Car and Driver drive
of the original Yellowbird, openly reproduced by RUF UK. Its source is
labelled as original vehicle reporting, not a new-car factory spec sheet.
The five-speed, 3.4-litre flat-six and body/aero description are separate from
modern seven-speed/carbon-chassis fields. English horsepower shorthand,
speculative higher output, unqualified mass and torque conversion are omitted.
An exhibition document with conflicting power notation and an unsupported
outright Nordschleife-record claim is not technical evidence for this import.

## Photography and held research

All ten selected photos were visually reviewed. Captions identify early IS F
show display, UK LC Sport+, 2004–2005 CTS-V, 2011 wagon, 2022 CT5-V Blackwing,
2012 ZL1 show car, 2018 ZL1 1LE, 2015 narrow-body Hellcat, original V2 Monaro
and classic Carrera-shell CTR. Later/body-ambiguous CT5 images, ordinary CTS
with an aftermarket grille, Black Panther LC show cars and modern CTR
Anniversary images were rejected. The CTR's event number does not make this
a race-car specification. Original build, drivetrain, market and optional
packages are not authenticated merely by exterior photos. Creator, licence,
source links and unchanged image bytes are preserved.

HSV GTSR W1 has strong factory evidence for its LS9 drivetrain and dedicated
suspension, but no clearly identified licensed representative photo was
selected. It remains research only; no ordinary GTSR or generic HSV replaces
it to increase the count. Caterham 620R, Cobra and other held candidates also
remain outside the count. Additional variants within represented generations
and dedicated racers/prototypes still await selection boundaries.
