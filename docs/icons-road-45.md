## Identity boundaries

No extra model year, cosmetic edition, body variant or market-badge alias is
counted. S8/D2, Shamal, Ghibli II, Ascona B, Chevette and Chrysler families were
absent. Existing Firebird is the second-generation SD-455; existing Camaros
cover first, fifth and sixth generations. The third-generation Camaro and
Firebird share GM's F-body platform but are separate named model programmes
with different selected engines, not duplicate badge spellings. Ascona B and
the existing Manta B share their technical base but represent separate saloon
and coupe homologation programmes. Shared platforms do not imply unrelated
engineering or justify adding every derivative. Modern Charger Hellcat is a
four-door road family, distinct from the existing 1969 Daytona; 2015 identifies
the selected facelift/powertrain reference, not a wholly new platform.

## Primary-source limits

- **S8:** Preserve the US reference and native bhp. The online driving report
  is dated 2012; no invented 2001 publication date, film claim or D3 specification.
- **Ascona:** The German factory history establishes road 106 kW, five speeds,
  rear discs and rear drive. The Italian account establishes the road engine's
  four cylinders, 2410 cc and sixteen valves. Race output, Conrero tuning,
  six-speed race transmission and an unverified production total are omitted.
- **Shamal:** Factory production begins in 1990. No unsupported 1989 launch
  date is substituted. Its new V8 and body, except doors, distinguish it from
  the old Biturbo. No inferred torque, acceleration or separate engine grade.
- **Ghibli:** Select the 2.0-litre road Cup introduced in December 1995. Do not
  import Open Cup racer data, 2.8-litre export specification, unprinted gearbox
  details or turn a broad torque band into one peak-rpm claim.
- **C-300:** All three physical pages of original brochure CS339 2-55 were
  rendered and read, including its specification back page. Preserve native
  cubic inches and horsepower; no guessed PowerFlite speed count, later
  letter-series engine or modern performance estimate.
- **300C SRT8:** The full Serious Wheels text explicitly identifies itself as
  DaimlerChrysler's press release. Use that company text, not secondary compiled
  tables. Select US 2005 6.1-litre/425-hp reference; preliminary acceleration
  estimates and later 6.4-litre/2023 data are omitted.
- **Charger:** Both factory brochure pages were rendered/read. Use the right
  Hellcat column, not adjacent 6.4-litre SRT 392 equipment or axle. A speedometer
  scale is not a maximum-speed claim. No later Widebody/Redeye or Challenger
  manual specification is transferred.
- **Turbo Trans Am:** Read the June 1989 first-hand test and table; retain its
  road specification without speculative higher output or race-pacer data.
- **IROC-Z:** Actual original GM physical pages 26 and 37 establish LB9 TPI,
  automatic with overdrive, native SAE-net output and IROC equipment; page 108
  shows body/wheel equipment. Inconsistent transmission labels elsewhere are
  not used for gearbox identity or speed count. No carburetted L69, base LG4,
  later 5.7-litre engine or unsupported LB9 manual is imported.
- **Chevette:** The museum's own 1979 HS 2300 record is collection L2021-130,
  privately loaned from the Vauxhall Heritage Collection. Use nominal 2.3 litres;
  omit its anomalous 2379-cc table entry, unprinted valve layout and gearbox
  speed count. Rally-only Lotus heads and ZF gearboxes are not road equipment.

## Photographs

All ten images were visually inspected and copied unchanged. The manifest maps
each photo to its own Commons description page, named creator, precise licence
or explicit creator's public-domain dedication, qualified caption and SHA-256.
Creative Commons licence URLs and original credit text are retained. The
IROC-Z photographer's PD-self / All Rights Released dedication is recorded
without inventing a Creative Commons licence URL.

The Ascona image is expressly labelled a competition-prepared rally example,
not evidence of standard road hardware. The later Ghibli Cup and Australian
2006 SRT8 illustrate their road families without certifying the selected
introduction/US specifications. Other captions likewise distinguish visible
body design from hidden engine, market, year and restoration condition. No
brochure scan or unlicensed manufacturer image becomes a display photograph.
