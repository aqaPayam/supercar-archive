# Road-icon batch 110: original Oldsmobile Rocket 88

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 906 existing identities. One original Rocket 88
performance-road family brings the working inventory to 907 records: 512

## Admission and identity

The Rocket 88 is a foundational American performance icon, placing Oldsmobile’s modern high-compression overhead-valve V8 in its lighter 88 body. The Henry Ford documents the programme’s early stock-car racing impact and its cultural connection to the song Rocket 88, establishing significance beyond ordinary period styling or rarity.

Full identities were checked for Rocket, Eighty-Eight, Eighty Eight, Super 88,
Super88 and Oldsmobile, with a separate review of all manufacturer names
beginning Old and plain 88 families. Only the second-generation 4-4-2 was
present. Count the original 1949–1953 Rocket 88 family once across years,
roofs, trim and Super 88 alternatives. Racing history supports admission;
race-car output and tuning are not road specifications.

## Technical evidence

The [original 1949 AMA sheets](https://autohistorypreservationsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/1949-OLDSMOBILE-AMA-Specs-88-Pg-1-10.pdf)
are ten scanned pages, 2,272,559 bytes, dated 14 September 1948. Full PDF
indexes 0, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 were visually read. Other pages were rendered
but not individually viewed. The model header identifies 88; sheet 9
specifies the five-passenger four-door body used for this reference.

Eighteen mapped attributes distinguish 135 bare-engine bhp from 129 bhp
with standard accessories, and 263 bare-engine lb-ft from 255 lb-ft with
accessories. Native historical test labels and units remain intact. Neither
accessory rating is relabelled modern net power. Documented displacement is
303.73 cu in; bore and stroke retain the factory fractions. Hydra-Matic is
standard on all 88 models in this reference. Coil springs are specified both
front and rear. No inferred road times, race output, shipping-to-curb mass,
later carburettor, later manual option or imported wheel/tyre conversions.

[The Henry Ford’s original engine artifact](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/216837)
provides independent engineering and early racing context. Confusing bore/stroke
labels in its artifact-dimension metadata are not reused. Its
[1950 advertisement collection record](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/367321)
provides the cultural connection to Rocket 88. The artifact’s July 1950 date
does not supply 1949 technical specifications. No lyrics or unqualified
first-muscle-car claim. Both public facts retain their evidence mappings.
