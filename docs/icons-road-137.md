# Road-icon batch 137: early Alfa Romeo 6C sporting-road family

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 933 existing identities. One absent early
6C sporting-road programme brings the working inventory to 934 records:
539 curated road identifiers across 137 manifests plus the preserved 395-record
baseline. This is not a certified qualifying count toward the 1,000-icon goal.

## Admission and identity

Vittorio Jano's early six-cylinder sporting cars belong through their nimble
chassis, twin-cam/supercharged engineering, major Mille Miglia heritage and
lasting place in pre-war sports-car history. Rarity and coachbuilder exclusivity
alone are not reasons for admission.

The complete inventory was checked for Alfa Romeo 6C, 1500/1750, SS/Super Sport,
GS/Gran Sport, James Young and related early Jano identities. No early 6C road
programme was present. Count the closely related early 1500/1750 sporting-road
family once across engine tunes, years, subtype names, coachwork and individual
chassis. No additional GS, GT, racing, fixed-head, replica or later 6C programme
is admitted in this batch.

The selected reference is the **1929 6C 1750 Super Sport with James Young
three-place body, Simeone Museum chassis 312910**. The museum explicitly
describes customer purchase and road-car suitability alongside the owner's
subsequent competition use. This is a production sporting-road family with
racing history; that history does not establish stock-racing engine tune.

## Selected museum and family engineering evidence

[The Simeone collection account](https://simeonemuseum.org/collection/1929-alfa-romeo-6c-1750-ss/)
was read in complete relevant scope, including technical header, programme
history and individual-car account. Seven attributes map to that reference:
six cylinders, quoted 1.75 L, quoted 85 hp, twin overhead camshafts,
supercharger, James Young coachbuilder and two main seats with small rear
dickey. Preserve its quoted units; no exact 1752-cc displacement, output rpm
or SAE/DIN standard is invented. The museum's originality description remains
an attributed account, including replaced seat bottoms and mechanical attention,
not an independent authenticity certificate. Production totals, speed and
unique competition superlatives are omitted.

[Alfa Romeo's original Italian programme account of 28 April 2020](https://www.media.stellantis.com/it-it/heritage-hub-italy/press/storie-alfa-romeo-seconda-puntata-l-iconica-6c-1750-anticipa-il-futuro-ed-protagonista-della-sua-epoca)
was read and compared with its English counterpart. Five explicitly labelled
family-level attributes use its shared road/racing engineering section:
pressed-steel frame, reinforced axles, mechanical drums, outboard leaf springs
and rearward fuel tank. This is manufacturer historical description, not a
new physical examination of every surviving chassis.

The same account separately identifies the limited fixed-head Gran Sport
special. Its 102-CV/840-kg/170-km/h figures are not transferred here. Nor are
later 6C independent suspension or the unrelated 1914 GP engine's four valves
per cylinder and dual ignition. No torque, gearbox, wheelbase, mass, measured
acceleration or maximum speed is imported for this museum reference. Racing
averages, lights-off anecdotes, fastest-car marketing, prices, production totals
and Flying Star body details are not stock-road specifications.

The manufacturer museum's fixed-head Spider Corsa listing was located during
discovery and supplies no selected technical field. Other individually restored,
rebodied or gas-generator-converted chassis found in research likewise do not
define this reference. Candidate discovery is not additional admission.
