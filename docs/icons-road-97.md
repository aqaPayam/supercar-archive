governed 250 km/h and factory 5.1-second 0–100. Only selected fields are
imported. The parenthetical horsepower label is not reused or converted.

90 percent of a power reserve at 2300 rpm is not adopted as a technical field.
Cover index 0 was not rendered or inspected. The official Tokyo show archive
was actually read as contemporary context; it does not prove an identical
Gallardo engine or an R8 race-engine transplant.

The actual 6 July 2017 Swiss Audi media release was read in full. It explicitly
identifies the second RS 5 generation, 2.9-litre V6, inner-V twin turbos,

40:60 quattro/self-locking centre and five-link front/rear suspension. The
standard 250-km/h reference is kept separate from the optional 280-km/h package;
rear sport differential, DRC, ceramics and carbon roof are optional. The
claimed 3.9 seconds is not presented as a measured road test. Exact cubic
centimetres, power-peak rpm, weight and dimensions are not inferred or added
from unrelated-market/2020 facelift tables. Japan's June 2017 launch release
was opened for context but is not technical authority for this Swiss record.
The Swiss PDF and later 2020 PDF were search results only, not downloaded or
