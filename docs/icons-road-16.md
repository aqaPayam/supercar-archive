## Identity and duplicate review

The complete inventory and previous manifests were checked before preview.
Existing TVR Cerbera and Sagaris do not cover Griffith, Chimaera or the
original road Tuscan. Griffith and Chimaera share engineering ancestry but
are separately named factory models with different bodies and touring roles.
The racing Tuscan Challenge is not the road-going Speed Six Tuscan. Only
one Tuscan reference is added; no additional standard, Red Rose or Mk2 car.

No Morgan, MG, Ariel or Caterham model was previously present. One early
Plus 8 and one original Aero 8 represent distinct chassis architectures;
later Plus 8 engines and Aero derivatives do not multiply the count. MG TC
and MGB are different sports-car generations, not year variants of one car.
TR2 and original TR4 are absent; the existing TR6 remains untouched. No
TR3, TR4A, TR5 or TR250 is inserted to complete a sequence. Atom 4 is the
first Atom entry; extra power packages and earlier versions are not added.
The existing Lotus Seven Series 1 remains separate from held Caterham work.

## Evidence and specification limits

TVR Engineering's October 19, 1999 London Motor Show press pack supplies
Griffith 500 and Chimaera data on pages 10–11. The selected Griffith is
320 bhp / 320 lb-ft, not the undated brochure/current history's 340 figure.
The selected four-litre Chimaera is 235 bhp; earlier 240 claims and optional
4.5/5.0 engines are excluded. An original Chimaera brochure independently
supports V8 configuration, roof and interior. The original 2003 UK range
sheet was rendered and visually checked at page 4: Tuscan S is 3996 cc,
390 bhp, 310 lb-ft, dry sump, larger brakes and close-ratio transmission.
The adjacent 3605-cc standard car, pre-production 1999 output, optional
Hydratrak differential and later Mk2 specifications are not combined.

The early Morgan Plus 8 handbook was rendered and visually inspected,
including PDF pages 6–9. Its 3528-cc engine's 184 bhp and 226 lb-ft are
explicitly **gross**, not modern net values. The document covers both Moss
and Rover four-speed alternatives; the later all-synchromesh Rover box is
not assigned to the 1968 introduction. Morgan's own history supports that
introduction and original four-speed lineage. Mixed later BMW specifications
on the same retrospective page are excluded.

The original Aero 8 company press release reproduced by Ultimatecarpage
refers to 2001 production and the BMW 4.4-litre V8 with VANOS and 286 bhp.
The editorial specifications table is not primary evidence. Morgan's own
Aero history supplies the original aluminium chassis/hand-formed body and
first BMW powertrain milestone. Late 4.8-litre numerical fields on that
page are not used. Undefined gross vehicle weight is omitted.

The 1948 MG/Riley Salesman's Reference was rendered and visually read in
the Midget Series TC column, PDF pages 3–4. It supplies standard 1250-cc,
54.4-bhp engine, twin SU carburettors, gearbox, brakes and chassis details.
Adjacent Y-series specifications are not TC values. Lane Motor Museum
supports TC family history and US influence only: its own car has cycle
fenders and a Shorrock supercharger, which are not standard specifications
or the selected photograph. For MGB, the original US brochure's technical
page 8 and importer page 9 were visually inspected. Early three-bearing
1798-cc/95-bhp data remain separate from later engines, gearboxes, rubber
bumpers, GT bodies and optional overdrive. British Motor Museum independently
documents the iconic roadster's 1962–1980 production lineage.

National Motor Museum's own TR2 collection record supplies the selected
1954 car's technical data and production-model history. Its historical hp,
mph and 0–60-mph notation are retained without invented conversions or a
modern power-rating claim. No Jabbeke prototype/race figures are imported.
The TR4 workshop manual 510322 general tables were rendered and visually
checked, PDF pages 5–7: standard 2138 cc, 100 net bhp, 1520 lb-in, four
synchromesh forward gears, front discs, rear drums and leaf-sprung rear axle.
The special-order 1991-cc column and optional overdrive are excluded. The
1964 range brochure visibly names T.R.4; its host's TR4A name does not turn
this into an IRS TR4A. The Spitfire data below it are also unrelated.

Ariel's original launch press pack, hosted by distributor Driving Event
Sweden, explicitly schedules late-2018 production and spring-2019 deliveries.
The host filename's 2021 is not treated as a model year. Original standard
320-bhp/420-Nm turbo data, bronze-welded chassis and pushrod-operated dampers
remain separate from later 350 upgrades, 4R, 4RR and Atom V8. Its standalone
specification sheet was also visually checked; unqualified mass and the
sheet's inaccurate parenthetical acceleration conversion are not imported.

## Photography and held research

All ten selected photographs were visually reviewed. Captions identify
representative years and bodies, including the 2004 Mk I Tuscan S, 1970
Rover-era Plus 8, early Aero headlamp form, conventional-fender TC and
Australian-photographed 1963 MGB. The TR2 image is not claimed to depict
the museum car. Exact installed engines, original markets, restoration and
optional equipment are not authenticated from photographs. Racing Tuscans,
late Aero bodies, unrelated image-search results and TR4 Dove coupes were
rejected. Image source links, creators, licences and unchanged bytes remain
in the manifest, database and exported gallery.

The original 2013 Caterham 620R has a usable company launch account but
no suitably identified licensed photograph was selected. It remains research,
not a catalogue addition; generic Seven images do not establish 620R identity.
Current 620S/620R equipment and a suspect Japanese torque unit are not mixed
into the original launch. Cobra and other previously held candidates remain
outside the count. Additional same-generation variants and dedicated racers/
prototypes still await selection boundaries.
