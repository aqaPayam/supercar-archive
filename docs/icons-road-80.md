## Mercury Cyclone

The Cyclone Spoiler II was Mercury’s road-going aero-homologation counterpart to the Torino Talladega, with dedicated front bodywork and parts rather than only driver-themed paint. Its role in the NASCAR aero era and distinctive production-road engineering justify one original Cyclone fastback family entry. Shared Ford components are acknowledged; the Mercury body/model programme is distinct. Gurney and Yarborough colours, normal-nose Spoilers and engine choices do not add separate counts.

Reference: Spoiler II 351 — original safety memo and owner-supplement reference.

US 1969 production-road Spoiler II reference. The surviving Safety & Services Department memo and owner-supplement spread establish the 351 engine and special body parts; the December 30, 1968 release describes the broader Cyclone Spoiler launch and is not proof that all of its gearbox choices reached Spoiler II production. ## AMC Javelin

The original Javelin brought American Motors into the pony-car contest with a distinctive fastback body with rear seating and a serious V8 performance package. The contemporary SST 343 test documents its strong engine, four-speed, limited-slip axle and optional front discs, alongside traction and heavy-steering shortcomings. That landmark AMC sports-coupe programme merits one first-generation road-family entry, separate from the shortened two-seat AMX and later 1971 Javelin AMX. Extra engines, facelifts and appearance packages do not add counts.

Reference: SST 343 four-speed, 3.54 limited-slip axle — December 1967 road-test reference.

US model-year 1968 Javelin SST, as tested in Car Life December 1967, printed pages 34–38. This is the 343 V8 four-speed road car, not a 390, shortened two-seat AMX, later Javelin AMX or Trans-Am racer. ## Evidence actually inspected

The original Lincoln-Mercury release 68-7429 dated December 30, 1968 was
visually read from its single-page scan. It describes the broader Cyclone
Spoiler with an available aerodynamic front end. Its manual/automatic choices
are not evidence of the final Spoiler II production transmission. The surviving
Safety & Services Department memorandum excerpt and the displayed two-page
owner-supplement parts spread were also visually read. These establish the
351 engine, exclusion of 428 CJ, different radiator, shared Talladega torque-box
parts, and dedicated front-body service components. The memo is not a complete
safety file and the spread is not a complete owner manual. No measured aero
benefit, lowered road ride height, specific transmission, axle ratio, power,
production total or NASCAR race-engine specification is imported. The marque
owners' original-document study supplies the historical homologation context;
its production totals and race-engine claims are not technical data here.
A registry option-package page returned a membership prompt and was not used
as evidence for unseen content.

All four pages of the December 1967 Car Life Javelin SST test, printed pages
34–38, were rendered and visually read. The tested model-year 1968 car has the
343 V8, four-speed, 3.54 limited-slip axle, quick-ratio manual steering and
optional assisted front-disc/rear-drum brakes. Its Go equipment was only partly
installed. The review records strong engine/gearbox/braking qualities but poor
full-power tyre adhesion and heavy low-speed steering. No race specification,
390 option, full Go package, measured performance, weight, production figure
or modern net/gross label is inferred. The data panel lists five passengers,
so a four-seat claim in Commons metadata is not copied into the record.
