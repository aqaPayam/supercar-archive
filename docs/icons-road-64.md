## Evidence boundaries

Peugeot's German May 2015 announcement provides native 153 kW / 208 PS, the
specific transmission/differential, chassis changes and 323-by-28 mm front discs.
The December 2014 Top Gear first drive found improved corner-exit traction,
brake feel and steering, but took place only on a very wet circuit. It does not
establish road ride quality. Its engine/exhaust and obscured-instrument criticism
is retained in source notes. UK bhp, prices and planned allocations are excluded.

Volkswagen's dedicated 1.8 GTX 16V table supplies 1781 cc, 102 kW, 6100 rpm,
165 Nm and 4600 rpm. Its history identifies the 1985 four-valve programme and
reinforced chassis/driveline with rear discs. This is a programme reference,
not a dated 1985 build sheet. L'Automobile Sportive's on-road assessment supports
its lively, entertaining character while acknowledging dated handling and heavy
steering. Its different torque number and internally conflicting rear-drum table
are not used. The 1988 photo is representative, not evidence of original 139 PS
output. No universal first-16-valve claim or favourable-wind speed is repeated.
A period Motor Trend scan host failed certificate verification; its search
excerpt is not imported and no security check was bypassed.

The Fiesta uses two January 2005 Ford-attributed releases preserved by an
independent archive. The hosting relationship is explicit. AUTO BILD's actual
January 2005 driving report corroborates performance hardware and enjoyable,
precise handling with retained comfort. Native 110 kW / 150 PS is retained;
optional 17-inch wheels are separate from standard 16-inch wheels. Ford's own
Presseportal release confirms Cologne ST production; its million-unit family
milestone is not an ST production total. Later turbo engines, foreign equipment,
universal stability-control fitment and cosmetic stripes are not transferred.
