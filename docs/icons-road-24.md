## Identity and duplicates

The full inventory and earlier manifests were checked for S15, 180SX, 200SX,
240SX, SR311/SRL311, Sports 2000, Fairlady Roadster, Z34 and 370Z aliases. None
was present. Existing Silvia entries cover CSP311, S110, S12, S13 and S14. The
S15 is the missing seventh generation, not another tune of those cars. Existing
Fairlady Z entries cover S30, Z32 and Z33; the Z34 is a distinct body/chassis
generation. The SR311 is the open Fairlady roadster, distinct from the Z coupe
and the separately named, hand-built CSP311 Silvia coupe.

The 180SX shares its platform with the S13 Silvia, but is the separately named,
long-lived hatchback/fastback road model with its own body and documented
cultural identity. It is counted once. Its export 200SX/240SX fastback badges,
later engine/Type X/Type S changes and body conversions add no records. No extra
S15 Aero/HICAS/packages/Varietta, Fairlady engine/year or 370Z Touring/NISMO/
anniversary/roadster records are added. Cosmetic and year changes do not pad the
count.

## Technical evidence

Full field/source mappings and sourced public admission reasons are saved in
[data/icons-road-24.json](../data/icons-road-24.json). All sources in this batch
were read as HTML; no PDF render is claimed.

S15 uses Nissan's original January 20, 1999 Japanese release, announcing sales
from January 19. The turbo spec-R six-speed output is 250 PS, distinct from
automatic and naturally aspirated spec-S data. It documents the close-ratio
six-speed, triple-cone synchronisers on first through third, helical LSD,
reinforced 16-inch-wheel-spec body, revised suspension and four-piston front
brakes. The helical LSD is specifically associated with manual/16-inch cars;
optional HICAS and Aero equipment are not universal base equipment. Nominal
2.0-litre class is not invented exact cc. Archived catalogue index links were
inspected, but their destinations returned current Nissan-homepage content;
that content was rejected as historical specification evidence.

180SX uses Nissan Heritage Collection No. 323, the 1990 RS13 Type II. Its
CA18DET 1809-cc DOHC turbo, 225 Nm, 6400-rpm power peak, strut/multi-link chassis
and disc brakes are separate from later SR20DET or export KA24 data. The English
page pairs 123 kW with 175 PS, an inconsistent conversion: the manifest retains
native 175 PS and excludes 123 kW without recalculating a replacement. The
[Japanese version of the same factory collection record](https://www.nissan.co.jp/HERITAGE/DETAIL/323.html)
was subsequently cross-checked: it explicitly gives 129 kW / 175 PS and confirms
HICAS-II and ABS as optional. The entry does not claim either as standard.
Factory history establishes global fastback aliases, unchanged basic design
through 1998 and its influence on contemporary young drivers. No modern drift
build or unsupported competition result becomes a stock-car fact.

SR311 uses Nissan Heritage Collection No. 348, explicitly an early 1967
Australian Datsun 2000 Sports. U20, 1982 cc, OHC, native 107 kW / 145 PS, 176 Nm,
6000-rpm power peak and road suspension/brakes apply to that reference. Other
market 135-hp notation, Solex competition upgrades, a gearbox specification not
supplied on this page and restored-car output are not borrowed. Historical
fastest-car superlatives and performance figures are omitted. The recorded
laminated glass and rear torque rod come from the selected collection account.

370Z uses Nissan North America's November 19, 2008 launch kit for the 2009
coupe. Its original 332-US-hp / 270-lb-ft VQ37VHR, VVEL, six-speed, rear drive
and aluminium panels are distinct from the same page's appended NISMO and 350Z
Roadster sections. The selected manual has the optional Sport Package, so
SynchroRev Match and viscous LSD are expressly package-specific. They are not
claimed for every base 370Z. No seven-speed automatic, NISMO 350-hp/chassis,
facelift, anniversary trim or unqualified world-first superlative is imported.

## Photography

All four selected photos were visually reviewed. S15 uses a stock-shaped Japan
spec-R b-package hardtop body, with later equipment/manual/HICAS limits in its
caption. The earlier candidate with front-mounted intercooler and modified body
was not selected. The 180SX uses a documented 1990 European 200SX fastback;
its export equipment, visible aftermarket wheels and current tune do not
certify Japan Type II hardware. Sileighty front swaps, a pickup conversion,
fixed-light/body-kit conversions and late round-taillight Type X candidates
were rejected for the early body reference.

Datsun uses a documented 1967.5 low-windscreen Sports 2000 body, without
certifying the selected Australian engine/market; nonstandard wheels and
restoration limits are stated. The 370Z is a 2009-model launch coupe photographed
at the 2008 San Francisco show. Visible touring/sport equipment does not certify
a manual gearbox or package content. It is distinct from later NISMO bodies.
Creators, licences, original source URLs and unchanged image bytes are retained:
CC BY-SA 4.0 for S15, CC0 for the European fastback and CC BY 2.0 for Datsun/Z34.
Factory press availability is not treated as an open image licence.
