## Selection and identity

The complete 636-car inventory was checked for identifiers, family names,
variants and aliases: Austin-Healey/100/100-4/BN1, MGA/Twin Cam, Daimler/SP250/Dart,
Volvo/P1800/1800S/1800E and Giulietta/Sprint Speciale/SS1300. The existing Healey
3000 and other MG families are distinct. No selected family was present.
One record per absent family is counted. Extra model years, gearbox choices,
body revisions, rebadges and competition derivatives do not multiply the count.

Facel II remains a research hold: the marque archive distinguishes a 6.3-litre
homologation reference from 6.7-litre production specifications, with differing
output/transmission accounts. A readable original production technical reference
is needed before admission. It has no record or count in this batch.

## Technical evidence

All public admission reasons, field-level sources and image provenance are in
[data/icons-road-30.json](../data/icons-road-30.json). Period PDFs were rendered
and read visually; extracted snippets alone were not treated as technical proof.

Austin-Healey uses the original Austin Motor Company 100 brochure, publication
1040/E, hosted as 1953. All five pages were rendered and the technical leaf PDF4
was enlarged and read. It establishes 2660 cc, 90 bhp at 4000 rpm, 150 lb-ft at
2000 rpm, 7.5:1 compression, three-speed manual with overdrive on second/top,
twin SU carburettors, independent front coils, rear semi-elliptic springs,
11-inch hydraulic drums and two seats. Native units are retained. Printed
Autocar test extracts are not relabelled as factory performance. BN2 four-speed,
100M kit, aluminium 100S and six-cylinder data are excluded. The National Motor
Museum's 100M account supports original 100/Austin history and export success
only; its 110-bhp collection car is not this standard BN1 reference.

MGA uses the original MG driver's handbook AKD879B, Third Edition. The 37-spread
file was rendered in a first-20-page contact sheet; title PDF2, printed pages
4–5/PDF4 and wheel illustration PDF20 were read separately. It states 1588 cc,
twin overhead camshafts, 9.9:1 compression, four-speed manual with synchromesh
on second/third/fourth, four hydraulic discs, 5.90–15 Road Speed tyres, 3.96-m
length and 4.3:1 rear axle. No unprinted power/acceleration is invented. Later
8.3:1 tune, pushrod models, De Luxe, coupe and Sebring racers are excluded.
The MG Car Club Twin Cam Group supplies the development history. An apparent
9700-rpm typo in a separate club model profile is not used as technical evidence.

Daimler uses the original Daimler/RAC base production application, FIA20 GT.
All 14 pages were rendered; cover, engine, transmission, chassis, options and
production-certificate PDF1/3/5/6/8/10 were read separately, plus covering letter
PDF9. The certificate is dated 22 June 1960 and refers to the application of
12 May: more than 600 identical cars were completed on 11 May, at least 33%
with optional wire wheels. It is not a certificate dated 12 May. Base technical
fields establish 2548 cc, cast-iron V8 block/aluminium heads, 8.2:1 compression,
pushrod valves, twin SU HD6 carburettors, four-speed manual, four hydraulic
discs without servo and independent-front/live-rear suspension. Seats retain
the form's 2/4 notation. Optional overdrive, automatic and later reinforced
B/C specifications are not silently standard. No unprinted output is invented.
Jaguar Daimler Heritage Trust's surviving prototype account supports Turner's
engineering and Daimler's sporting departure, without assigning prototype
identity or all-aluminium engine wording to the production form.

Volvo uses the original AB Volvo USA P1800 brochure RK131/3, footer 2.61,
100000 USA. Both pages were rendered and technical PDF2 read individually.
Its native 1.78 litres, 100 SAE bhp at 5500 rpm, 108 lb-ft at 4000 rpm, 9.5:1
compression, twin horizontal carburettors, fully synchronised four-speed,
steel unitary body, occasional rear seats and servo front-disc/rear-drum brakes
are retained. Selected without optional electrical overdrive. No SAE-net/kW
conversion, later power, 2-litre, injected E, automatic or ES estate is substituted.
Volvo's factory history supports Petterson, Jensen/Pressed Steel, the 1963
assembly transfer and The Saint association; its broad range table is not used
to blend technical alternatives.

Alfa Romeo uses original ACI/manufacturer application AR13, FIA122 GT, valid
from 16 May 1959, with Bertone closed coachwork. All 14 pages were rendered;
base cover/engine/cam/gearbox/performance PDF1/3/4/6/7 read individually.
The base specifies 1290 cc, aluminium block/head with wet iron liners,
approximately 9.7:1 compression, twin chain-driven overhead cams, two Weber
40DCO3 or 40DCOE carburettors, five-speed synchromesh and hydraulic drums with
two permissible drum assemblies. Native 100 DIN CV at 6500 rpm is preserved.
The printed 200-km/h claim is omitted because the factory retrospective gives
a differing speed account. Later extensions, SVZ/SZ and 1600-cc Giulia SS are
excluded. Alfa's factory anniversary account supports Scaglione/Bertone design
and long-distance GT positioning. Its accessible web-reader text was read;
a local 403 route was not bypassed. Racing results for other Giulietta bodies
are not assigned to the SS.

## Photography

All five final Commons photographs were visually reviewed. Original bytes are
unchanged; each manifest records creator, licence URL, description page, caption
and SHA-256. Photographs represent the road families and do not authenticate
installed engines, exact output, market or gearbox.

Mr.choppers' CC BY-SA 3.0 black BN1 is attributed approximately 1954 and shows
the narrow four-cylinder body, standard-style bonnet and folding screen.
Thesupermat's CC BY-SA 3.0 red 1959 MGA at Bonhams Paris shows the Twin Cam badge
and centre-lock disc wheels; restoration details do not certify 9.9:1 internals.
Rutger van der Maar's CC BY 2.0 1962 SP250 at InterClassics Brussels represents
the family with current wire wheels, without certifying the 1960 base chassis.
Herranderssvensson's CC BY-SA 3.0 1961 P1800 at Tjolöholm shows the early curved
bumpers and Jensen-era road body, without inferring tune from the open bonnet.
Andrew Bone's CC BY 2.0 Giulietta SS1300 at Beaulieu is identified as 1959 but
its restoration/body details do not authenticate a particular low-nose revision
or engine. No additional body revision is counted. Original factory and FIA
photographs are not treated as freely licensed assets.
