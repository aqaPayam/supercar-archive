## Evidence boundaries

BMW M's historical table and chassis account support the selected M535i road
reference. Optional spoilers/stripes are not standard equipment. Broad first-M
claims, a simplified M1-engine relationship, production totals, market values
and mixed factory/press acceleration figures are omitted. The 1980 selection
follows the launch/table account rather than treating the initial 1979 wording
as a dated brochure.

Mercedes-Benz's R129 anniversary release distinguishes the original V8 tune
from later revisions. The hardtop condition accompanies its drag coefficient.
ADS availability does not establish standard fitment; later panoramic roof,
gearbox and AMG hardware are excluded. Design and safety innovations support
the sporting-GT admission without a universal first-in-the-world assertion.

Lane Motor Museum's own 1969 Saab road-car record supplies its technical
reference; the output rating standard stays unspecified. Saab Car Museum's
account of its preserved Swedish Rally winner establishes competition lineage.
Neither competition power nor studded tyres is transferred to the road car.
The museums' broad production-year, first-victory and final-appearance claims
are not repeated as comprehensive global history.

The Corsa uses the English manufacturer release reproduced beneath the dated
April 2011 article. Normal torque and overboost stay separate, and the source's
fuel qualification is retained. Planned production does not establish an actual
total. Later retrospective date/power discrepancies are not silently combined
with this release. Joshua Dowling's actual European driving report supplies
manual/front-drive details and the chassis assessment, with price/interior/body
criticisms intact. Australian availability and converted pricing were speculative.
No Nürburgring lap record is asserted.

These are source-specific road references, not certificates of every market,
year or derivative. The full baseline technical/media audit remains separate.
