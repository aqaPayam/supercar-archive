## Identity

All 694 existing identity records were checked using Giulia/Quadrifoglio/952,
75/Evoluzione/162B, 155/Q4/167, HSV/GTS/GEN-F, Commodore/VL/SS Group A,
Torana/LX/A9X and Falcon/XY/GT-HO aliases. Existing Giulia Sprint GTA is the
historic 105-series coupe, not the modern 952 saloon. No 75, 155, HSV,
Commodore, Torana or Falcon family was present. Substring matches in Ferrari
GTS, Mustang, Nissan GTS-R, 675LT and model years are unrelated.

The two HSVs represent the foundational VL road-homologation family and a much
later VF-based modern flagship, not two trims or years of one contemporary
reference. No VL SV88, VN Group A, optional gearbox, later GTS tune/W507,
Torana hatchback, Falcon Phase I/II/IV, extra 75 engine or 155 facelift is counted.
Shared Holden origins do not make Torana, Commodore and the existing Monaro
one named production model. Same-generation variant counting and dedicated
racing/prototype scope remain pending user boundaries.

## Evidence

Field-level sources and native ratings are in [the manifest](../data/icons-road-39.json).

- Giulia uses the actual June 2016 German factory introduction, which explicitly
  pairs 375 kW with the manual gearbox. Carbon construction, active splitter and
  twin-clutch differential are distinguished from optional carbon-ceramic brakes
  and seats. Diesel/ordinary Giulia figures, dry-versus-kerb mass and later tunes
  are excluded. Blocked technical PDFs are not treated as read.
- 75 uses the manufacturer's Italian engineering history. It distinguishes the
  1762-cc Evoluzione from the preceding 1779-cc turbo, America and Twin Spark.
  The preceding turbo's output is not assigned to the Evo without an explicit
  reference. Race tune, cage, mass and an erroneous absolute last-RWD claim
  are excluded. Original homologation quantity explains the programme but is
  not imported as a survivor count.
- GEN-F uses the original May 2013 release and the archived equipment table.
  Manual TR6060 and optional automatic are separated. Brake-applied torque
  vectoring is not described as a twin-clutch differential. Exact cc, peak rpm,
  kerb mass, later GEN-F2 and tuned W507 data are not guessed or mixed.
- VL uses the factory archived production-equipment table and separately mapped
  HSV marque history. Adjacent VN and later models supply no technical data.
  Exact output/capacity absent from this reference remain omitted. The unusual
  gearbox-code spelling is not silently corrected; the stated manufacturer and
  five-speed type are retained. Optional intake mesh is not standardised.
- A9X uses the actual GM Parts & Accessories bulletin No.444, 31 August 1977,
  found at PDF725 of the 754-page factory LX parts compilation. Header and full
  standard/option list were enlarged/read, together with relevant PDF727 parts.
  The appendix was located in context using rendered overviews; the whole
  catalogue is not claimed reviewed. Standard M21 is separated from optional
  MC7 Borg Warner T/10, flares and hatchback spoiler. Unusual axle-ratio wording
  is omitted. A separately cited factory memorandum excerpt establishes
  four-wheel braking; the responsible chassis engineer's first-hand account
  supplies development context. The magazine's tribute car, L34 tune and tank
  are not stock-road evidence.
- Falcon uses Mel Nichols' original October 1971 Wheels first drive, explicitly
  republished by its publisher. The selected road-test car's wide ratios and
  3.25 axle remain qualified. Its standard road rev limiter is distinguished
  from the competition engine. Ford's horsepower claim and the author's
  estimate are not silently reconciled. The later CARS passage concerns a
  modified Phase Three plus and supplies none of its cam, 850-cfm carburettor,
  axle or performance fields. Modern tests of restored as-raced cars are not
  road-specification sources; the speedometer photograph is not an independent
  speed certification.
- 155 uses the original Fiat and Alfa Romeo Motors Japan Q4 brochure occupying
  PDF1–20 of a 55-page compilation. Relevant cover, engine/drivetrain and actual
  specification/imprint spreads were rendered and read. PDF9 and PDF19–20 were
  individually enlarged. The imprint says Ediz.Japan 9210-2315; no unprinted
  year is inferred. The native 185-PS DIN specification is distinguished from
  European 190-PS and later 186-PS references. Adjacent Twin Spark/V6 brochures,
  modified ECU files, optional sunroof and GTA/DTM competition hardware are
  excluded. No claim that the Q4 is the DTM car's road homologation is made.

## Photographs and held research

All seven licensed Commons pictures were visually inspected, with original
source pages, creator/licence links and unchanged downloaded-byte hashes stored.
Captions qualify exact market, year, gearbox, mechanical originality and build
provenance. The 75 uses a complete rear three-quarter road-event view rather
than a cropped front detail. The GTS photograph illustrates the correct GEN-F
body; the photographer's conflicting generic LS3 text is explicitly rejected
in favour of HSV's LSA engineering source. The Falcon's later offered Globe
wheels are not passed off as the 1971 road-test wheel reference. The A9X photo
cannot certify original provenance or which body options were originally fitted.

Earlier HSV E-Series, police-liveried/tuned GTS search matches, ordinary VL
Commodore/SV88, racing Toranas and unrelated Falcon GT/utility images were not
selected. A temporary image-server rate limit was allowed to clear. The Torana
PDF downloaded with normal system TLS certificate verification after the
custom research CA bundle lacked its issuer; certificate checks remained on.
The 155 brochure came from a public alternative host; a challenge page on
another host was not bypassed or treated as read.

Eighth-generation Galant VR-4, Racing Puma, Ginetta G4 and Chevette HS remain
research only from earlier turns.
