## Identity and duplicates

Identifiers, manufacturers, families, generations and variants in the complete
632-car inventory were searched for Jensen/FF/Interceptor, Mark 2/Mark II/Mk2,
240/340/Daimler aliases, Ace/Aceca/Cobra/Ruddspeed and Sunbeam/Tiger/Alpine 260.
No selected family was present. The existing Talbot Sunbeam Lotus is a different
later hatchback. Generic Mk I/Mk II and 240 search hits belonged to unrelated
makes/models and were checked. Jaguar's existing E-type, XJ-S and XK/XKR do not
cover the Mark II sporting saloon.

Only one original Jensen FF, one Mark II, one original Ace and one Tiger is
counted. No separate Interceptor, FF II/III, SP, alternate Mark II engine,
Daimler rebadge, AC-engine/Ruddspeed Ace, Aceca, Tiger IA/II or Alpine 260 badge
multiplies the count. Dedicated racers, prototype CV8 FF, replicas, continuation
cars, current AC Classics and restoration/tuning packages are excluded.

## Technical evidence

All public admission facts and field/source mappings are retained in
[data/icons-road-29.json](../data/icons-road-29.json).

Jensen FF uses the original Jensen Motors English two-page brochure, publication
CGA/LB/966/2½M, hosted by Auto-Brochures as 1966. Both pages were rendered;
the full technical leaf PDF2 was enlarged and visually read. It establishes
6276-cc Chrysler 90-degree OHV V8, 10:1 compression, Carter four-barrel
carburettor, three-speed Torqueflite automatic, Ferguson four-wheel drive,
steel body/tubular chassis, four seats and Dunlop four-wheel discs with
Maxaret anti-skid control. No power or torque rating is printed, so none is
invented or taken from another magazine/market. The Jensen Museum's separate
Ferguson and Maxaret engineering accounts support significance and distinguish
the earlier CV8 FF development/demonstration from the selected production body.
No universal first-in-the-world or aircraft stopping-distance claim is used.
The former Swiss club PDF route returned 404 locally and could not be rendered
by the web reader; it is not the final evidence route. The complete readable
original brochure on Auto-Brochures resolves that access gap.

Jaguar uses the original Jaguar Cars Inc., New York, four-page US 3.8 Sedan
brochure hosted by Jaguar Club Russia as 1964. All four pages were rendered;
PDF2's specification and PDF3–4's road-body/manual context were read individually.
It states 3781-cc XK six, twin overhead camshafts, 8:1 compression, SU HD6
carburettors, native 225 bhp at 5500 rpm, four-speed manual with overdrive,
rear hypoid drive, integral steel body, five seats and servo-assisted Dunlop
four-wheel discs. The manual/overdrive option is selected; Borg-Warner automatic
ratios are excluded. The host year is identified as an attribution rather than
an authenticated print date. No SAE-net label or kW conversion is added.
The Heritage Trust's preserved 1967 Mark II supports touring-car/rally and
cultural context. Its 220-bhp narrative and performance figures do not replace
the chosen US reference; the museum car's restored finish is not factory
specification. The separately located 1960 catalogue was not used to mix fields.

AC Ace Bristol uses AC Cars/RAC application KJ2, FIA117 GT, whose cover is
marked 1963. The complete ten-page original file was downloaded and rendered
in a contact sheet; base pages 1, 3–6, the blank/special engine page 7 and
optional-equipment page 8 were individually read. It establishes 1971-cc inline
six, cast-iron block/aluminium head, two pushrod valves per cylinder, one
chain-driven block camshaft, three Solex downdraught carburettors, four-speed
Bristol manual, rear drive, front discs/rear drums, independent transverse-leaf
suspension and two seats. The cover covers several Bristol engine prefixes,
and the base text permits alternate compression/axle ratios: no single power,
compression or D2 rating is invented. The selected road reference omits optional
overdrive and later anti-roll-bar extension; alternative tanks, duplicate pumps
and special equipment are not silently standard. The current FIA index date
is not used to authenticate a precise original print day.

AC Owners Club's model archive supplies Tojeiro tubular/aluminium construction,
Bristol factory adoption and later Cobra-production lineage. It separately
discusses AC engines and Ruddspeed modifications; their output does not belong
to the selected Bristol road car. The Martin museum page found in discovery
mixes some broader Ace history and a later Ruddspeed label; it is not used to
supply technical fields. Recognition describes the production basis and is not
presented as a currently registered road car or event-eligibility certificate.

Sunbeam uses the original Rootes/Sunbeam Talbot FIA509 Group3 application,
whose cover says valid from 1 January 1966. The 30-page PDF was downloaded;
first 15 pages were rendered in a contact sheet and base pages 1, 6, 8–11
individually read. The cover records production starting 1 July 1964 and
500 identical cars by 31 October 1964. Those are the form's declarations,
not a claim that homologation was the original public launch. The current
index's neighbouring date is not substituted for the signed cover.
The base gives 4261 cc, 90-degree V8, 8.8:1 compression, cast-iron block/heads,
central chain-driven pushrod camshaft, single Ford two-barrel carburettor,
wet sump, water cooling, four synchronised manual forward ratios and steel
unitary construction. The manufacturer's declared catalogue output is explicitly
164 gross BHP at 4400 rpm and 258 lb-ft at 2200 rpm; it is not modern net output.
Later extensions, optional ratios, LAT kits, 289 engines, scoops, bigger fuel
systems and racing brakes are excluded.

The Shelby American Collection's account of works Tiger B9470035 supports
Shelby's contract/development and motorsport lineage only. Its modified
289/200-hp specification, rally-car details and results do not become this
260 production road reference. No separate works racer is added.
