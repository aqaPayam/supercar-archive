## Mercedes-Benz E55 AMG type 210.074 — original 1997–1999 factory reference

The W210 E55 AMG combined an AMG-developed V8 with uprated suspension and brakes and a heavy-duty automatic drivetrain in an everyday executive saloon. Contemporary first-hand driving evidence documents a substantial dynamic transformation beyond the ordinary E-Class. Count this W210 performance-road family once; engine choices, facelifts, estate bodies and model years do not add entries.

Original W210 E55 AMG saloon, type 210.074, selected from the factory 1997–1999 reference. The archive distinguishes August 1997 pre-production from October 1997 standard production; the year field denotes introduction, not a US model year. M113 E55/113.980, 5439 cc, 260 kW at 5500 rpm and 530 Nm at 3000 rpm. Standard five-speed torque-converter automatic, rear-wheel drive and original European tyres. No later W211 supercharged specification, US 349-hp/tyre/test data, 4MATIC, later facelift equipment or inferred production total is imported.

## Ford RS Turbo Series 1 — 1984 introduction / original 1985 base recognition

The Series 1 Escort RS Turbo carried Ford SVE development into a front-drive performance hatchback intended as a Group A competition basis. Its intercooled turbo engine, electronic boost/ignition control and standard viscous limited-slip differential distinguish the engineering from an ordinary Escort. One MkIII sporting-road family is counted; XR3i/RS1600i badges, model years and competition preparations do not add entries.

Original MkIII / Series 1 production-road programme, introduced in 1984 according to Ford’s retrospective, with base mechanical reference from manufacturer form A-5272. Actual cover stamps June 1, 1985; portal metadata instead says June 2. Neither is a photographed chassis date. Actual 1597.2 cc is kept separate from the form’s 2236.1 cc turbo-equivalent competition capacity. Five-speed main manual column only; no additional competition gearbox, alternative final drive, minimum weight, brake option or later extension is imported. Ford retrospective supplies road output in native PS, road torque and original turbo/compression details; no bhp conversion.

## Evidence boundaries actually inspected

The actual Mercedes model archive body/specification was read in full, together
with the actual 1995–1999 W210 series history. It distinguishes August 1997
pre-production from October standard production and dates the E55 presentation
to September 1997. The selected 210.074 mechanics are M113 E55/113.980, 90-degree
alloy-block V8, 5439 cc, 97.0 × 92.0 mm, 10.5:1 compression, three valves per
cylinder, one overhead camshaft per bank, Bosch ME manifold injection, 260 kW
at 5500 rpm and 530 Nm at 3000 rpm. Rear drive, standard five-speed planetary
automatic with governed lock-up torque converter, steel unit body, double-
wishbone front/multilink rear suspension, factory braking description and
original European 235/40 and 265/35 ZR18 tyres remain tied to that reference.
No exact performance, weight, production total, price or generic E-Class safety
feature is imported. The archive's total through 2002 is not a 1997–1999 total.

The actual archived C/D first-hand March 1999 road test was read. It supports
the admission assessment of a substantial drivetrain/chassis transformation.
US output, larger tested tyres, rear-brake detail, acceleration and test figures
do not overwrite the selected European archive specification. No superlative
fastest-saloon claim is imported. The later W211 E55 remains a separate preserved
record with its own supercharged reference.

The actual original Ford A-5272 file downloaded intact: 11,295,611 bytes, 73
pages including extensions. Actual PDF indexes 0, 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 (printed
pages 1–4, 6–7) were visually read. The cover stamps June 1, 1985; the FIA
portal instead says June 2. This is a document-date discrepancy, not a new
model year. Ford's retrospective independently identifies the 1984 road
introduction. The cover and engine page show actual 1597.2 cc multiplied by
the 1.4 turbo equivalence to 2236.1 cc. The latter and portal's rounded 2236
are competition-class capacity, not physical engine size. No rounded conversion
replaces the original 1597.2-cc figure.

The base pages document front-transverse four-stroke inline-four layout,
liquid cooling, Bosch KE-Jetronic, belt-driven single overhead camshaft and
steel unitary construction. Only the five-speed manual main column is used;
the separate additional competition gearbox column is not the road gearset.
No individual ratios or final-drive option is imported. Original front viscous
coupling is also visible, consistent with the Ford road history. Recognition
seating counts, competition minimum weights, brake dimensions/options and later
engine/turbo/body extensions are omitted. Remaining PDF pages were not visually
read or used for specifications. Historical forms are consultation evidence,
not current event eligibility or individual-chassis registration certification.

The actual complete Escort III RS Turbo section of the July 2018 retrospective,
explicitly credited to Ford, was read. It supplies SVE development and Group A
production-basis intent plus original 132 PS, 180 Nm at 3000 rpm, 8.3:1,
intercooled Garrett T3 and electronic boost/ignition control. Native PS is kept;
no UK bhp conversion or race output is invented. A planned 5000-car series is
not a final production count. No absolute first-turbo-Ford or all-white claim
is repeated without its qualifications. Road and dedicated racing data remain
separate; the Group A context does not attribute race wins to every road car.
