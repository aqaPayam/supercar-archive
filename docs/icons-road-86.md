## Admission and selected road references

### Renault 17 Gordini

The Renault 17 Gordini connected a fuel-injected sporting road coupe with Renault’s factory rally-development programme, culminating in the R17’s 1974 Press on Regardless World Championship rally victory. The racing winner used a different engine: its achievement explains the family’s heritage, not the road car’s power. One original R15/R17 sister-coupe programme is counted, without adding ordinary R15s, TS/Gordini badges, roof styles, market engines, model years or racing evolutions separately.

Its model family became a TC2000 championship icon through the Berta/Traverso era, while the selected road reference is the European 97-kW petrol Turbo. ## Evidence actually inspected

### R17 road and competition boundaries

Renault's 38-page Gordini history was already downloaded intact. Actual full
PDF indexes 15 and 16 (printed pages 16–17) were now visually read in English
and French. They distinguish the October 1974 European 1605 cc type 844-12
fuel-injected Gordini, rated 108 ch DIN at 6000 rpm, with 78 x 84 mm bore/stroke,
from the earlier 1565 cc 807-12 TS and 807-13 North American reference and the
1647 cc 843-13 American engine. R1317 is the coupe and R1327 its opening-roof
counterpart; both are a single engine/programme reference, not separate counts.
No gearbox, torque, curb weight, acceleration or road top speed is invented.

The same pages explain the 1774 cc 807-Ga Group V development at 170 ch DIN
and the different 807-G4 four-valve/twin-overhead-cam 200-ch engine used by
Thérier/Delferrière for the 1974 Press on Regardless victory. That World
Championship event is heritage evidence, not proof that the road car had those
engines, power, valve gear, prototype steering or ZF limited-slip equipment.
The chapter's racing illustrations are not used as standard-road photographs.
No exact production total is inferred by combining the market/type counts.

The Renault Austria article's actual R15/R17 section was also read for adjacent
body-programme context, but its earlier 1565 cc TS rating is not substituted
for the selected October 1974 1605 cc Gordini. No new technical field depends
on an uninspected homologation form. A Group 2 form surfaced in search but
was not downloaded/read or used for this road profile.

### Fuego road-market and heritage boundaries

The actual Renault Austria coupe-history article, dated January 27, 2009, was
read both through the web reader and from a saved HTML-text extraction. The
Fuego section explicitly identifies the original 1983 petrol Turbo programme,
four cylinders, 1.6-litre class, and 97 kW / 132 PS. The kW figure is stated by
the source rather than calculated. It describes the fully glazed hatch and
0.34 Cw within its general Fuego-family history. The coefficient is labelled
as a family historical reference, not certification of each Turbo trim or the
photographed car. It does not justify inventing an exact cc/engine code,
transmission, torque, weight or imported tyre/equipment specification. Its
acceleration and consumption figures are not imported.

The full indexed text of Renault Argentina's July 25, 2022 manufacturer history
was actually retrieved through web search and read, including the model's
R18-platform background, wind-tunnel design, European petrol Turbo distinction,
Argentine road-engine development and TC2000 section. A direct open/raw request
returned only the Media Room header, so the read evidence is explicitly the
indexed article text, not an unseen live article body. That account documents
the Fuego's championship era from 1986 to 1993 and lasting Argentine sporting
identity. It is used for admission, without making its TC2000 engines or Berta
kit into European petrol-Turbo road specifications. Exact race-win/production
totals, absolute world-first aerodynamic claims, market-share statistics and
Argentine equipment are not imported.

French/English/Spanish Originals Museum Fuego URLs returned 503 on opening.
A Spanish search extract was visible but is not used as a technical source.
In particular, turbo-diesel speed/world-record wording is not used for this
petrol reference. No unavailable brochure/shop preview, racing data or unseen
museum specification table is used to fill missing fields. Access controls
were not bypassed.
