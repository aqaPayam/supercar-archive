## Identity and evidence boundaries

All eight manufacturer/model families were absent. Search aliases included
X-Bow/Xbow, BAC/Briggs/Mono, Donkervoort/D8, Dallara/Stradale, MF3/Wiesmann,
Ultima/GTR, Gumpert/Apollo and Radical/SR3. Unrelated Ferrari, Lancia, Maserati
and Alfa Romeo uses of “Stradale,” and Lamborghini/Aston Martin names containing
“Ultima,” do not represent these cars. Other makers' Seven-inspired designs
are not automatically Donkervoort aliases; the selected GTO has its own heavily
renewed programme. No earlier Donkervoort or further GTO derivative is added.

- **X-Bow:** Actual original press-release body establishes standard production
  construction, engine and manual gearbox. Use native 177 kW, avoiding its
  inconsistent bhp/PS labels. The 2007 prototype and Dallara-edition options,
  later R, GT and enclosed GT-XR are not the selected specification. Actual
  physical page 3 of KTM's 7 June 2016 factory PDF was rendered and read to
  distinguish the historical programmes. No later structural detail, optional
  differential, projected production, preliminary performance estimate or
  absolute “first-ever” marketing claim is backdated.
- **Mono:** The public 21 March 2011 article explicitly introduces the text as
  BAC's original press release. Its body, not a secondary compiled table,
  establishes the road concept and hardware. BAC's own history independently
  identifies the original 2.3-litre generation and distinguishes 2016 wider
  chassis/2.5-litre cars, Mono R and later generations. No Cup racer is imported.
- **D8 GTO:** The heritage page contains incompatible bhp/kW values and
  differing torque figures. Omit output, torque and peak rpm rather than
  reconcile them by guesswork. It is an undated hardware reference, not an
  asserted model year. The factory's Swiss-delivery account establishes road
  use; its approval timing differs from another factory history, so no exact
  universal approval date is claimed. Premium, RS, GTO-40, JD70, Individual
  Series and F22 hardware do not become extra records or fill missing fields.
- **Stradale:** Read Dallara's complete 16 November 2017 product paragraphs,
  including carbon construction, base barchetta, six-speed manual and Bosch
  controls. The dated release explicitly records road homologation and first
  deliveries. Windscreen, T-frame, gullwing closures, rear wing and robotised
  paddle shift are options. Current Euro 6E data, EXP race hardware and
  configuration-dependent downforce/acceleration are excluded.
- **MF3:** Select the factory's historical S54 specification. Its broad 1993
  family-origin statement does not establish when that engine entered service.
  Retain a null reference year rather than backdate S54 hardware. No MF4/MF5,
  inferred gearbox or Final Edition cosmetic count is added.
- **GTR:** Use the LS1 test configuration; registration varies by jurisdiction.
  No blanket US crash approval or later power upgrade is asserted.
- **Apollo:** Read the complete original Gumpert release body. Use its native
  478 kW and stated engine configuration, displacement, gearbox and structure.
  Contradictory horsepower labels and bore/stroke numbers, compiled header
  data, later Sport/Speed/S variants, race hybrids, aero-dependent top speed
  and the undemonstrated upside-down driving claim are omitted.
- **SR3 SL:** Original handbook No. 7225 V1 has imprint 7/12. Actual physical
  pages 2, 5 and 39 were rendered and read. The introduction explicitly
  identifies a purpose-designed, European-type-approved roadster. Page 39
  distinguishes standard 240 bhp from optional 300 bhp; only the former is
  selected. The reference year is the handbook revision, not the 2011 launch.
  No motorcycle-powered SR3/RS/RSX, current generic SR3 manual, optional tune
  or race-car mass is substituted.

## Licensed representative photographs

All eight photos were visually reviewed and copied unchanged. Seven Commons
description pages and the Radical photographer's own Flickr page retain
named creators, precise licence versions and URLs, original credits, captions
and downloaded/exported SHA-256 hashes in the manifest.

The Radical photograph's source page explicitly links **CC BY 2.0**, verified
through the link to its Creative Commons deed. The author identifies it as an
SR3 SL on the road. No all-rights-reserved alternative or manufacturer press
image is reused. Image metadata and visible bodywork are not technical proof
of an individual car's hidden engine, approval or current condition.

The X-Bow has an added beacon; the Dallara is shown with optional windscreen
and rear wing; the Ultima is a later-year coupe. These differences are expressly
captioned. Photographs of an enclosed KTM, Mono R, competition Apollo and
unidentified SR3 race cars were reviewed or encountered but not selected.
The two undated hardware references remain undated in the public data.
