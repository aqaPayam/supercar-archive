## Identity and duplicates

The complete inventory and previous manifests were checked, including BMW 328,
328i/3 Series, 507, Z1 and Mercedes 190/190 SL/W121 aliases. None of these road
models was present. The pre-war BMW 328 is unrelated to later 328i saloons. The
507 and Z1 are distinct roadster platforms, not further specifications of the
existing Z3 M Coupe, Z4 M Coupe or Z8. The W121 190 SL is a separate four-cylinder
Ponton-based grand tourer from the existing W198 300 SL Gullwing and W201 190 E.

One representative road reference is counted per model. The 328's special
Touring race bodies, 507 series/equipment changes, Z1 prototypes/Alpina builds,
Art Car paint and 190 SL hardtop/sports-accessory alternatives add no records.
The 190 SL qualifies through its lasting sporting-GT identity and place in the
SL tradition, rather than matching the 300 SL's racing ability or price.

## Evidence and specification boundaries

Sources, technical fields and public admission reasons are recorded in
[data/icons-road-22.json](../data/icons-road-22.json).

BMW 328 uses the standard-car technical section of the factory May 13, 2009
Mille Miglia heritage release, page 2. That page was rendered/read. The 1971-cc
inline six, native 80-bhp historical notation at 4500 rpm, four-speed central
shift, hydraulic drums and 155 km/h are separate from the adjacent 136-bhp,
6000-rpm, 200-km/h Berlin-Rome Touring racer. The factory May 12, 2011 anniversary
release, page 1, was also rendered/read: it explicitly separates the 1936 racing
debut from spring 1937 road production. Modified racers' victories explain the
model's lineage without becoming a claim that the standard road car had the
same tune, body or results. No modern 328i data are used.

BMW 507 uses the factory April 2005 anniversary release reproduced by Car Body
Design in November 2006, with its dedicated 507 technical table. Native 150 PS,
3168 cc, 7.8 compression, OHV central camshaft, twin Zenith carburettors and
four-speed all-synchromesh transmission are distinct from adjacent 140-PS 503
data. The 1957 reference does not count a series revision as another car.
Factory May 28, 2009 roadster history supplements the design/engineering context.
Its grille-orientation error and broad world-first claim are not imported.
The reproduced release explains why the 220-km/h advertised maximum depended
on a seat cover and racing windscreen; no unqualified top speed is recorded.
Optional power upgrades, gearbox ratios, fuel-tank evolution, late disc brakes
and conflicting production totals are omitted. Current archive pages marked as
AI-assisted are not the technical basis of this entry.

BMW Z1 uses the factory June 6, 2012 engineering retrospective. Its steel
monocoque, bonded/partly bolted composite floor, removable plastic panels,
front-mid inline six, five-speed manual, electric retracting doors and Z-axle
are explicitly described. Nominal 2.5-litre class is not converted into an
unsourced exact displacement. Historical hp convention is omitted. Early 1989
customer production is separate from the 1986 development study, 1987 show
reveal and 1988 press presentation. No prototype, Alpina, later Z3/Z4 or current
legal claim about driving with open doors is imported.

Mercedes-Benz 190 SL uses the factory Public Archive's exact 121.042 roadster
technical page and its family history, cross-checked against the August 11,
2021 manufacturer SL retrospective. Original 1955 main-series engine, 1897-cc
swept volume, 8.5 compression, four-speed, rear drive, independent suspension
and hydraulic drums are separate from later changes. The archive's 1882 cc is
a tax classification, not displacement. The source's uncertain carburettor
orientation wording is omitted while the two Solex 44 PHH units are retained.
The 2021 source supplies native 77 kW. No US gross-hp convention, W198 fuel
injection or racing tune is inherited. September 1959 compression, May 1956
brake booster, 1960 weight and August 1961 engine changes are not combined into
the selected original reference. Optional transverse rear seating means no
blanket two-seat attribute is asserted.

## Photography

All four selected photographs were visually reviewed. BMW 328 uses a documented
1938 Louwman Museum car with standard roadster body. BMW 507 uses a documented
1957 roadster at Greenwich Concours. Z1 uses a production-body side view with
nonstandard-looking wheels; its caption explicitly limits originality claims.
The 190 SL is a soft-top roadster photographed in 2007 without exact build-year
or early-equipment certification. No photo authenticates a chassis or measured
engine tune, and later visible trim does not change the selected technical
reference. The 328 and 507/Z1 image declarations are public domain; the 190 SL
uses CC BY-SA 3.0 Germany. Creator, licence, source URL and unchanged bytes are
preserved. Custom attribution-only and incomplete-author alternatives were not
selected. A candidate description wrongly claiming 190 SL production to 1993
was rejected as factual evidence.
