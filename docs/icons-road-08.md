## Duplicate review

The complete inventory was checked by family, generation and aliases. Existing
Mustangs do not cover S197 Boss 302 or S550 GT350R; the existing Viper is the
later Gen V ACR. Existing Corvettes do not cover C1, C2, C3 or C5. One reference
per absent generation is added, rather than multiple engines, bodies or years.
The 1971 Javelin AMX is the second-generation Javelin coupe, distinct from the
existing 1970 two-seat AMX. Daytona and Superbird are separate manufacturers'
road homologation models; no ordinary Charger variant is added alongside Daytona.

The manifest records admission reasons as sourced public facts, technical
source mappings, exact specification boundaries, photo credits and hashes.

## Evidence boundaries

- Boss 302: the final 2012 Ford brochure's standard Boss equipment column
  supplies 444 hp, rather than the early 440-hp estimate. Optional Torsen and
  Recaro equipment is not assumed. Laguna Seca's track-only splitter is excluded.
- GT350R: the original Ford 2016 technical sheet is preserved by a facsimile
  host because the old factory link no longer serves it. The R-specific wheel
  and two-seat columns are used. Base GT350 equipment is not substituted.
- Viper: the museum documents an original 1992 RT/10, its V10, six-speed and
  minimal equipment. Its incorrect coupe body label is rejected. A brochure
  describing 400 cubic inches, five speeds and torsion bars concerns prototype
  hardware and is excluded from the production record.
- C1: the GM heritage kit's original AMA pages identify the 283-cu-in injection
  engine with optional competition camshaft, 283 bhp at 6200 rpm and 10.5:1
  compression. The lower-output injection tune and racing SS/Airbox equipment
  are not mixed into this road reference.
- C2: GM's own collection history supplies the split-window coupe, independent
  rear suspension and fuel-injected 327. Its suspect power-peak field and
  undefined mass are withheld. No Grand Sport or Z06 option certification is made.
- C3: the January 1971 Chevrolet brochure identifies LT-1, mechanical lifters,
  9.0:1 compression, manual-only gearbox and unavailable air conditioning.
  An erroneous museum automatic claim is rejected. The period 330-hp gross
  rating is preserved; conflicting retrospective net ratings are not imported.
- C5: the 2001 Chevrolet brochure supplies the original 385-hp LS6, FE4 and
  titanium exhaust hardware. Later 405-hp output, base LS1 equipment and
  optional base-car roof/wheels are excluded.
- GTO: the original brochure's standard 389 single-four-barrel engine and
  three-speed manual are used. Tri-Power output and close-ratio equipment are
  excluded. Shipping weight is not relabelled as curb weight. GM's separate
  historical account supports its significance.
- GSX: the 1970 Buick brochure's Stage 1 column supplies 360 hp, 10.5:1,
  four-speed manual and differential/brake equipment. Standard 350-hp GSX and
  later lower-compression specifications are not mixed in.
- Daytona: the museum's documented restored road car supplies the 440 Magnum,
  four-speed and A34 drivetrain. Dubious race-speed chronology is excluded.
- Challenger: the original dealer brochure's 426 Street Hemi option and
  four-speed manual apply. Standard 383, Six Pack and unverified optional
  axle/brake/Shaker equipment are excluded.
- Superbird: the museum's four-barrel 440 and Torqueflite road specification
  is used. Six-barrel, Hemi and NASCAR race-car specifications are excluded.
- AAR: the museum supplies the 340 road engine, three twin-barrel Holleys,
  manual gearbox and fibreglass bonnet. Race-car 305 engines, incorrect Hemi
  comparisons and claims that Gurney's team built the road cars are excluded.
- Javelin: original AMC brochure pages identify the optional 401 Go package,
  four-speed, cowl induction, Twin-Grip and front discs. Trans Am's own series
  history separately supports the 1971 championship context; its racing
  specifications are not transferred to the road car.

Relevant original technical tables and equipment columns were rendered and
visually inspected. Historical advertised horsepower remains explicitly
qualified in its native units, without invented modern net-kW conversions.

All 14 chosen photographs were visually inspected for model and body. Wrong
year/trim, tightly cropped and missing-credit alternatives were rejected.
Representative photos do not authenticate original engines, reference-year
builds or optional equipment. Creator, licence, source link and unchanged image
bytes are retained, including the replacement C3 coupe photograph's CC BY 2.0 credit.
