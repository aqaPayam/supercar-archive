## Identity and selection

The complete inventory was searched for Z31/300ZX, Galant/VR-4/E39A,
X1/9/X 1/9/X19 and Dolomite/Sprint. Its only existing 300ZX is the distinct
Z32/GCZ32 twin-turbo generation; no Z31 was present. Other Mitsubishi, Fiat
and Triumph records cover different models. No selected family was present.
Only one Z31 and one original family for each other car is counted. Additional
years, facelifts, transmissions, editions and dedicated racers are excluded.
Previously pending extra-variant/prototype counting boundaries remain pending.

## Technical evidence

Public admission facts, exact sources and attribute mappings are in
[data/icons-road-31.json](../data/icons-road-31.json). Period PDF pages were
rendered and read visually rather than accepted from extracted snippets alone.

Nissan uses the original nine-spread Nissan's 1986 Cars and Trucks USA brochure.
Every spread was rendered; the full 300ZX spread PDF6, printed pages 10–11,
was read individually. It explicitly gives a turbocharged, fuel-injected
3.0-litre overhead-cam V6, five-speed overdrive manual, four-wheel independent
suspension, power rack-and-pinion steering, three-way adjustable Turbo dampers
and new 16-inch Turbo wheels. It does not print exact road output or swept
capacity in that spread, so neither is invented. Its approximately 215-mph
GTP race-car reference is not a road top speed. Optional leather/electronics,
the normally aspirated 2-seater, 2+2, later facelift, 200ZR and Z32 are excluded.
Nissan's factory Z anniversary account supplies the Z31's 1983 introduction
and transition to VG30ET turbo V6. Anniversary totals and Canadian museum car
specifications do not become this US 1986 reference.

Galant uses original Mitsubishi/JAF JA-117, FIA A-5364. The complete 183-page
file was downloaded; its first 16 pages were rendered in a contact sheet and
base PDF1–7, 9 and 11 read separately. The signed cover says valid from
1 May 1988. Physical swept capacity is 1997.4 cc, explicitly multiplied by
1.7 for 3395.6 cc competition equivalence; the latter is not engine capacity.
The index suffix '(1997)' is not the model year. Base fields give steel unitary
construction, five seats, transverse-front inline four, cast-iron block/alloy
head, liquid cooling, turbocharger, belt-driven twin cams, four valves per
cylinder, ECI MULTI electronic injection, wet sump, five-speed manual and drive
to both axles. The base also documents independent suspension, front ventilated
and rear solid discs, and hydraulic rear-wheel steering. No maximum rebore,
rally output, later extension, automatic or subsequent V6 VR-4 is substituted.
Mitsubishi's own team archive supports six WRC wins by competition derivatives
and final testing of technology intended for the Lancer Evolution. Its 290-ps
table and 300-plus-horsepower narrative describe racing cars, not this road basis.

Fiat uses the original two-page Fiat Motors of North America X1/9 brochure,
hosted as 1977. Both pages were rendered; full technical PDF2 was read. It
establishes a transverse mid-mounted, water-cooled SOHC 1290-cc four with
8.5:1 compression and Weber vertical dual-barrel carburettor, four synchronised
forward manual ratios plus reverse, rear drive, unitized construction,
independent coil/strut suspension, four disc brakes, two seats and removable
roof panel. No unprinted output or European 75-CV figure is assigned to this
US emissions-market reference. California catalyst/final-drive alternatives
are not silently universal. Later 1498-cc Five Speed, injection, Bertone-badged
series, Dallara racers and Abarth prototypes are excluded. Stellantis/Fiat
Heritage supplies Gandini/Bertone styling, packaging and US success, not mixed
later specifications.

Triumph uses original British Leyland/Triumph/RAC FIA5542 Group 1 production
recognition. The 51-page file was downloaded; first 16 pages were rendered in
a contact sheet and base PDF1, 8–12 and 14 individually read. The signed cover
says valid from 1 January 1974; the neighbouring index date is not substituted.
Base fields establish 1998 cc, a 45-degree inline four with chromium-iron block
and alloy head, 9.5:1 compression with its stated tolerance, one duplex-chain
camshaft operating inlet bucket tappets/exhaust rockers, two inlet and two
exhaust valves per cylinder, twin SU HS6 carburettors, four synchronised manual
ratios, front wishbones/rear four-link live axle, coils and servo-assisted front
discs/rear drums. The manufacturer's declared catalogue output is explicitly
127 net BHP at 5700 rpm; no DIN/SAE conversion is added. Optional Laycock
overdrive, alternate ratios, subsequent automatic, racing extensions and the
1854-cc eight-valve form 5477 are excluded. The source is a production basis,
not a currently registered survivor or event eligibility certificate.
Motor Sport's July 1973 first-hand drive supplies contemporary evidence of
its distinctive engine and sporting/refined road character. The online
republication date is 2014; test maxima and tuning discussion are not assigned
as factory specifications. No universal first-multivalve claim is made.

## Photography

All four final Commons images were visually reviewed, with bytes unchanged.
Creators, licence URLs, source pages, captions and SHA-256 hashes are recorded.
Elise240SX's CC BY-SA 4.0 1986 300ZX Turbo at Sault Ste. Marie shows the short
Z31 road body and T-bar roof. Its Canadian location, current exhaust and trim
do not authenticate selected US tune or gearbox. A later facelift photo and
blurred moving early car were rejected. A separately reviewed Australian car
with current wheels was not chosen.

The CC0 E39A VR-4 road saloon by 先従隗始 at Shimoiso, Gifu, is explicitly
identified as a later version of the original family. Its body and wheels do
not authenticate May 1988 tune or operating 4WS. A clearer modified example
with bonnet pins and aftermarket seats was not chosen. Mr.choppers' CC BY-SA
3.0 white 1978 US Series 1 X1/9 shows the road body, removable roof and ladder
bumpers; exact 1977 carburetion and restoration originality are not certified.
Later 1500s, modified cars and Dallara racing images were rejected. Akela NDE's
CC BY-SA 2.0 France 1978 Russet Brown Dolomite Sprint at Morges shows the road
saloon, Sprint-style wheels, vinyl roof and spoiler. Its exterior does not
authenticate the selected early internals or absence of optional overdrive.
Manufacturer brochure/FIA photographs are not used as freely licensed assets.
