# Lamborghini baseline identity review

Reviewed 2026-10-30 at 897 working records. This bounded review inventories
29 existing profiles: ten Aventadors, seven Gallardos and twelve Huracáns.
Their stored flags identify 27 road profiles and two competition cars.
No cars, specifications, photographs or flags are changed.

The [structured ledger](../data/baseline-identity-review-01.json) retains every
identifier and its existing generation, variant, years and road flag. It maps
the profiles to three provisional road programmes and two competition branches.
These are relationship groups, not three newly certified qualifying cars.
Different generation descriptions in the database do not establish independent
vehicle generations. No record is automatically approved, merged or deleted.

The manufacturer's [Ultimae archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/aventador-lp-780-4-ultimae)
places that derivative at the end of the Aventador family. Its
[Aventador S archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/aventador-s)
describes rear-wheel steering and the carbon-fibre monocoque. These establish
meaningful engineering within the programme, without proving every coupe or
roadster deserves a separate count. The V12 carbon-monocoque supercar programme
is a strong admission candidate; detailed baseline approval remains open.

The [Gallardo LP 560-4 archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/gallardo-lp-560-4-coupe)
identifies the named four-wheel-drive coupe. Its current revised-nose description
must not be applied to every earlier model year. The
[Spyder Performante archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/gallardo-lp-570-4-spyder-performante)
identifies a lightweight V10 soft-top derivative. This supports preserving those
identities, not approving all six road variants independently. Early 5.0
Superleggera, Balboni, LP 570-4 Superleggera and ordinary Spyder details still
need their own fresh evidence checks.

The [Huracán EVO archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/huracan-evo)
describes an evolution and its dynamics-control and rear-steering systems.
The [STO archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/huracan-sto)
explicitly identifies a road-legal model with motorsport-derived bodywork and
brakes. The [Sterrato archive](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/history/huracan-sterrato)
describes raised suspension and Rally calibration, while qualifying the road
homologation of additional front lamps by market. Those features are not
transferred to every Huracán record. STO and Sterrato deserve engineering
review rather than dismissal as cosmetic editions; separate qualifying credit
still depends on the owner's variant boundary.

The manufacturer's [motorsport heritage](https://www.lamborghini.com/en-en/motorsport/motorsport-models/heritage)
identifies the 2009 Gallardo Super Trofeo and the original Huracán LP 620-2
Super Trofeo, which debuted in the 2015 racing season. It distinguishes later
Super Trofeo evolutions and GT3 cars. The two stored competition profiles keep
their non-road flags; the road-going STO is not interchangeable with them.
Production totals on that retrospective were not audited or imported.

## Review scope and verification

All 29 identity rows were inventoried. Eight primary manufacturer pages were
read for selected named derivatives and family relationships; the ledger
distinguishes nine directly checked identities from twenty inventoried
identities whose individual derivative evidence was not newly verified.
Family grouping is a curation inference, not a full historical or mechanical
audit of every variant. Existing years, performance values, photos, licences,
prices and source rows are not recertified. Unavailable retrospective URLs and
homepage redirects provide no supporting evidence here.

The review preserves the database byte for byte, every local and exported
asset, and the existing static export. The ledger's identifiers and stored
fields were compared with the actual database; the 29/27/2 inventory and family
totals agree. Database integrity, foreign keys and the complete 897-car export
comparison pass. No importer, application or viewer code changed.

Same-programme body/variant counting and dedicated competition-car eligibility
remain pending owner boundaries. The 395-record baseline audit and the
1,000-qualifying-icon goal remain open; 897 is a working record count.
