# Road-icon batch 136: original T-head Mercer Raceabout

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 932 existing identities. One absent Mercer
sporting-road family brings the working inventory to 933 records: 538 curated
road identifiers across 136 manifests plus 395 preserved baseline records.
This is not a certified qualifying count toward the 1,000-icon goal.

## Admission and identity

The Raceabout belongs for its established place in early American production
sports-car engineering, motorsport heritage and enduring rivalry with the
Stutz Bearcat. Its stripped-down road design has significance beyond being
old or rare. The complete inventory was checked for Mercer, Raceabout, Type 35
and 35-R/35-C/35-J aliases. No Mercer programme was present; an unrelated
BMW M635CSi text match is not a Mercer identity.

Count the original T-head Raceabout road programme once across years, subtype
labels and equipment. Later L-head cars, dedicated competition machines,
replicas and unrelated Mercer touring models receive no extra admission.
The selected reference is **Revs Institute's 1912 Model 35-C, serial 764**;
it is not the mixed 1911-chassis/1913-drivetrain Simeone reference.

## Selected museum evidence

[The current Revs collection entry](https://revsinstitute.org/vehicle/1912-mercer-raceabout)
was read in full relevant scope, including the technical header and history.
The former museum.revsinstitute.org collection address redirects to this page.
All eight technical fields map to this reference: inline-four T-head engine,
300 cubic inches, quoted 58 hp at 1700 rpm, 108-inch wheelbase, two bucket
seats, driver monocle windscreen, rear tank and described cockpit controls.
Wheelbase converts exactly to 2743.2 mm. No SAE/DIN test standard or exact
metric displacement is invented. The stated 2610-pound weight has no resolved
basis and is omitted; no gearbox count, bore/stroke, torque, measured speed,
acceleration, production total or universal equipment originality is imported.
The photographed body is not proof of every mechanical detail.

[Heritage Museums & Gardens' original collection account](https://heritagemuseumsandgardens.org/hmg/1912-mercer-raceabout/)
was read, including its historical road-use guarantee, rivalry and technical
panel. It supports sporting context and road identity, not another counted
car. Its rounded five-litre figure does not replace Revs' 300 cubic inches.
The proposed Barney Oldfield ownership is only a belief and is not asserted.
Historical road-speed guarantees and race results are not newly measured
performance of the selected museum car. No prices or priority superlatives
are imported.

The earlier Simeone reference was read during candidate discovery and explicitly
discloses mixed years and possible racing history. It is not selected as stock
road specification. The Henry Ford's 1916 honeymoon touring car and the
American Heritage Museum's Ford-based replica likewise supply no technical
fields or representative photograph for this record. No full rebuild history
or individual chassis-authenticity certification is claimed here.
