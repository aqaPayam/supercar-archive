## Identity and duplicate review

The complete current inventory and earlier manifests were checked. M3 E30
and E46 CSL already exist; E36 and E92 are absent generations. No E36 3.0,
GT, SMG, E92 GTS/CRT, separate saloon/convertible or Competition-package
entry is added. The 1 Series M Coupe is not a rebadged M135i or M2; it is
represented once under its model family. Z4 is distinct from the existing
Z3 M Coupe. Only one original F87 M2 is selected; Competition and CS are
not additional records.

Audi's R8 and original quattro do not cover the RS2, RS4, RS6 or TT families.
Audi/Porsche/Avant RS2 are aliases of one car, stored once under Audi. One
B5 RS4 is selected. RS6 C5 and C6 are separate generations, without plus,
extra body styles or racing derivatives. Each of the TT's three generations
receives one road-coupe reference; 8N quattro Sport, V6, Roadster and 8J RS
plus are not extra entries.

Porsche's existing 924, 928 and 944 do not cover the 968. The original
914/6 is absent and distinct from 914/6 GT, 916 and eight-cylinder prototypes.
The existing 718 Boxster Spyder RS belongs to generation 982, not the original
986 Boxster. Existing 718 Cayman GT4 RS is also generation 982; 987 and 981
are absent. Only one Cayman reference per missing generation is selected.
No ordinary variants or cosmetic editions are used to multiply the count.

## Technical evidence and qualifications

### BMW

BMW M's own historical portraits provide the selected engineering details.
The E36 record uses the European S50B32 3.2 after the autumn 1995 update.
Its native 321-hp English notation remains qualified rather than converted
into an assumed SAE or metric rating. Earlier 3.0 and North American engines,
GT special edition, Compact prototype and optional SMG are excluded. The
explicit six-speed manual description is used; a contradictory translated
five-step phrase is not a second gearbox specification.

The E92 uses the 309-kW S65 V8 and standard coupe carbon roof. The article
also describes later DCT acceleration and optional higher speed limiters;
neither is applied to the original 2007 reference. A reliable selected
gearbox/rev-limit figure is not inferred. No claim is made that it uses a
Formula 1 engine rather than a production engine inspired by racing practice.

The 1M uses 250 kW, 450 Nm and a separately labelled temporary 500-Nm
overboost. Conflicting historical 0–200 claims are omitted. The original M2
uses 272 kW, 465 Nm and separately labelled 500-Nm overboost. Its standard
manual and rear drive are explicit; 4.3-second acceleration requires optional
DCT/Launch Control, and 270 km/h requires the Driver Package, so neither is
standardised. M2 Competition/CS figures are excluded.

The Z4 M uses the production coupe's 3246 cc, 252 kW, 365 Nm and stated
road performance. The European-formatted capacity is interpreted as 3246 cc.
Roadster, 3.0si and 430-hp GT3 race-car data are excluded. Undefined masses
and gearbox details absent from the selected account remain unfilled.

### Audi

Porsche's co-developer history supplies the RS2's 2.2-litre five-cylinder,
turbocharger, all-wheel drive, 410 Nm and road performance. Audi's own
five-cylinder history separately supplies 232 kW. The original collaboration
and significance are documented without duplicating the car under Porsche.

Audi Tradition's 2025 anniversary publication page 6 was read, rendered and
visually inspected for the B5 RS4. It supplies the Cosworth-modified 2.7-litre
V6, 280 kW, five valves per cylinder, twin turbochargers, 18-inch wheels and
lowered standard chassis. The optional 2001 sport package is excluded.
No exact capacity, gearbox or torque is inferred from another RS4 generation.

Audi's RS6 generation history separates the C5's 450 PS/560 Nm twin-turbo V8
from the C6's 580 PS/650 Nm dry-sump V10. Native PS is retained. The C5
record does not turn the account's five driving modes into a gearbox gear
count. The C6 explicitly has a six-speed automatic. Avant acceleration is
4.6 seconds, not the saloon's 4.5 seconds. Optional higher limiters, ceramics,
adjustable DRC, plus models and manual racing cars are excluded. Engine-only
mass is not treated as vehicle mass.

The 8N TT uses Audi UK's original March 2005 model-year price/specification
guide. PDF pages 3–6 were read, and the technical table on page 4 rendered
and visually inspected. The 225-PS manual quattro coupe inherits the stated
180-quattro equipment, including lowered suspension and 18-inch wheels;
240-PS quattro Sport, V6, DSG and Roadster data are excluded. Weight given
for emissions calculation includes a 75-kg driver and is not curb mass.
Audi's own 25-year history independently supports design significance and
the turbocharged four-cylinder identity. Exact capacity and torque are
withheld in this selected reference.

Audi's current five-cylinder history distinguishes the 2009 250-kW 8J engine
from the 2016 2480-cc, 294-kW/480-Nm lightweight evolution used by the 8S.
The 8J's direct injection and transverse layout are explicit. A retired
2021 PDF endpoint returned 404, so the 8S is sourced to the reachable current
Audi history instead. Injection/intercooler details are withheld in that
selected transcription. Gearbox, optional speed limiters and unsupported
acceleration remain unfilled; later RS3 500-Nm output is not imported.

### Porsche

The 968 CS historical section of Porsche's 718 T press kit, page 14, was
read, rendered and inspected. It supplies the naturally aspirated 3.0-litre
four, four-valve layout, VarioCam, 240 PS, 17-inch wheels, lowered chassis
and performance. Comparative weight reduction is not a generic curb mass;
standard 968 comfort equipment and Turbo RS race-car details are excluded.

Porsche Museum's 2019 914 history pages 2–4 distinguish the six-cylinder road
car from four-cylinder, GT, 916 and prototype variants. Page 3 was rendered
and inspected. The road reference uses 110 PS at 5800 rpm, two-litre boxer,
overhead camshafts, carburettor induction and capacitor ignition. The
translation's triple-carburettor wording is not used as a count claim. Dry
mass is not curb mass; race power and gearing are excluded.

Porsche's Boxster history explicitly separates the original 1996 2.5-litre,
150-kW water-cooled flat-six from later 2.7, 3.2 S and subsequent generations.
Selected technical fields include mid-engine position, four-valve layout and
variable intake timing. Unverified gearbox and numerical performance remain
unfilled.

The Cayman R uses Porsche's November 2010 global release for the February
2011 introduction: 243 kW, manual six-speed, rear drive and manual-specific
performance. PDK, Sport Chrono acceleration and optional lithium battery are
excluded. The USA-hosted global release's metric power is not reinterpreted
as a North American SAE specification.

The 981 GT4 uses Porsche's February 2015 road-car launch: 3.8-litre flat-six,
283 kW, six-speed manual, lowered GT3-derived chassis and downforce package.
The Clubsport racer has a different transmission and is excluded, along with
later 982 4.0/GT4 RS figures and optional ceramic brakes or seating packages.

## Photographs and held research

Every selected photograph was visually checked. Search results initially
included Z3s in a Z4 search, race-prepared M3s, TT RS plus models and 914 GT
racers; these are not used as the selected road references. The E36 photo is
Australian and spans the update period. The 8N photo does not authenticate
UK 225-PS equipment. The 8S photo identifies a 2018 pre-facelift coupe. The
RS6 C5 has accessory wheels and does not certify standard/plus identity.
The Boxster's museum-labelled 1997 exterior has modified headlamp appearance.
All these limits remain in gallery captions. Photos do not certify original
engines, gearboxes, optional equipment or exact build markets. Creators,
licences, source links and unchanged licensed image bytes are retained.

The B7 RS4 remains outside the import. Manufacturer history supports its
significance and engine family, and suitable road photos were reviewed, but
the matching original technical PDF could not be retrieved. Indexed fragments
and a Cabriolet sheet are not combined into an invented saloon-market
reference. It is a research candidate, not a catalogue addition.
