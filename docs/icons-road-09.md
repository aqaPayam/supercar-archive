## Duplicate review

The full 484-car inventory was checked for models, generations and aliases.
No MX-5 / Miata / Eunos Roadster record existed; one reference represents each
of its four substantially different generations. Additional trims, roofs,
facelifts and anniversary colours do not become separate entries. Cosmo Sport
is distinct from RX-7, RX-8 and the later luxury Eunos Cosmo. RX-3 / Savanna is
distinct from its RX-7 successor. Autozam AZ-1 is one Mazda record, not an extra
record under each Mazda/Autozam alias or the related Suzuki Cara name.

No GTO / 3000GT, Starion, FTO, S800 or CR-X existed. Toyota's Sports 800 is
distinct from Honda's S800. A60 Celica XX and Celica Supra are aliases for one
selected generation, while A70 is distinct from the existing A80 Supra. The
new GDB STi is distinct from the existing GC8-based 22B. No extra same-generation
derivatives or dedicated competition cars are included.

## Evidence boundaries

- NA: original US 1990 Mazda brochure, printed July 1989, PDF page 9 / printed
  pages 15–16. Its 1597-cc, 116 SAE-net-hp, five-speed specification applies.
  Optional viscous LSD, power steering, alloys and hardtop are not standardised.
- NB: Mazda's January 1998 chronology gives the original Japanese 1.8 DOHC,
  145 net PS and six-speed option. The contemporary annual report explains its
  second-generation development and MAGMA structure. Later variable-valve,
  Mazdaspeed turbo, US output and anniversary-specific equipment are excluded.
- NC: Mazda's August 2005 launch release distinguishes base five-speed from
  six-speed RS and the automatic. Manual 125 kW / 170 PS is used, not automatic
  122 kW / 166 PS or US output. Ambiguous mass/tyre column spans are omitted.
- ND: the May 2015 launch release identifies the Japanese base S, 1.5 direct
  injection, six-speed, rear drive and soft-top. Optional equipment and later
  RF, NR-A, KPC and export 2.0-litre power are not imported into this reference.
- Cosmo: the National Museum of Nature and Science's 1967 record supplies
  the original 10A, 110-PS and dimensional references. Mazda's history supports
  significance and distinguishes the following year's 128-PS version. Undefined
  mass and historical torque convention are withheld. A misleadingly named
  1967 photo whose description identifies L10B was rejected for an L10A image.
- RX-3: the original English-language brochure A47111T 102-358 was rendered
  and the table on PDF page 3 inspected. It identifies S102A and 491 cc per
  rotor, four-speed, four-barrel, chassis and separate coupe/sedan dimensions.
  Despite its host filename, the brochure does not clearly establish a US
  market; imperial gallons and local-equipment wording are preserved as a
  qualification. Native SAE 110 HP has no stated net/gross basis and is not
  converted. Later 12A/1146-cc road data and race-car power are not mixed in.
  A 1975 US government engine test was researched but is a different engine
  reference and is excluded. Mazda's own Savanna history supports the road
  family's motorsport significance without transferring competition specs.
- AZ-1: Mazda's chronology explicitly supplies 64 net PS, midship layout,
  DOHC three-cylinder, intercooler turbo, gullwing doors and plastic panels.
  Rounded 660 cc is a displacement class; the panels are not described as
  a plastic structural monocoque. No Cara or special body-kit duplicate is added.
- GTO: Mitsubishi's original-model history supports the twin-turbo 3.0 V6,
  Getrag five-speed, full-time AWD, 45:55 split and active chassis/aero. Naturally
  aspirated, later six-speed, MR and related Dodge Stealth values are excluded.
- Starion: only original narrow-body Japanese 2.0 turbo dimensions and chassis
  are used. Later intercooled power, widebody, 2.6-litre and 4WD prototype
  specifications are excluded. No unsupported exact output is invented.
- FTO: Mitsubishi's original 1994 history supports the 2.0 MIVEC V6 and INVECS-II
  automatic, adaptive learning and manual selection. Unsupported exact engine
  capacity, power and ratio count remain unfilled. No non-MIVEC or later GP
  Version R equipment is substituted; the award is context, not its only merit.
- S800: Collection Hall's live-axle technical record is paired with Honda's
  history, which explicitly dates the shaft-drive change to May 1966. The
  initial chain-drive configuration and later S800M brake equipment are not
  combined with it. Undefined mass and modern net-power conversion are omitted.
- CR-X: the September 1989 Honda release supplies the SiR's VTEC, nominal
  displacement, power and specific brakes/tyres/spoiler. Optional LSD and ALB
  are not standard. European output, North American Si and later del Sol data
  are excluded; unstated exact displacement, ratios and rating basis are withheld.
- A70: Toyota's February 1986 release explicitly specifies 7M-GTEU, 2954 cc,
  230 net PS and double wishbones. Later 1JZ, 1G twin-turbo, Turbo A and US
  power do not apply. ABS is optional; gearbox and rear-disc variants are omitted.
- A60: Toyota's lineage table's original GT_2800 column supplies 5M-GEU,
  2759 cc and dimensions. Its retrospective generic net-rating footnote merits
  caution for an early Japanese model; native 170 PS is qualified rather than
  converted. Later engines, optional navigation and US trim values are excluded.
- GDB: Subaru's own September 2001 grade catalogue supports GH-GDB, 1994 cc,
  206 kW, 373 Nm, six-speed and full-time AWD. Its history separately supports
  chassis development. No RA, DCCD, Prodrive Style, S202, later twin-scroll or
  export 2.5-litre specification is assumed. HID and rear spoiler stay optional.

Calculated net metric conversions use 0.73549875 kW per PS: NB 145 PS =
106.65 kW; AZ-1 64 PS = 47.07 kW. A70 230 net PS = 169.16 kW. NC and Subaru
use their manufacturer's direct kW values. Unqualified historical output stays
in native notation, with uncertainty visible in the specification boundary.

All chosen photos were visually checked. Wrong-model, modified-body, interior
and wrong-series alternatives were rejected. The early European 2.0 ND and
US narrow-body Starion are explicitly identified in public captions as exterior
generation references, not evidence for Japanese engine specifications. Later
accessory wheels are noted on the Cosmo. Photos do not certify original engines,
options or exact reference-year build. Creator, licence and source links are
preserved with unchanged image bytes. Password-protected archives were not bypassed.
