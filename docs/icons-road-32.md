## Identity and selection

The complete 645-car inventory was checked for 117/PA90, Celica/TA22/A20,
C110/KPGC110/Kenmeri, Prince/S54/2000GT and Charade/G100/GTti aliases, and
Bellett/PR91W/GT-R. No selected model/generation family was
present. The only S54 search hit was BMW's unrelated engine. Existing C10,
R30/R31/R34 and later GT-R records do not cover C110 or Prince's S54 generation;
existing Celicas cover later GT-Four generations. Manufacturer changes do not
make the same Skyline generation a second car.

One original family per entry is counted. Extra reference years, facelifts,
engine alternatives, rebadges, transmissions, race replicas and dedicated racers
do not multiply this batch. Pending extra-variant/racer boundaries remain pending.

## Technical evidence

Exact source and field mappings, public admission reasons and media provenance
are in [data/icons-road-32.json](../data/icons-road-32.json).

Isuzu uses its own Plaza exhibition page's 1968 117 Coupe table: G161W
water-cooled inline-four DOHC, 1584 cc, native 88.0 kW/120 PS at 6400 rpm,
four seats and 4280/1600/1320-mm dimensions. Its account supports Giugiaro/Ghia,
early manual construction and 2014 Japanese automotive hall-of-fame recognition.
The English weight label says gross vehicle weight; it is not silently
relabelled as curb mass, and no weight is imported. Display-versus-picture
qualifications are respected. Later engine sizes, fuel injection, diesel,
XC/XE/XG and square-headlamp specifications are excluded. No unprinted gearbox
or museum car's current mechanical originality is invented.

Toyota uses its 75 Years lineage table, released 1 December 1970, selecting
only the 1600 DOHC GT column: water-cooled 2T-G four, 1588 cc, 115 PS at
6400 rpm, 940-kg curb mass, 4165/1600/1310-mm dimensions and 2425-mm wheelbase.
The table abbreviates its GT model type to TA-MQ; the accompanying note identifies
1600 models as TA22. No unprinted full suffix or gearbox is invented from a
range-wide list of transmission alternatives. Native PS is retained without
inventing a kW or period gross/net conversion from the page's generic net-output
footnote. Lower-grade OHV and later Liftback/2000 figures are excluded.
The admission fact's source covers the specialty-car design and full-choice
system. A separate public fact links Toyota's motorsport chronology for the
1972 RAC class victory and European endurance lineage. Racing specifications,
an overall RAC victory, and later GT-Four achievements are not assigned to this
road car.

KPGC110 uses Nissan road collection No.069: S20 inline-six DOHC with four
valves per cylinder, 1989 cc, native 118 kW/160 PS at 7000 rpm, 177 Nm at
5600 rpm, independent strut/semi-trailing-arm suspension, four discs,
175HR14 tyres and 1145-kg curb weight. Road dimensions are 4460/1695/1380 mm
and 2610-mm wheelbase. No rating standard is invented. Nissan explicitly
states that this generation did not participate in touring-car races. Its
short production and Ken-and-Mary/Phantom identity do not transfer earlier
C10 wins, the 1972 green race concept, or restored demonstration specifications
to the production record. The source says fewer than 200; no unsupported exact
total is inserted merely to amplify rarity.

Prince uses Nissan Heritage No.291, explicitly the 1965 S54B-2 road 2000GT:
G7 overhead-cam inline-six with triple carburettors, 1988 cc, native 92 kW/125 PS
at 5600 rpm, 167 Nm at 4400 rpm, double-wishbone/parallel-leaf suspension,
front discs/rear drums, 5.60-13-6PR tyres, 1070-kg curb weight and the archive's
180-km/h road claim. An unrelated Caball image-alt label is not taken as car
identity; the model heading and technical table identify the reference.
The manufacturer's separate Grand Prix story supports the long-nose six-cylinder
S54 and its 1964 challenge, with places second through sixth behind a Porsche
904. It explains family relevance without claiming this 1965 road car raced
or won overall. No unprinted gearbox or race tune is invented; later GT-B
revisions and replicas are not extra entries.

Bellett uses original Isuzu/JAF T-127, FIA1604 Group II, valid from 1 July
1970. The complete 16-page file includes the original fourteen-page base form
and a two-page later bodywork evolution; an indexed amendment snippet alone
was insufficient to assess its contents. All pages were rendered in a contact
sheet; base PDF1, 4–7, 9 and 14 read separately. The cover declares manufacture
starting August 1969 and 500 identical cars by January 1970; these are the
manufacturer's declarations rather than a public-launch date or total lifetime
production figure. The base establishes 1584 cc, cast-iron block/alloy head,
10.3:1 compression, two chain-driven overhead cams, two valves per cylinder,
twin Mikuni/Solex side-draught carburettors and four synchronised manual ratios.
Native declared 120 PS JIS at 6400 rpm, 14.5 kg-m at 5000 rpm and 200-km/h catalogue
claim are retained without a modern-net conversion. Front independent wishbones
use coils; the rear suspension is hand-corrected to independent swing axle,
not the struck-through diagonal-link description, with coils and transverse leaf.
Hydraulic vacuum-assisted front discs/rear drums are retained. Alternate ratios,
later grille/lamp evolution and competition tune are excluded.

Isuzu's separately rendered one-page exhibition sheet documents a 1970 GT-R
racer and the GT-X prototype's 1969 Suzuka twelve-hour victory. A separate
public fact identifies this as competition lineage, without assigning prototype
results or racing specifications to the road coupe. The original range-brochure
forum was inaccessible; the complete readable manufacturer/JAF base form is
the technical evidence route that resolves the earlier research hold.

Charade uses an original Dutch Daihatsu Holland B.V., Rotterdam, range brochure
hosted by PakWheels as 1992. No legible print date authenticates that host year.
The year 1987 in the profile describes the family independently documented
by Bill Boddy's September 1987 first-hand drive, not the brochure's publication
date. All 16 PDF pages were rendered; GTti presentation PDF5, chassis PDF14,
technical column PDF15 and importer footer PDF16 were read individually.
The selected GTti column establishes 993-cc DOHC three-cylinder, 74 kW/101 PK
at 6500 rpm, 130 Nm at 3500 rpm, 7.8:1 compression, five-speed manual, five seats,
835-kg unladen weight and 234-cm wheelbase. The same brochure documents twelve
valves, electronic injection/ignition, intercooler, independent MacPherson
suspension and four discs, with factory claims of 185 km/h and 0–100 in 8.2 s.
The generic text's other high-rpm remark does not override the explicit power
peak in the GTti column. Diesel/basic and automatic columns are excluded.

The contemporary Motor Sport assessment is primary first-hand evidence of the
GTti's technical merit and road performance; its online republication date is
2014. Its UK output, 0–60 sticker claim, test maxima and measured consumption
do not become Dutch factory fields. A separate FIA A-5344 file was located and
rendered in a first-20-page contact sheet for research, but its Japanese GT,
physical 993.5-cc capacity, 1.7 turbo-equivalence, options and racing extensions
are not mixed into the selected 993-cc Dutch brochure reference. An AutoWeek
facsimile could be text-read but returned access/render errors; it is not the
final technical evidence route. No restriction was bypassed.

## Photography

All six selected Commons images were visually reviewed. Original downloaded
bytes remain unchanged; creator, licence URL, source page, caption and SHA-256
are recorded. Images represent road families and do not authenticate installed
engines, exact market output or restored mechanical originality.

D.Bellwood's CC BY-SA 3.0 early 117 at Toyota Automobile Museum shows the
round-headlamp road coupe with standard-style body; exact year and hand-built
originality are not inferred. A bonnet-up event car and engine-only close-up
were rejected. Kieran White's CC BY 2.0 1972 Celica 1600 GT shows the early
hardtop and GT badge, without certifying the 1970 Japan tune. A wide-bodied
modified show car was rejected; a restored red GT was considered but not chosen.

The CC0 white KPGC110 by 先従隗始 at Prince and Skyline Museum shows the road
GT-R front/body; it is not the green race concept. Detail-only grille/interior
views were rejected, and a modified road car does not authenticate original tune.
The same photographer's CC0 1966 S54B-3 GT-B at that museum represents the later
revision of Prince's original road-saloon family; its trim is not called 1965
S54B-2 factory trim. Displayed 1964 Grand Prix replicas were excluded.
Rutger van der Maar's CC BY 2.0 1989 GTti, XF-47-DX, at Japan Classic Sunday
2022 shows the Dutch three-door road body and turbo/twelve-valve markings,
without assigning a verified brochure year or current engine output.
sv1ambo's CC BY 2.0 1970 Bellett GT-R at the 2011 Shannons Eastern Creek Classic
shows the orange road coupe with black bonnet and DOHC/GT-R markings. Current
wheels, auxiliary lamps and finish do not certify original internals or the
selected JIS rating. The GT-X prototype and museum racer are not the asset.
Original manufacturer and FIA images are not treated as freely licensed assets.
