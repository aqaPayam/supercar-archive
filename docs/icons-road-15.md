Reviewed 2026-10-29 against 549 existing records. Eleven missing production
road references bring the raw catalogue to 560. The goal remains 1,000
qualifying cars; the baseline eligibility audit is still outstanding.

## Identity and duplicate review

The complete catalogue, alternate manufacturer/model wording and preceding
manifests were checked. Existing Ferrari 250 GTO, 330 GTC and Daytona do not
cover 166 Inter or 275 GTB4. No separate 275 two-cam, GTS, NART Spyder or
competition car is added. The Dino 308 GT4 is the Bertone 2+2 with a distinct
body/chassis concept, not the existing two-seat Pininfarina 308 GTB/GTS.
It is stored once under Ferrari; Dino badging does not create a second car.
The FF family is absent; no GTC4Lusso, Lusso T or extra body style is added.

Maserati's existing six-cylinder 3500 GT is not the 5000 GT V8 flagship.
Only one 5000 GT reference represents its varied coachbuilt production.
Merak is the separately named AM122 V6 2+2, not an extra engine tune of the
AM117 two-seat Bora. No Merak SS or 2000GT is added. One original Biturbo
represents the early family; S, i, Si, export engines, Spyder, saloons and
later short-wheelbase derivatives do not increase this batch's count.

The 3200 GT is a missing later model, represented by its manual reference.
There is no additional automatic, Assetto Corsa or 4200 entry. Quattroporte
I and V are absent, distinct generations separated by decades of chassis,
engine and body development. Ordinary intervening generations are not
added simply to complete a model list. One original two-seat MC Stradale
represents the missing M145 GranTurismo generation; standard, S, later
four-seat updates, US MC and race versions are not separate additions.

## Evidence and specification limits

Ferrari's original 275 GTB4 brochure page 5 was rendered and visually read.
It explicitly supplies 3285.722 cc, four overhead camshafts, dry sump, six
Weber 40 DCN 9 carburettors, rear-mounted five-speed, tubular chassis and
independent suspension. Native Italian 300 CV is retained with its historical
rating qualification; the English 300-bhp translation is not a modern net
conversion. Empty weight and speeds calculated from gearing are omitted.
The Design Museum independently documents a 1967 road GTB4 exhibit and the
Ferrari-Pininfarina design partnership.

Louwman Museum's own 1949 166 Inter collection record supplies the Touring
Aerlux body, Plexiglas roof, aluminium/Superleggera structure and Colombo
2.0-litre V12. Ferrari's official prospectus, printed page 94 / PDF page 106,
was rendered and checked for the 166 Inter's place at the start of the road
car business in 1948. Family introduction is separate from this car's 1949
build year. No 166 MM output, race tune or power from a photo filename is used.

The Hellenic Motor Museum's own 1974 GT4 record supplies the rounded 2927-cc
capacity, mid-V8, native 255-bhp notation, power peak and Bertone significance.
It is explicitly a museum-car reference, without an invented original build
market. An archive-hosted file labelled 1973 instead contains an undated US
Dino brochure with a different 240-SAE-net specification. That document was
rendered and read but its values are not mixed into the 1974 museum reference.
No gearbox, fuel system or valve layout is borrowed from that US sheet.

Both pages of Ferrari's January 21, 2011 FF launch release were rendered
and visually checked. Its original European reference explicitly gives
6262 cc, 660 CV, 683 Nm, 4RM, direct injection, dual-clutch transaxle, SCM3
damping and carbon-ceramic brakes. The selected release does not state a
gear count. Dry weight and HELE-dependent consumption are not generalised.
The official prospectus's printed page 109 / PDF page 121 independently
corroborates the 2011 first-four-wheel-drive milestone.

For 5000 GT, Maserati's November 2019 anniversary release distinguishes the
original 1959 4937.8-cc/325-HP engine and four-speed from the 1960 engine
revision and 1963 five-speed. Only the original reference is imported.
The generic archive's later brake description is not combined with the
original table. Dry mass and unsupported modern power conversions are omitted.

Maserati's historical pages distinguish the original 2965-cc Merak from
SS and 2000GT, and original 1996-cc Biturbo from S and fuel-injected i/Si.
The Merak page's questionable oil-crisis launch chronology is not repeated.
Biturbo production is dated 1982 in the selected table, following the
December 1981 introduction. Unverified transmission and torque stay unfilled.

The 3200 GT page contains apparent errors: 370 km/h maximum speed,
contradictory translated power-peak speeds and unrelated automatic-column
years. Those fields are omitted; its documented original manual, capacity,
engine layout, chassis and design are retained. The archive's broad claim
that these were the first LED lights on any production car is not repeated.
No specific global lighting-first claim is necessary for admission.

For Quattroporte I, only the first-series 4.2 column is used. The 4.7 and
Series II are excluded, along with conflicting speed statements, dry mass
and unsupported global-first/fastest superlatives. The separate 60-year
manufacturer history supports the October 1963 debut. For Quattroporte V,
only the original dry-sump DuoSelect/transaxle reference is used; later
front-transmission Automatica, 4.7 and sixth-generation twin-turbo data are
excluded. Historical English hp retains its unspecified rating convention.

Maserati SpA's September 30 / October 1, 2010 launch release, distributed
through PR Newswire, supplies the 2011 MC Stradale's 330 kW, 510 Nm,
electro-actuated MC Race Shift transaxle, two seats and carbon-ceramic brakes.
It explicitly excludes USA, Canada and Korea for this configuration. The
later four-seat 460-PS version and actual Trofeo/GT4 racers are not imported.
Undefined mass is not treated as curb weight.

## Photography and held research

All eleven selected photographs were visually inspected. The 166 photo is
of the Louwman collection car. The early Touring 5000 GT photo is identified
as 1960 chassis 103.004, so it illustrates the Touring body rather than
certifying the first 1959 car's engine. The 308 GT4 image does not certify
the museum car or original Dino badging. The early Biturbo image is a 1983
coupe without the S-style hood ducts, but does not authenticate Italian 2.0
engine specification. The Quattroporte I photo identifies a 1964 first-series
museum car; the V photo identifies a 2005 car at the 2004 San Francisco show.

Wrapped MC Stradale, later/export Biturbo examples, detail-only views and
historic race/start-grid photographs were rejected. Gallery captions retain
year/market, interior and original-powertrain limits. Creators, licences,
source links and unchanged licensed image bytes are preserved.

The 250 GT SWB remains research only. A file labelled 1961 250GT proved to
be a different long-wheelbase road coupe brochure, and is not used to invent
SWB technical data or as a reason to add a different car. Factory-web access
challenges were not bypassed. Original documents and museum collection
records provided the selected Ferrari evidence instead. Additional same-
generation variants and dedicated racers/prototypes remain pending.
