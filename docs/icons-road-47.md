## Identity and road-use boundaries

All seven families were absent. Existing Bugattis cover EB110 and modern W16
programmes; neither Type 55 nor Type 57 is represented. The prewar 8C 2900
is distinct from the modern 8C Competizione. Bentley's later R-Type, Turbo R
and Continental GT do not cover the Blower. Talbot-Lago's prewar T150 is not
the existing Talbot Sunbeam Lotus; Honda Accord name matches are not Cord cars.

No extra 852/812 model year, coachwork variation, prototype, Grand Prix car,
Birkin racer, Le Mans Speciale or later supercharger state adds another count.
An existing road car's competition use explains significance without turning
race-car hardware into standard road specification. The selected references
describe historical road programmes, not universal present registration status.

## Primary evidence and limits

- **Auburn:** Use the museum's own 1935 Montagu car: native bhp/mph and side
  valves. No inferred inline layout, gearbox, production total or guarantee of
  untouched originality. The custodian's period fast-touring quotation supports
  the performance admission; price and rarity do not substitute for it.
- **Alfa:** Read the complete factory museum technical catalogue. The road
  Lungo uses its quoted 180-hp, twin-supercharged architecture. Milan collection
  D-1974, chassis 412035, independently documents a Touring Berlinetta first
  registered on 23 July 1938. It is not asserted to be the same individual car
  as Arese's reference. No Le Mans engine, race body, unclear production total
  or individual restoration-provenance claim is transferred.
- **Atlantic:** The 2019 factory account explicitly says the first 1936
  Rothschild car was delivered without a supercharger. Do not backdate SC
  equipment or its up-to-200-PS family figure. Production aluminium is separate
  from the Aérolithe prototype's Elektron. Omit anomalous length, unprinted
  gearbox, speculative individual identity and modern ownership/value claims.
- **Type 55:** The 2021 factory history explicitly establishes the eight-cylinder
  supercharged 2.3-litre road programme. The 2019 account supplies road design
  and historical speed; the 2015 account supplies component genealogy only.
  Its erroneous “ten-cylinder” wording is excluded, as are adjacent Type 57,
  Type 35 and Veyron specifications. No inferred ratios or universal race tune.
- **Blower:** Select the custodian's 1931 MS3926 original-body road tourer and
  original D-type gearbox. Its quoted 182 hp is explicitly a collection-car
  quotation, not a universal factory rating. Beaulieu's different rebodied
  example and malformed rpm field, its 175-bhp reference, Birkin 240-bhp racing
  tune and modern Continuation cars are not the selected technical reference.
- **Cord:** Select Simeone's own chassis 8121446F with its older restoration.
  The article discusses conflicting horsepower estimates, so output is omitted.
  The Bonneville record sedan was a different car; its measured speeds, body
  and presumed tune are not this cabriolet's performance. No gearbox speed
  count or standard non-supercharged 125-hp reference is inferred.
- **Talbot-Lago:** Louwman's 1937 car has documented private prewar use and
  quoted 160 hp. Frist's separate curatorial account establishes SS-family
  independent front suspension and hemispherical head architecture. It does
  not prove that Louwman's car has optional triple carburettors, a 170-bhp
  tune or a Wilson gearbox. No extra unique coachwork or later T26 is added.

## Licensed representative photographs

All seven photographs were visually inspected and copied unchanged. Each
Commons page retains creator, precise licence version and URL, original credit,
qualified caption and SHA-256 in the manifest. The Cord's downloaded PNG remains
a PNG; no file is relabelled as JPEG or locally edited.

The Auburn and Cord show their referenced museums' cars. The Alfa illustrates
the road Lungo, not the Le Mans body. The blue Atlantic's later display is not
proof of its original engine state or exact chassis identity. The Type 55 is
not asserted to be factory-history chassis 55215. The Bentley photograph shows
a different Beaulieu road-family example, not Simeone MS3926 or its original
coachwork. No restoration or measured power claim comes from appearance alone.

## Held candidate: Duesenberg SSJ

The SSJ has a strong performance-icon case, supported by its former custodian's
historical accounts, but no suitable reusable photograph is established in this
batch. Commons results include an explicitly identified hand-built replica and
a different 1931 Duesenberg once owned by Gary Cooper. Genuine SSJ photographs
on the checked photographer's Flickr pages have no Creative Commons grant.
None is imported or used to stand in for the SSJ. Its research does not count
as a catalogue addition. The 1950s engine modifications mentioned by the former
custodian also require care before presenting original output or condition.
