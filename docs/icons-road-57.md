and variant, including E89/G29, R170/R172, SLK and SLC aliases. The archive already

The four selected generations were absent. SLC is the later R172 rename, not a
fourth SLK generation. Shared Z4/Supra architecture is acknowledged rather than

## Technical evidence boundaries

The E89 uses the original December 2008 global release and UK launch account for
May 2009 introduction. Native 225 kW/400 Nm, 2979 cc and the standard six-speed
manual are preserved. A later 35is, US horsepower notation, optional seven-speed
DCT and optional Adaptive M Suspension are not mixed into the reference. The
roof milestone is explicitly BMW's first retractable-hardtop roadster, not the
world's first such car. The engineering case does not imply it was the sharpest
handling car in its class.

The G29 uses the original September 2018 European release for March 2019
introduction: 250-kW six-cylinder M40i, eight-speed automatic, fabric roof and
standard M Sport differential/brakes and Adaptive Sport suspension. The
contemporary first-hand review distinguishes the production European M40i drive
from prototype impressions and US four-cylinder measured results. It supports
the engine/steering/handling assessment and rear-wheel drive without importing
US 382-hp output, US 2020 model-year convention or estimated acceleration.
First Edition, facelift and later manual package do not add entries.

The SLK 32 uses page 3 of the factory technical sheet printed September 2003,
read both as extracted text and as a rendered page. Its V6, supercharger,
3199 cc, 260 kW, 450 Nm and five-speed automatic belong to its own column.
The generic brake-construction text conflicts with the dimensional row; brakes
are therefore omitted. A separately read/rendered factory history supplies the
original R170's Vario-roof significance and AMG flagship position. Its printed
date is 14 April 2016, despite the hosting filename. Family-wide production
figures are not AMG totals. The selected 2003 reference is not an introduction
claim, and no universal retractable-hardtop first is asserted.

The R172 SLK 55 uses Mercedes-Benz Netherlands' 21 August 2011 release for
Frankfurt unveiling. Its 2011 reference denotes announcement, not an inferred
local delivery date or US model year. Naturally aspirated 5.5-litre V8, native
310 kW/540 Nm, direct injection, cylinder deactivation and seven-speed automatic
are distinct from R171 hardware. The Handling Package, mechanical limited-slip
differential and composite front brakes are optional; brake-based torque
vectoring is not that optional mechanical differential. The factory retrospective
also supports the later SLC rename. No SLC 43 V6 specification is transferred.

Direct downloads of the BMW article URLs currently redirect to the modern
newsroom homepage; those saved responses contain no historical specifications
and were not used. The public web reader returned the original complete factory
articles. Mercedes' direct-download TLS-chain failure was not bypassed; its
public factory release was read through the web reader. The original club-hosted
factory PDFs downloaded with verified TLS. No access controls were bypassed.
