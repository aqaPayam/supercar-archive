## Identity and duplicates

The complete inventory and previous manifests were checked, including Jaguar
SS/SS100, Bentley Continental, Mercedes W29/500 K/540 K and Ferrari Lusso aliases.
There was no Bentley record and none of these four selected road identities.
The SS100 precedes the existing XK120/E-Type; the original Continental GT is a
new model identity. The W29 is distinct from the archive's later 300 SL and
sporting saloons. The Lusso is a road grand tourer distinct from the existing
competition-derived 250 GTO; a shared 250-series label does not make their body,
chassis purpose and model identity interchangeable.

One road reference is counted for each. Later SS100 3½ Litre, Continental
Speed/Supersports/GTC/Mulliner equipment and newer generations, other W29 bodies
and Ferrari SWB/GTO variants do not multiply this batch's count. No SSK is
imported while the road/race and representative-body questions remain unresolved.

## Technical evidence

Complete field/source mappings and public admission facts are saved in
[data/icons-road-21.json](../data/icons-road-21.json).

The SS100 uses the Jaguar Daimler Heritage Trust's account of its own running
vehicle, ERB 290 / chassis 49033, built January 20, 1938. The original 102-bhp
OHV six with twin SU carburettors and 151-km/h background specification is
separate from the earlier SS90's 70-bhp side-valve engine and later 125-bhp
3½ Litre. The nominal 2½ Litre model designation is labelled as such; it is not
converted into a false exact 2500-cc claim. Restoration and competition history
are documented without claiming the current engine has been measured by us.

The Continental GT uses Bentley's September 9, 2003 Frankfurt production
launch release reproduced by Automotive Intelligence on September 17. It
supplies the W12, twin turbos, native 560 PS, 650 Nm, ZF six-speed automatic,
four-wheel drive, chassis, brakes and four seats. Rounded kW/bhp conventions
are not recomputed or mixed with early pre-production estimates. Bentley's
August 21, 2023 anniversary PDF independently identifies the original 2003
production car and the model's role in the marque's revival. PDF pages 1 and 3
were rendered and read. Only historical passages apply: current GT Speed
hardware, emissions footers and an unrelated historical date typo are excluded.

The 500 K uses the factory March 22, 2016 Techno Classica release, hosted by a
Mercedes-Benz club. Its dedicated Special Roadster technical section on page 6
was rendered/read: inline eight, 5018 cc, 74 kW without and 118 kW with the
supercharger, 160 km/h. These are two operating conditions, not competing power
claims. The museum's March 12, 2024 Close-up release, pages 1–2, documents the
October 1934 Special Roadster introduction, Friedrich Geiger design, Roots
engagement and front trapezoidal-link/rear swing-axle suspension; page 2 was
rendered/read. The family reference spans 1934–1936 and does not assign a
surviving chassis or claim the photograph is the museum car. Rumble-seat layout
precludes a strict two-seat claim. Adjacent Cabriolet B data, 540 K displacement
and conflicting body-production counts are excluded. The museum warns that
later conversions require expert body authentication; our photograph is
representative, without making that authentication claim.

The Lusso uses Gooding Christie's first-hand auction catalogue/consignor report
for 1963 chassis 4837 GT / engine 4837, Rétromobile Paris lot 17. Its specification
supplies 2953 cc, SOHC Tipo 168U V12, three Weber 36 DCS carburettors, four-speed
manual, Dunlop discs, front coils, rear live axle/leaf springs, body materials
and design/build attribution. The report states Classiche restoration and
certification in 2017; this is labelled auction documented, not independently
verified factory certification. English BHP/French ch notation, a questionable
copied acceleration figure and inconsistent production totals are omitted.
A separate museum webpage's all-round coil-spring table was rejected. The
Ferrari factory webpage presented a JavaScript verification challenge and was
not bypassed or treated as a reviewed source.

## Photography

All four selected images were visually reviewed. The SS100's ERB 290 registration
matches the heritage collection reference; its red paint is a later documented
restoration. The Bentley image identifies a 2006 Mulliner car and represents
the original body, without certifying 2003 trim, wheels or tune. A generic front
image with incomplete creator metadata was not selected. The Mercedes is an
exhibition Special Roadster body without chassis/year/original-body certification;
it is not presented as the factory museum car. The Ferrari depicts the Lusso
road body at an event without identifying it as auction chassis 4837 GT.

Captions preserve those limits. Creators, licences, source URLs and unchanged
image bytes are saved. All four images use Creative Commons licences; factory
press availability is not treated as an open image licence. Image downloading
stopped on a temporary rate limit during candidate review and resumed later
with only the required remaining file.
