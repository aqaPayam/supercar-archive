## Z/28 350 four-speed manual, 3.73 axle — February 1970 US factory reference

The second-generation Z/28 developed the Camaro’s performance identity with a high-compression 350-cubic-inch V8 and revised steering and road behaviour. Contemporary first-hand testing assessed it as a distinctive road GT while also recording its handling compromises. Count this absent Camaro generation once; engines, RS/SS appearance or performance packages and later years do not add entries.

1970 US second-generation Camaro Z/28, selected four-speed manual with factory 3.73 axle. Actual Chevrolet February 1970 Power Trains pages 2–6 and AMA 4B supply the reference: 350 cu in nominal, 11.0:1, cast-iron 90-degree OHV V8, four-barrel, 360 gross brake hp at 6000 rpm and 380 gross lb-ft at 4000 rpm. These are advertised gross ratings, not SAE net or wheel output. No inferred cc/kW/Nm conversion, specific Muncie ratio set, optional 4.10 axle, later detuned engine, race preparation or period-test performance figure is selected. The C/D test car was automatic and its published power-peak rpm and torque figures differ; those do not replace the factory reference.

## LS1 Z28 coupe, optional MM6 six-speed manual — 1998 US factory reference

The fourth-generation Camaro carried the affordable V8 pony-car line into its LS-engine era. The selected LS1 Z28 combines the Corvette-derived aluminium-block V8 with a six-speed manual, marking a substantial mechanical evolution within an established American performance family. Count this generation once; LT1/LS1 years, SS packages, transmissions, open-top bodies and anniversaries do not add separate entries.

1998 US-market fourth-generation Z28 coupe, optional MM6 six-speed manual, power-team D in the actual factory MVMA table. M30 automatic is standard in this table; the chosen manual is not mislabelled standard. Factory F-car LS1 data: 5665 cc, 10.1:1, 228 kW (305 bhp) at 5200 rpm and 454 Nm (335 lb-ft) at 4000 rpm, SAE J1349 net. Stated metric figures are retained without recalculation. Not the Corvette Y-car 345-hp/350-lb-ft calibration, base L36 V6, SS/1LE package, later-year output or 1970 gross rating. Model year 1998 is not the start of the fourth generation. Factory page 1 identifies Canadian assembly; origin denotes the US Chevrolet programme, not assembly location.

## Factory evidence actually inspected

Both original GM-hosted PDFs downloaded intact. The 1970 kit contains 138
pages (17,540,455 bytes); the 1998 kit contains 84 pages (2,866,971 bytes).
Discovery links led to GM's actual files. CamaroFacts' text and its claimed
page counts are not substituted for the original scans. Kits can combine
documents with different dates and authors; no whole-kit or final-production
certification is implied by the GM hosting. Only identified Chevrolet factory
pages supply imported fields. Appended third-party histories and option-count
tables do not become manufacturer evidence merely by appearing in a kit.

For 1970, actual full PDF indexes 29–33 (February 1970 Power Trains 2–6) and
58 (February 1970 AMA 4B) were read. The Z28 column explicitly identifies a
350-cu-in 90-degree OHV V8, four-barrel, 11:1 compression, cast-iron block and
heads, and nominal 4.00 × 3.48-in bore/stroke. The rating table labels the output
and torque GROSS: 360 brake hp at 6000 rpm and 380 lb-ft at 4000 rpm. Native
cubic inches are preserved; the period test's rounded 5740 cc is not adopted.
Four-speed combinations are documented, with 3.73 axle and required Positraction;
4.10 is an alternative and is not selected. No particular Muncie ratio set is
asserted. The factory tyre chart's F60 × 15B notation is preserved, not replaced
by the photographed car's modern radials. The performance-factor table defines
performance weight as kerb weight plus four passengers: it is not imported as
an unqualified kerb weight or acceleration measurement. Identification sheet
index 2 and other-engine AMA 4A at 57 were also viewed; incomplete basic-code
examples or other engine columns do not supply additional fields. Blank/contents
pages 27–28 were viewed but contribute no technical evidence.

The actual May 1970 C/D test was read. It supplies the admission assessment and
shared front-engine/rear-drive layout, while its qualifications about steering,
limit understeer and driver confidence are respected. No universal best-handling
or Ferrari-equivalence claim is repeated. Its selected car was automatic with a
4.10 axle; its 5600-rpm power peak, 370-lb-ft torque and measured performance do
not overwrite Chevrolet's manual/factory-rating reference. No race specification
or result is assigned to the selected road coupe.

For 1998, actual full indexes 28 (GM's LS1 engineering release), 31 (LS1 product
sheet), 32 (MVMA page 1), 37 (MVMA page 2), 39 (3A), 45 (6A) and 46 (8A) supply
the selected fields. LS1 F-car output is distinguished from Corvette Y-car
calibration and the separately printed L36 V6. The factory explicitly states
5665 cc, 10.1:1, aluminium block/heads, 99.0 × 92.0 mm, front-longitudinal OHV
layout, in-block chain-driven camshaft and sequential port injection. The MVMA
power table states SAE J1349 NET: 228 kW (305 bhp) at 5200 rpm and 454 Nm
(335 lb-ft) at 4000 rpm. These factory-stated metric figures are used without
recalculating the rounding. Power-team D uses the optional MM6 six-speed;
M30 automatic is standard in this table. The six-speed sheet identifies
Borg-Warner. Individual ratios, SS/1LE equipment, optional tyres, 345-hp Corvette
output, later-year ratings and unqualified performance claims are not imported.
The car is a US-market Chevrolet programme with Canadian assembly; these are
different concepts. Four seats and rear-wheel drive are explicit in page 1.

The 1998 contact sheets were navigation aids, not a full mechanical audit.
Other full pages viewed included cover/index/definitions, preliminary dealer
feature page 2, order-guide page 9 at index 14 (September 2, 1997 revision), V6
product/engine pages 25/34/36, LS1 exhaust page 44 and V6 exhaust page 47.
Their preliminary, optional, V6 or unselected data are not silently imported.
The actual independent fourth-generation retrospective was read for generation
history and performance context only; its quoted prices, modification advice,
earlier 1993 results and later SS horsepower do not supply the 1998 fields.
Remaining PDF pages were not individually read for technical certification.
