## Evidence boundaries

ASA's original FIA-hosted form was downloaded and rendered. Printed pages 1–3
identify the production coupe, manufacture beginning in 1964, chassis layout,
seats, wheelbase, coil-sprung rear axle and disc brakes. Optional plastic panels,
competition equipment and homologation dates are not universal road fitment or
production dates. The printed 1034.5 cc figure conflicts with the bore/stroke
and conventional 1032 cc description; the production-road feature is separately
cited for 1032 cc, SOHC and the overdrive manual. No numerical output, prototype
performance, total production, weight basis or silent reconciliation is claimed.
Graeme Hurst's actual driving feature was originally published in January 2008
and republished in 2025. ASA remains an independent marque, not an official
Ferrari production model or a duplicate of an early prototype.

The OSCA's nine technical entries are explicitly the 2012 catalogue reference
for road-registered 1963 chassis 0097, including its published 105 bhp. They do
not represent every 1600 GT/GTS or claim a universal factory rating. Competition
engines, uncertain production totals, other coachwork and another car's history
are excluded. The reason for admission is the Maserati brothers' specialist GT
engineering and Zagato design, beyond scarcity or concours prizes.

Motor Sport's October 1984 Strada test supplies road hardware and driving
character. Its OCR output/torque and tyre shorthand are not converted into
factory numerical fields. FIA's identity index corroborates 1995 cc, four
cylinders, front drive and Italy without importing any competition extension.
Le Moniteur reproduces its own March 1984 test and records both engaging
handling and comfort/shift shortcomings. Its anachronistic personal Carlo
Abarth development reference is not repeated. Bucket seats are not declared
standard for every market. The 1986 UK photograph is a later representative.

GoAuto's February 2001 Australian Super Test provides separately labelled
engine, transmission, suspension and driving sections for the Satria GTi.
Native 103 kW / 164 Nm and their peaks are kept. Only the tested tyre size is
recorded, not a universal brand. The reviewer values the Lotus-developed chassis
but notes harsh ride and poor stereo controls. Claimed acceleration is disputed
within the engine report and is not imported. UK 133 bhp, Malaysian tunes,
R3 and later Neo/Gen.2 specifications do not replace this reference. A separate
first-person UK retrospective supports its enduring enthusiast identity while
disclosing the writer's work on Proton's advertising account; it is not used for
Australian technical fields.

The inaccessible brochure host returned HTTP 403 for the Fiat index and Satria
Australian PDF. Those responses and their search snippets are not evidence for
imported fields; access checks were not bypassed.
