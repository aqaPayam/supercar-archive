# Road-icon batch 123: V6 Galant / Legnum VR-4

Reviewed 2026-10-30 against all 919 existing identities. One absent eighth-generation
V6 VR-4 road family brings the working inventory to 920: 525 curated road additions
across 123 manifests plus the preserved 395-record baseline. This is not yet a
certified qualifying count toward the 1,000-icon goal.

## Admission and identity

The engineering reason is the production introduction of rear-wheel torque
vectoring. In [Technology DNA of MMC, Technical Review 2005](https://mmc-manuals.ru/manuals/misc/technical_review/technical_review_2005.pdf),
Mitsubishi engineers Hashimoto, Fukuyama, Oikawa and Kume explicitly date
Galant and Lancer Evolution AYC application to 1996. Printed pages 8–9 explain
how controlling the torque difference between the rear wheels affects yaw.
This concrete chassis milestone supports admission beyond power or rarity.
It is manufacturer engineering evidence, not an independent road test; no
unqualified worldwide priority claim is adopted.

The full inventory was checked for Galant, Legnum, EC5A, EC5W, 6A13, Aspire
and Eterna. Only the existing E39A four-cylinder rally-road Galant appears.
The V6 eighth-generation programme is distinct. Mitsubishi's model history
explicitly derives the Legnum wagon from this Galant, so saloon/wagon, trims,
transmissions, Super VR-4, facelift and years count as one family here.
No extra body or grade record is proposed. The older Galant's rally results
do not become V6 VR-4 competition achievements.

## Selected specification and limits

The [manufacturer's company history](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com/en/company/history/company/)
has a 6A1-series section, filed under its 1991 engine-family introduction.
That complete relevant section was read. It separately dates this Galant/Legnum
application to August 1996 and gives the selected 6A13 engine reference.
Eight attributes map there: designation, V6/DOHC layout, nominal 2.5 litres,
twin turbos, intercooling, 280 PS and 37.0 kgf-m. Engine-family totals and
the broader family displacement range are not VR-4 specifications.

Both relevant [Galant and Legnum model-history sections](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com/en/company/history/car/)
were read in full, including their displayed generic dimensions. Two driveline
attributes map to the Galant's full-time viscous-coupled 4WD description.
Generic dimensions and the ordinary Galant's GDI engine are not imported.

The model history describes a 280-PS INVECS-II automatic. It does not establish
that combination for every launch-year trim. This record therefore selects
the historical engine reference without assigning a gearbox or individual
grade. The year 1996 denotes programme introduction, not certification of
every 1996 version's output. A dated original catalogue is needed before
combining launch manual/automatic specifications or adding exact trim equipment.
AYC is the family's historical significance, not an all-grade equipment claim.
The model history qualifies Galant AYC/ASC by trim and separately describes
Legnum VR-4 AYC and Type S ASC/TCL; these descriptions are not flattened.

The engineering paper's heading, authors and complete vehicle-dynamics section
were read as web PDF text. The 1987 ECS/4WS discussion and later Evo MR roof
do not become V6 VR-4 attributes. No full 90-page visual audit is claimed.
The inaccessible CiteSeer mirror and unrelated 2002 review did not supply fields.
No exact cc, peak rpm, test performance, mass, dimensions, brake sizes, tyres,
fuel injection, later ACD/Super AYC or all-trim fitment is inferred.
Exact qualifications and mappings are in the [manifest](../data/icons-road-123.json).
