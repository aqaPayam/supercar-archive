## Matra Bagheera

The Bagheera made Matra’s transverse mid-engine sports-car layout work with three seats across a single front row. Its glass-fibre-reinforced polyester panels, self-supporting steel structure, independent suspension and four assisted disc brakes give that unusual packaging a substantive sporting basis. It represents a distinctive French sports-car design programme, not merely a rare trim. One original Bagheera family is counted; later engines, facelifts, Courreges trim and Talbot badging add no extra entries.

## Matra Murena

The Murena developed Matra’s three-abreast mid-engine sports-coupe idea into a distinct successor with a five-speed transmission and a hot-dip galvanised structure. Its combination of unconventional passenger packaging, independent suspension, disc brakes and an innovative corrosion-protection process gives it lasting engineering significance within French sports-car history. One original Murena family is admitted; the 1.6, tuned 2.2, kit 142 and S versions do not create extra counts.

## Evidence actually inspected

The selected French Bagheera brochure was downloaded as a four-page PDF.
Its narrative, illustrated feature and technical pages (PDF indexes 1–3)
were rendered and visually read. The technical footer dates the specifications
September 1, 1974 and identifies Chrysler France. It defines the 1294 cc
four-cylinder, 84 ch DIN at 6,000 rpm, four-speed and associated road hardware.
The illustrated narrative elsewhere says 83 cv; the explicit technical rating
is retained and that discrepancy is recorded. Torque remains in printed mkg;
no conversion or modern power convention is inferred. Optional alloy wheels
and version-specific equipment are not represented as universal standards.
Brochure references to Matra's Le Mans success concern the marque's competition
programme and do not establish Bagheera road-car wins or race specifications.

An earlier host-labelled French/1974 PDF was also downloaded, but its actually
read cabin/back pages identify Chrysler Belgique and a Belgian/Luxembourg
sales network. It is not the source of this French technical record. A hosted
English transcription was a discovery aid; it repeats clutch text under its
gearbox heading, so the original French scan supplies the specifications.
Other brochure hosts returned 403 or connection errors; unseen material was
not used and access controls were not bypassed.

The 12-page French Murena brochure was downloaded. Cover/edition page 0,
road-layout page 2, engine-position page 4, gearbox page 6, seating page 7 and
manufacturing/technical spread page 10 were rendered and visually read.
The printed model-year is 1981 and edition is July 15, 1980, reference 1 E 011.
The ordinary 2.2 column, not 1.6 or a tuned package, supplies 2155 cc,
118 ch DIN at 5,800 rpm, 18.5 m.daN at 3,000 rpm and the five-speed.
The table separately labels ISO kW and DIN ch; these unlike ratings are not
silently treated as identical unit conversions. Native DIN and torque units
are retained. Some consumption entries are marked undergoing homologation;
no consumption, road performance, absolute world-first claim, production total
or current warranty entitlement is imported. The other brochure pages were
not all read.

The actual Matra historical account and Lane Motor Museum's collection page
were read for the unusual three-abreast architecture, distinct successor and
manufacturing significance. The museum's numerical specs and production count
are not substituted for the selected French brochure. Admission is an editorial
judgment based on these engineering features, not a claim that either road car
won the marque's prototype races or set a speed record.
