## Admission and selected evidence

### Facel Vega Facel II

The Facel II is a significant French-American performance GT, combining Chrysler V8 power with a distinctive low French coupe body. First-hand road driving documents forceful acceleration and sustained touring ability. Its place in postwar GT history supports one original family entry; rarity, luxury and a fastest-coupe slogan do not establish admission by themselves.

Selected reference: 6.3-litre manual architecture — HK2 B109, Chicane 4/2011 road reference.

Architecture reference HK2 B109 as driven in Chicane Magazine 4/2011, not a claim of unchanged factory condition. The article covers a modern electric steering conversion and corrected alignment: neither is original equipment or unmodified factory handling. Its 390-pk/5500-rpm, approximate speed/weight and production figures are not imported. 1962 identifies the start of the stated 1962–1964 model range, not a verified build year for B109. ### MG XPower SV

The XPower SV was MG’s ambitious carbon-bodied V8 flagship, pairing a steel box frame and integrated rollover structure with a rear-drive sports-car layout. Its engineering and distinctive place in MG’s performance history justify one original family entry. A long-term SV-R road test records involving handling and braking alongside substantial finish and reliability shortcomings, rather than establishing merit through rarity alone.

Selected reference: Entry-level 4.6 V8 five-speed manual — 29 October 2003 launch specification.

29 October 2003 manufacturer launch release, initial entry-level manual SV specification. Retains native 320 hp SAE and 410 Nm without adopting the inconsistent 230 kW conversion. ## Reading and specification boundaries

The four-page Chicane PDF was downloaded, text-extracted, rendered and all four
spreads visually inspected. It is issue 4/2011, printed pages 40–47, despite
its later hosting path. Michiel Mulder and Peter Dielissen describe actual
road driving of HK2 B109. EZ Power Steering hosts this supplier-focused
feature; it is not an untouched 1960s factory road test. Only the selected
engine/driveline/chassis architecture is retained. Modern electric steering,
alignment corrections, Offenhauser covers and the authors' experience of the
modified car do not certify original equipment or factory handling. The club
model account supplies separate HK2 identity; its contradictory power figures
and fastest-coupe claim are not substituted for the road reference.

The full MG 29 October 2003 launch-release body and specification table were
read. SAE hp is retained without adopting its inconsistent kW conversion.
Performance estimates, contradictory speed-unit pairs and proposed very-high-
power options are omitted. This is a UK-marque programme with Italian chassis/
body work and Longbridge final assembly, not an all-UK manufacture claim.

The actual evo long-term conclusion was read. It follows BX54 BBV, a 5.0-litre
SV-R, and records enjoyable steering, braking and handling alongside faults,
poor finish and development concerns. Those findings explain family significance;
SV-R engine specifications do not transfer to the 4.6-litre entry. Autocar's
2013 article returned payment-required on its canonical page; its unread
road-driving section is not used and no alternate host was used to bypass it.
An unrelated Sports Car International Facel page returned 429 and was not used.
